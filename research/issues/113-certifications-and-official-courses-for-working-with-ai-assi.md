# #113 Research: certifications and official courses for working with AI assistants — what exists, and whether Part 6 should mention them

<!-- Copied from https://github.com/datafromzero/datafromzero.github.io/issues/113 by scripts/sync-research-issues.mjs; edit the issue, not this file. -->

- Issue: [#113](https://github.com/datafromzero/datafromzero.github.io/issues/113) (open), opened 2026-11-22

**Question asked (2026-11-22):** Vendors now advertise certifications and badges for working with their AI assistants. What really exists, what does each one teach and test, and should Part 6 "Working with AI" (lessons C36–C43 + the capstone) mention any of them? Method: primary sources only — each issuer's own pages, exam guides and announcements, opened 2026-11-22. Every fact is as of that date. Prices, hours or dates not on an issuer page are written "not stated"; items that could not be verified on an opened page are marked [uncertain].

## Summary

- The headline product barely exists: there is **no public, open-to-anyone certification for "working with an AI assistant"** from Anthropic or OpenAI. Anthropic's four proctored Claude exams are gated to its partner firms; OpenAI's certification program is pilot-only. What the public can earn from both is free, unproctored course badges — and OpenAI states in writing that its badges are *not* certifications.
- The proctored exams anyone can buy (Google Generative AI Leader $99; Microsoft AB-730; AWS AI Practitioner $100; Databricks, Snowflake) mostly test **building or governing generative AI on one vendor's stack**, not the end-user skills Part 6 teaches. AB-730's syllabus is the closest mirror of Part 6 — inside Microsoft 365 Copilot.
- A shared transferable core does exist across vendors: capability/limits literacy, prompting and context, verification and evaluation, privacy and responsible use, delegation, and agent/RAG literacy. Anthropic's **AI Fluency Framework (the 4Ds)** names it best — but it is licensed CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, so the site can link to it, not adapt it.
- **No primary evidence exists that employers ask for these credentials.** The only documented demand mechanism is vendor partner programs that count certified staff toward a firm's partner tier.
- Part 6's exercises can run free: **Claude Free and ChatGPT Free** both officially include CSV upload and in-chat code execution; **Gemini CLI** is the only free command-line agent.
- Recommendation: **Option 2** — one dated "Where to go next" box at the end of Part 6 listing free official courses from several vendors side by side, with one honest line about paid certifications and the missing demand evidence. Details in the comments that follow this issue body.

## Findings

**Finding 1 — Anthropic's real certifications are unreachable for our learners; its free courses are the actual offer.** The Claude Certification Program has four proctored, identity-verified Pearson exams (Claude Certified Architect: Foundations, launched 2026-03-12; Associate: Foundations, Developer: Foundations, and Architect: Professional, added 2026-07-23) — open **only** to members of Claude Partner Network organizations. Fees, duration, validity and languages are not stated on any public Anthropic page [the figures in secondary guides — $99–175, 120 min, 12-month validity — are unverified]. What anyone can take is **Claude Academy** (academy.claude.com): 29 free self-paced courses, most ending in an unproctored completion badge. Beginner-relevant: Claude 101 (2.5 hr), AI Fluency: Framework and foundations (4 hr), AI capabilities and limitations (3.5 hr). Sources: anthropic.com/news/claude-partner-network; claude.com/resources/articles/four-role-based-claude-certifications; academy.claude.com/courses (all opened 2026-11-22).

**Finding 2 — OpenAI has no publicly available certification, and says so itself.** OpenAI Academy offers 14 short courses (30–180 min) with an unproctored badge at ≥80% and pathway completion certificates; OpenAI's Help Center states these badges and certificates **are not certifications** and do not guarantee eligibility for a future one. The announced **OpenAI Certifications** program (September 2025) runs only through employer and public-sector pilots; the OpenAI Certified app is described in OpenAI's Help listing as invite-only for ChatGPT Enterprise/Edu workspaces [uncertain — that page itself was rate-limited (HTTP 429) and could not be opened]. "Certify 10 million Americans by 2030" is an issuer claim. Sources: help.openai.com/en/articles/20001270-openai-academy-courses; openai.com/index/openai-certificate-courses/ (opened 2026-11-22).

**Finding 3 — Google's worker-level line is paid course certificates on Coursera; its one proctored exam is a business-leader exam.** Google AI Essentials (<5 hr) and Google Prompting Essentials (<10 hr) are unproctored Coursera completion certificates on a **$49/month** subscription (7-day trial; financial aid exists; neither is free). The new Google AI Professional Certificate (launched 2026-02-19; ~8 one-hour courses, including data analysis in Google Sheets and "vibe coding") rides the same subscription and bundles 3 months of Google AI Pro. The Prompting Essentials Grow landing page already redirects to it — churn within a year. Google's only proctored generative-AI exam, **Generative AI Leader** ($99, 90 min, no prerequisites, valid 3 years, launched 2025-05-14), is ~55–60% transferable concepts, but its largest single section (35%) is Google product knowledge. Google Skills adds many short Gemini skill badges (Workspace apps, Code Assist, a 3-hour Gemini CLI course); **no Gemini or Gemini CLI certification exam exists**, and several older Gemini badges are already deprecated. Sources: grow.google/ai-essentials; coursera.org/google-certificates/prompting-essentials-google; grow.google/ai-professional; cloud.google.com/learn/certification/generative-ai-leader + its exam guide (opened 2026-11-22).

**Finding 4 — Microsoft rebuilt its AI credential line in 2026; the closest Part 6 mirror is a Copilot exam.** **AB-730 AI Business Professional** (proctored, no code) tests generative-AI fundamentals including privacy and verification (25–30%), managing prompts and chats in Microsoft 365 Copilot (20–25%), content and collaboration (20–25%), and driving outcomes with pre-built agents and Copilot Cowork (20–25%) — Part 6's topic set, taught inside one product. **AB-900** covers Copilot/agent *administration*. **AI-900 was retired 2026-06-30** and replaced by **AI-901**, which now requires Python and is 55–60% hands-on building in Microsoft Foundry; **AI-102 was retired** and replaced by **AI-103** (Foundry apps/agents, RAG, evaluation; 1-year validity with free renewal). An unproctored Applied Skills lab, "Streamline business workflows with AI chat", also exists. Microsoft's certification pages as opened did not display exam prices or durations (price varies by region and appears at scheduling) — no dollar figure is printed here for these exams. Sources: learn.microsoft.com AB-730, AB-900, AI-901, AI-103 cert pages and study guides (opened 2026-11-22).

**Finding 5 — GitHub Copilot's GH-300 is a genuine proctored exam, weighted to product features.** Syllabus updated 2026-08-07; valid 2 years (with a 6-month transition extension). Domains: responsible use 15–20% [transferable], product features ≥50% across two domains [tool-specific], data/architecture 10–15%, prompt engineering and context crafting 10–15% [transferable], developer productivity 10–15%, privacy and safeguards 10–15%. Price and duration not stated on the pages opened. Source: learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/github-copilot + GH-300 study guide (opened 2026-11-22).

**Finding 6 — Cloud and data-platform exams are real, proctored, and aimed at builders, not assistant users.** AWS Certified AI Practitioner (AIF-C01: $100, 65 questions/90 min, 3-year validity, 12 languages) is the only beginner-level one, and is still AWS-service-centred; above it sit AWS Generative AI Developer – Professional (AIP-C01, $300) and the updated ML Engineer – Associate (MLA-C02, in beta; English MLA-C01 retired 2026-09-28) with added Bedrock/RAG/agent content, plus an announced hands-on Agentic AI Demonstrated microcredential [details not stated]. Databricks fields Generative AI Engineer Associate ($200, 45 questions/90 min, 2-year validity) and Snowflake fields SnowPro Specialty: Gen AI (proctored; expects 1+ years of enterprise Snowflake GenAI experience; price [uncertain] — a $225 voucher value appears on Snowflake's own Summit FAQ but the certification page could not be opened) and a new AI Governance & Security specialty. Sources: aws.amazon.com/certification pages; databricks.com/learn/certification/genai-engineer-associate + exam guide; Snowflake official Gen AI study guide (opened 2026-11-22).

**Finding 7 — The newest syllabus in the whole inventory is about context, and it validates Part 6's thesis.** Databricks Certified **Context Engineer Associate** launched 2026-07-29 ($200, 2-year validity, English only [uncertain — the exam-guide PDF itself was rate-limited; facts are from the guide's text as published on databricks.com]): context foundations 16%, system-prompt and instruction design 9%, knowledge retrieval 20%, memory architecture 18%, tool design/MCP 13%, context compression 11%, multi-agent design 13%. Vendors are converging on exactly Part 6's ground — context, agents, evaluation — but always inside their own tooling. Source: databricks.com Context Engineer exam guide (2026-11-22).

**Finding 8 — No widely used vendor-neutral AI-literacy credential exists.** Elements of AI (University of Helsinki + MinnaLearn, since 2018) is a free, unproctored **course certificate** — widely used, but its centre of gravity is classical AI, not working with assistants [its certificate terms for the Building AI part — a €50 certificate per the issuer's FAQ as reported second-hand — are uncertain; the FAQ page could not be opened]. CertNexus AIBIZ/AgenticAIBIZ are unproctored business micro-badges with no adoption evidence found. ISACA's AI credentials are advanced audit add-ons requiring existing designations. The EU issues no personal AI-literacy credential (the AI Act obliges organisations; DigComp is a framework, not a credential). Sources: elementsofai.com; CertNexus AIBIZ blueprint; isaca.org (opened/surfaced 2026-11-22).

**Finding 9 — There is no primary evidence that employers ask for any of these credentials.** No issuer or job board publishes job-posting counts naming an AI-assistant certification; no named employer states a requirement or preference for one on its own pages. Job-board trackers measure AI *skills* in postings (e.g. Indeed's AI tracker, ~7.3% of US postings in September 2026), not certificates. The one real, checkable demand mechanism is **vendor partner programs**: Anthropic's published partner tiers require 10 / 100 / 1,000 certified individuals per firm, and large consultancies are certifying staff in bulk to satisfy them — demand manufactured by the vendor's channel, aimed at consultants, not junior data roles. Everything else found is issuer claims (e.g. Google's "62% of employers expect AI familiarity", footnoted to a Coursera report; Anthropic's "firms are prioritizing certification in hiring"; OpenAI relaying a PwC wage figure) or reseller marketing, and is labelled as such wherever this report repeats it. Sources: cluster E evidence file; anthropic.com/news/claude-partner-network; cloud.google.com blog (opened 2026-11-22).

**Finding 10 — Part 6 teaches things no credential tests, and the credentials' extra topics mostly belong in the tracks.** No syllabus found teaches statistical misuse (wrong test, p-hacking, causal verbs, citations that do not resolve — C41) or error-hunting drills (C39, C40); Part 6 is ahead of the market there, not behind it. Conversely, the credential topics Part 6 does not cover — formal evaluation engineering (test sets, LLM judges), building RAG/agents rather than judging them, platform administration and governance, adoption strategy — are builder/operator material for the Data Engineer, Data Scientist and ML Engineer tracks, not for a pre-track core part. One fold-in is recommended for Part 6 itself: context-window management (what falls out of context, compression) inside C37, backed by Anthropic's capabilities course and the Context Engineer syllabus. Full table in the first comment on this issue.

**Finding 11 — The Part 6 exercises can be run entirely on free tiers, with one honest caveat.** Claude Free and ChatGPT Free both officially include file upload **and** in-chat code execution/data analysis at $0 (Claude caps code-execution files at 30 MB; OpenAI gives its data-analysis tool its own limit). Neither publishes a numeric allowance, so exercises should be designed to work on **either** assistant, each as the other's backup. Gemini's free tier documents upload-and-discuss (10 files/prompt, ≤100 MB) but **no in-chat Python**, and its free context window is 32K tokens; free Microsoft Copilot documents uploads (20 files, ≤50 MB) and no in-chat Python either — both are partial fits. For the command-line stretch, **Gemini CLI is the only free agent** (officially 1,000 requests/day with a Google sign-in); Claude Code is paid-plans-only, and the Codex CLI starts at Plus [uncertain — OpenAI's Codex pricing page could not be opened; the Free tier's "limited Codex access" on OpenAI's pricing page is desktop-app access per corroborating summaries]. Full table in the second comment on this issue.

**Finding 12 — Anything the site prints about this market must carry a date.** Within the research window alone: AI-900 and AI-102 retired (2026-06-30), Microsoft's AB-730/AB-900 line is new, Anthropic's program went from one exam to four (March→July 2026), Databricks launched a new credential (July 2026), AWS's ML Engineer exam changed version (September 2026), and Google's Prompting Essentials page began redirecting to a newer certificate. A mention without a date and a re-check cycle would go stale inside a year.

## Common core (Question C)

Competencies that recur across most vendors' syllabi, in our words:

1. **Capability and limits literacy** — what a large language model is; hallucination and plausible-but-false output; knowledge cut-offs; context windows and what falls out of them. (Anthropic's capabilities course; Google AI Essentials and Generative AI Leader §§1/3; AB-730; AIF-C01; OpenAI AI Foundations.)
2. **Prompting and context-giving** — state the task, supply context and examples, iterate; assemble the right material for the task (context packs, retrieval, memory). (Prompting Essentials' five-step routine; AB-730; GH-300's prompt/context domain; Databricks Context Engineer.)
3. **Verification and evaluation** — check outputs against sources before use; human-in-the-loop review; at builder level, test sets, metrics and LLM judges. (AB-730; GH-300 responsible use; Generative AI Leader §3; Databricks evaluation sections; OpenAI's Evaluate AI Applications.)
4. **Privacy, security and responsible use** — keep sensitive data out, watch for bias and prompt injection, guardrails and governance. (Every platform exam: AIF-C01 domains 4–5; Snowflake governance 26%; AB-900; GH-300 privacy domain.)
5. **Delegation and workflow design** — break work into steps; decide what the AI does, what stays human, and where the checkpoints are. (AI Fluency's Delegation; OpenAI's Agents and Workflows; AB-730's agent section.)
6. **Agent and RAG literacy** — what an agent is, grounding/retrieval, tool use, oversight of multi-step runs. (Generative AI Leader; AI-103; Databricks exams; GH-300 agent mode.)

**The shared framework:** Anthropic's **AI Fluency Framework** (the "4D Framework"), by Rick Dakan and Joseph Feller with Anthropic: **Delegation** (decide whether/when/how to engage AI), **Description** (communicate the product, process and performance you want), **Discernment** (evaluate outputs, reasoning and behaviour), **Diligence** (choose systems thoughtfully, be transparent about AI's role, verify and vouch for what you share) — practised across three modes: **Automation, Augmentation, Agency**. It maps closely onto Part 6: Delegation ≈ C36/C42, Description ≈ C37, Discernment ≈ C38–C41, Diligence ≈ the privacy/transparency rules and the capstone's verification log. **License: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0** (verified on Anthropic's course page and framework one-pager, opened 2026-11-22) — non-commercial and share-alike, so Data From Zero (CC BY 4.0) may **link and describe** it but must not adapt its text into site content. No cross-vendor standard framework was found; Google's five-step prompting routine is course content, not a licensed framework.
