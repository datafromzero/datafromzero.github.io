term: Confounder

A confounder is a third thing that is linked to both the groups and the result, so that it could produce the difference between the groups without any help from the group. In the cafe data, Harbor opened on April 1, so a count of its orders over six months sets three months against six: the months open are tied to the store and to the count at once. Concepts in Statistics, an open course text, describes confounding variables as other factors that can also influence the response, which can make the results of an observational study misleading. Only an assigned, randomized comparison removes this kind of doubt.

## Where you meet it

- [*Correlation is not causation*](/core/correlation-and-causation/) (C10): three rival explanations for a correlation, one of them a confounder.
