## The question

The owner asks an assistant the question every owner asks:

> How much did each cafe sell?

The assistant has never seen the cafe tables. It sees only what the prompt holds, and fills the gaps with what such tables usually look like. By the end you can build a prompt that holds the facts, ask for a plan before code, and follow one card.

## Learn by doing

About 50 minutes, 4 new ideas: the context pack, a plan before code, the recipe card, and a chat that gets too long. The SQL runs in the page. Exercise 2 uses Claude or ChatGPT, free tier, each the other's backup.

### 1. The context pack

**Idea.** A *context pack* is the part of a prompt that holds your data, in four parts in this order: the *schema* (every table and column with its type), the *grain* (what one row of each table is), a few real *sample rows*, and a *data dictionary* (what the columns mean that a name cannot say). Anthropic and Google advise putting long material before the question, and Anthropic and Microsoft advise labeling it; the four parts are ours.

**Try it.** First, no pack. This reply to the bare question was written for this lesson, in an assistant's style. Run it:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT store_name, SUM(total) AS revenue
FROM orders
GROUP BY store_name
ORDER BY revenue DESC;`} />

**Predict:** The question names no table and no column. Will the reply run?

Expected: a message instead of rows.

*Why:* the message says `store_name` was not found in the FROM clause. The assistant guessed a store name in `orders`, which holds a store id. Now the real schema part: look for the amount of a line.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT table_name, column_name, data_type
FROM information_schema.columns
WHERE table_schema = 'main'
ORDER BY table_name, ordinal_position;`} />

**Predict:** Which of the 24 columns holds the amount of an order line?

Expected: 24 rows, 3 columns.

*Why:* none does. A line's amount comes from three columns, and only the dictionary can say how. Here is a full pack for cafe-sales; its sample rows are real, chosen for the odd cases (a walk-in, meaning a customer with no loyalty card, and a canceled order).

```text
SCHEMA (DuckDB)
stores(store_id INTEGER, store_name VARCHAR, city VARCHAR, opened_on DATE)
customers(customer_id INTEGER, signup_date DATE, city VARCHAR, loyalty_tier VARCHAR, referred_by INTEGER)
products(product_id INTEGER, product_name VARCHAR, category VARCHAR, unit_price_usd DECIMAL(6,2))
orders(order_id INTEGER, customer_id INTEGER, store_id INTEGER, ordered_at TIMESTAMP, status VARCHAR, payment_method VARCHAR)
order_items(order_item_id INTEGER, order_id INTEGER, product_id INTEGER, quantity INTEGER, discount_usd DECIMAL(6,2))

GRAIN: one row of stores is a store; customers, a loyalty-program customer; products, a menu item;
orders, an order (one visit to the cash register); order_items, one product line inside an order.

SAMPLE ROWS
orders: 1001 | 15 | 2 | 2026-01-01 08:02:00 | completed | mobile
orders: 1335 | (empty) | 1 | 2026-02-14 08:59:00 | completed | mobile
orders: 1461 | 42 | 2 | 2026-03-01 08:10:00 | canceled | cash
order_items: 5001 | 1001 | 10 | 1 | 0.00

DICTIONARY
- An empty customer_id is a walk-in without a loyalty card. status is completed or canceled.
- The amount of a line is quantity * unit_price_usd - discount_usd; discount_usd is for the whole line.
- Sales means completed orders only.
```

Never paste personal data into a prompt: names, emails, phone numbers, payment details; [*Using an AI assistant on a spreadsheet, safely*](/excel/ai-draft-and-verify/) (E22) lists more. Sample rows of real data are invented or anonymized. Keep the pack as a file next to your code and update it when a table changes: a stale pack makes the assistant confidently wrong. The Missing Semester's coding agent loads its whole `AGENTS.md` at the start; the pack does that job for data work.

### 2. A plan before code

**Idea.** Give the assistant an *output contract*: the form of the answer, its columns, the grain of one row, and a way out when the data cannot answer. Then ask for a *plan* first: tables, join keys, the grain after each step. Judge it by its join keys, its cardinality (how many rows of one table match one row of the other) and its grain, before any code exists.

**Try it.** A plan written for this lesson, for "the average sales per customer, by tier": *1. Join `customers` to `orders` on `customer_id`: one row per customer. 2. Join `order_items` on `order_id`: one row per customer. 3. Add up the amounts and divide by the customers of each tier.* The rows after each step:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM customers) AS customers,
       (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM customers c
        JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id) AS after_orders,
       (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM customers c
        JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
        JOIN order_items i ON i.order_id = o.order_id) AS after_lines;`} />

**Predict:** How many rows will there be after each of the two joins?

Expected: 1 row, 3 columns.

*Why:* 150, then 1,799, then 3,286. The keys are right, but the cardinality is wrong at both steps: a customer has many orders, and an order has many lines. The grain after step 1 is an order, and after step 2 a line. A wrong grain is cheap to find in a plan and expensive to find in a number: send the plan back before you say "write the code".

### 3. The recipe card

**Idea.** One card for every data prompt. Each step names its support: *evidence* means a paper measured it; *vendor advice* means vendors recommend it and no paper cited here measures it.

<Aside type="tip" title="Recipe card: a data prompt in eight steps">

1. **Put the data in the room.** Paste the pack inside clear labels and say: answer only from this material. *Vendor advice (Anthropic, Google, Microsoft).*
2. **Name the job and the reader.** One line each: what you want, who reads it, the shape of the answer. *Vendor advice. A role steers style, never accuracy: personas in system prompts did not improve performance across a range of questions (Zheng et al., 2024).*
3. **Show a few examples** in the exact shape you want back. *Vendor advice: Anthropic suggests 3 to 5, Google says always.*
4. **Give it an out.** "If the answer is not in the data, say exactly: not in the data. Never invent a column, a row, a number or a source." *Vendor advice (Anthropic, Microsoft, Google); Anthropic adds that it reduces hallucinations and does not eliminate them.*
5. **Code computes; the model puts it in words.** Ask for the SQL or Python, run it, and take no total, average or count from the model's head. *Evidence: writing code and running it beat chain-of-thought answers by 15 points on GSM8K, grade-school math problems, with 2022 models (PAL, v2). Vendor advice (Google): turn code execution on for any arithmetic, counting or calculation.*
6. **Evidence before the answer.** "First quote the rows your answer rests on, then answer." *Vendor advice (Anthropic, for long documents).*
7. **Ask for the shape, loosely.** Name the format and the length. For strict JSON, get the answer in plain words first, then ask for the conversion. *Evidence: one model scored 86.51 on GSM8K in plain text and 23.44 under a strict JSON schema (Tam et al., Table 1).*
8. **Check it against the data, yourself.** Run the code, recompute one number another way, confirm every name exists, then accept, revise or reject and write it down. This is [*Trust, but verify: checking AI output*](/core/trust-but-verify-ai-output/) (C38).

</Aside>

<Aside type="note" title="Dated note, read 2026-11-22: models that reason on their own">

The card says to state the goal and the constraints and let the model think. For models that reason on their own, the four vendors' current guides advise against scripting "think step by step". OpenAI, the maker of ChatGPT, says it may not help and can hinder; Anthropic: "Prefer general instructions over prescriptive steps" (for example, "think thoroughly"); Google: "it's generally not necessary to have the model outline, plan, or detail reasoning steps in the returned response itself"; Microsoft: "These techniques aren't recommended for reasoning models". Older guides still teach it. Re-check every six months (next 2027-05-22).

</Aside>

**Try it.** A prompt written for this lesson: *"You are an expert data scientist. Think step by step. Check your work. What is the sales trend?"*

**Predict:** Which steps of the card does it use, and which of its three instructions help accuracy?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the answer</summary>

It uses none of steps 1 and 3 to 7: no data, example, out, code, quotes or shape. "Expert" is a role: style only. "Think step by step" is the scripted reasoning of the note. "Check your work" is not a check: C38 shows why.

</details>

*Why:* the clever-sounding instructions carry no support; the dull ones, the data and the out, do the work. An optional extra: the author of this site wrote [keeploop](https://github.com/johnha912/keeploop) (MIT), a loop that keeps a change only when it scores better. Nothing here needs it.

### 4. A chat that gets too long

**Idea.** Anthropic defines the context window as all the text a model can reference when it writes a reply, including the reply itself, and says that as the token count (a token is a small piece of text, about a word or part of one) grows, accuracy and recall degrade (*context rot*). It adds that what is in the window matters as much as how much room is left, and that some chat applications drop the oldest messages first.

**Try it.** A chat about one query has run to forty messages, and the assistant has lost a decision from message 6.

**Predict:** What goes into a fresh chat?

<details>
<summary>Reveal the answer</summary>

The pack, the question, and a note for the next reader of what was decided and checked:

```text
Decided: sales means completed orders; join orders to order_items on order_id.
Checked: the rows after the join (3,287), the grand total by a second route.
Open: whether to split the sales by payment method.
```

</details>

*Why:* the fresh chat holds only what the task needs: one chat for one task, started from the pack.

<Aside type="tip" title="Remember">

- The pack has four parts in order: schema, grain, sample rows, dictionary. The question comes last.
- Ask for a plan first; judge it by join keys, cardinality and grain.
- Follow the card: data, job, examples, out, code, quotes, shape, check.
- Keep chats short; restart from the pack and a handover note.

**Common trap:** a stale pack. If the data changed and the pack did not, the assistant is confidently wrong.

</Aside>

## On your own

<Exercise id="core-prompting-with-the-data-in-the-room-2">

**Prompt with the card.** Open Claude or ChatGPT (free tier). Paste the pack of unit 1 and attach `orders.csv`, `customers.csv` and `stores.csv` from the [dataset folder](https://github.com/datafromzero/datafromzero.github.io/tree/v0.1.0/datasets/cafe-sales/v1). Ask for a plan first: *How many different gold-tier customers ordered at Campus, and how many completed orders did they place there?* Judge the plan by its keys, cardinality and grain, then ask for the SQL and run it below. First write what you expect the answer to look like. Replies differ from run to run: compare yours with the query. Expected: 1 row, 2 columns.

<RunSQL query={`-- Write your query here, then press Run.`} />

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Join `orders` to `stores` and to `customers`. Count distinct customers, and count orders; keep completed orders of gold-tier customers at Campus.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Half-solution</summary>

Expected output in words: *"One row, two whole numbers: the customers, then the larger number of orders."*

</details>

<Solution>

```sql
SELECT COUNT(DISTINCT c.customer_id) AS gold_customers,
       COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders o
JOIN stores s ON s.store_id = o.store_id
JOIN customers c ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
WHERE s.store_name = 'Campus' AND c.loyalty_tier = 'gold' AND o.status = 'completed';
```

```text
gold_customers  orders
11              31
```

A good plan joins on `store_id` and `customer_id`, says one row per order after the joins, and counts the customers distinct. If your assistant's numbers differ, find where its plan or code does.

</Solution>

</Exercise>
