## The question

The owner asks:

> Which cafe sells the most, and is it far ahead of the others?

The sales of a cafe are the sums of the amounts of its order lines. The first three lines:

| order_item_id | store_id | amount |
|---:|---:|---:|
| 5001 | 2 | 3.25 |
| 5002 | 2 | 4.75 |
| 5003 | 2 | 6.00 |

A chart answers faster than a table of totals, if it is drawn honestly. By the end of this page you will draw it, read it back as numbers, and say what it shows in its title.

In matplotlib `plot.bar()` is the column chart, `plot.line()` the line, `set_title` the title and `set_ylim(0, ...)` the axis minimum.

**Diverge.** In E17 you set the axis minimum in the chart's axis settings and looked at the result. In matplotlib the chart is an object you can question, and unit 3 prints where its axis starts and what a cut axis does to the picture.

</Aside>

### 1. Figure and axes

**Idea.** `fig, ax = plt.subplots()` makes an empty figure, the whole picture, and one **Axes**, the plot area inside it. `table.plot.bar(ax=ax)` draws the bars on that Axes, with `rot=0` to keep the labels horizontal. The Axes is the object that knows the chart: its bars are `ax.patches`, its title is `ax.get_title()`.

**Try it.** The sales of each store as bars, and what the objects say:

```python
fig, ax = plt.subplots()
sales_by_store.plot.bar(ax=ax, rot=0)
print(type(fig).__name__, type(ax).__name__)
print([round(float(p.get_height()), 2) for p in ax.patches])
```

**Predict:** what are the types of `fig` and `ax`, and what are the heights of the three bars?

</details>


### 2. Bar or line: the question decides

**Idea.** Compare separate categories with bars, one bar each. Follow a number over time with a line, because a line joins the points in order. `plot.line()` puts the line in `ax.lines`, while bars are in `ax.patches`.

**Try it.** The sales of each month, as a line with a mark on every month:

```python
monthly = sales.groupby("month")["amount"].sum()
fig, ax = plt.subplots()
monthly.plot.line(ax=ax, marker="o")
print(len(ax.lines), len(ax.patches))
print(ax.lines[0].get_ydata().round(2).tolist())
```

### 3. Honest bars start at zero

**Idea.** The height of a bar is its value, so a bar chart must start at 0: the eye compares heights. `ax.get_ylim()` returns the bottom and the top of the y axis, and `ax.set_ylim(low, high)` changes them.

**Try it.** The store bars with the axis as pandas drew it, and the names of the stores:

```python
named = sales_by_store.rename(index=store_names)
fig, ax = plt.subplots()
named.plot.bar(ax=ax, rot=0)
print(ax.get_ylim()[0])
```

<Aside type="tip" title="Remember">

- A chart is a figure that holds axes. `fig, ax = plt.subplots()` makes both, and `plot.bar(ax=ax)` draws on the Axes.
- Read a chart back from its Axes: `ax.patches` for bars, `ax.lines` for lines, `ax.get_title()`, `ax.get_ylim()`.
- Bars to compare categories, a line to follow time.
- Bars start at 0. `set_ylim` can cut the axis, and then the picture says something else than the numbers.
- One message for each chart: a title that states it, labeled axes with the unit, and `savefig` for the file.

**Common trap:** a bar chart on a cut axis, which makes small differences look big.
