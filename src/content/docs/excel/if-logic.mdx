## The question

The owner of the cafes asks you:

> I count the cash in the till every night against the orders. Which orders put cash in the till,
> and how much?

Here is the top of `raw_orders`.

| order_id | customer_id | store | ordered_on | payment_method | status | amount_usd |
|---|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|
| 1001 | 15 | Campus | 2026-01-01 | mobile | completed | 14.00 |
| 1002 | 46 | Old Town | 2026-01-01 | mobile | completed | 12.50 |
| 1003 | 61 | Campus | 2026-01-01 | mobile | completed | 12.50 |
| 1006 | 15 | Old Town | 2026-01-02 | card | completed | 13.75 |

An order puts cash in the till when it was paid in cash **and** it was not canceled. By the end of this
lesson you will have a column that says `in till` or `skip` on every one of the 1,800 rows, and you
will prove that it is right.

An **IF** asks a yes-or-no question about a row and answers with one label if the answer is yes and
another if it is no. The question is the **test**: a comparison such as `E6="cash"` (equal),
`G6>=16` (at least), `F6<>"canceled"` (not equal). Text goes in quotation marks; a cell or a number
does not.

`IF(test, if_true, if_false)`

When one question is not enough, put `AND` or `OR` inside the test:

- **`AND(test1, test2)`** is true only when every test is true.
- **`OR(test1, test2)`** is true when at least one test is true.

For three or more answers, use **`IFS`**: it checks the tests in order and returns the answer of the
first one that is true. End it with `TRUE` as a catch-all for everything left.

`IFS(test1, answer1, test2, answer2, TRUE, answer3)`

`IFS` needs Excel 2019 or later, or Microsoft 365, and Google Sheets has it. In an older Excel, put an `IF`
inside the `if_false` slot of another `IF`: that is a **nested IF**, and it gives the same answers.

A column that holds the answer of a rule for every row is a **flag column**. Adding the flag is the
first half of a pattern; the second half, counting and totaling by flag, comes in the next lesson.
Until then you can filter on the flag and use `SUBTOTAL`.

## Worked example

Question: **which orders put cash in the till, and how much?** Open the practice file and follow along.

1. **Copy the data to `calc` and make it a Table.** Copy all of `raw_orders` to cell **A5** of `calc`,
   so that rows 1 to 4 stay free for your totals: the header is in row 5 and the records run from row 6
   to row 1805. Click a cell in the data and press **Ctrl+T** (Mac: **Cmd+T**). In Google Sheets select the data and
   choose **Format > Convert to table**.
   *Why:* the Table has one header row and one record per row, as the first lesson asked.

2. **Write the rule in words, one condition per part.** "An order is `in till` when `payment_method` is
   cash **and** `status` is completed. Otherwise it is `skip`."
   *Why:* two conditions and an `and` already tell you which function to use, before you type
   anything.

3. **See what a test is.** In a spare cell, such as **J1**, type `=E6="cash"` and press Enter. It shows
   **FALSE**: row 6 is a mobile payment. Change the 6 to 463 and it shows **TRUE**.
   *Why:* a test is a question the sheet answers with TRUE or FALSE. `IF` turns that answer into a
   label.

4. **Write the flag.** In **H5** type `in_till`. In **H6** type
   `=IF(AND(E6="cash",F6="completed"),"in till","skip")` and press Enter. In an Excel Table the
   formula fills the whole column by itself. In Google Sheets, select **H6:H1805** and press
   **Ctrl+D** (Mac: **Cmd+D**).
   *Why each part:* `AND(...)` is the test, true only when both conditions hold. `"in till"` is the
   answer if it is true and `"skip"` the answer if it is not. Column E is `payment_method` and column
   F is `status`.

5. **Predict the odd row, then look.** Row **466** is order 1461: paid in cash, but canceled. What
   will H466 say? Scroll to it. It says **skip**, and row 463 (cash, completed) says **in till**.
   *Why:* a rule that is right for the ordinary rows can still be wrong for the one row that is not
   ordinary. Look at it before you trust the column.

6. **Count and total the flag, above the table.** Type `records_visible` in **A1** with
   `=SUBTOTAL(103,A6:A1805)` in **C1**, and `amount_visible_usd` in **A2** with
   `=SUBTOTAL(109,G6:G1805)` in **C2**. Rows 1 to 4 are not part of the table, so a filter never hides
   them. With no filter they show 1800 and 22754.25. Now filter `in_till` to **in till**: **252**
   records and **3159.50**.

7. **Verify against a different route.** Clear that filter and filter `payment_method` to **cash**
   only: **253** records and **3164.50**. The flag removed exactly one cash order, and exactly 5.00:
   order 1461, the canceled one. Clear the filter again: 1800 records.

Answer: **252 orders put cash in the till, \$3,159.50 in all.**

<details>
<summary>Explain it in your own words: why check row 462 and the cash-only count, instead of trusting the formula?</summary>

Because a formula that returns something in every row can still be wrong in the one row that
matters. The cash-only count is an independent route to the same number: it must be larger than the
flag's count by exactly the cash orders the rule was meant to remove. Here that is one, and it is
the canceled order, so the rule did what the words said.

</details>

## Finish it

<Exercise id="excel-if-logic-1">

**Label every order small, medium or large.** The rule: *small* is under \$8, *medium* is \$8 up to but not
including \$16, and *large* is \$16 or more. Use `raw_orders`. Expected output: a `size`
column on every row, and how many orders fall in each size.

The first steps are done for you on the sheet `calc_ex1`:

1. One row is one order, and the data is there as a Table (header in row 5), with the count and the
   total above it in **C1:C2**.
2. The rule is written in words, as above.
3. The formula is yours to type in **H6**, under a new header `size` in **H5**. It starts like this:
   `=IFS(G6<8,"small",`

Now finish it: complete the formula, then count the orders in each size with the filter and
`SUBTOTAL`, and check the edges.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

`IFS` returns the answer of the first test that is true, so order matters. Which test comes first, and
what does the last pair, `TRUE` and a label, catch?

</details>

<details>
<summary>Half-solution</summary>

`=IFS(G6<8,"small",G6<16,"medium",` and then one more pair that catches everything left. The sizes
must add up to 1,800.

</details>

<Solution>

1. The full formula in H6: `=IFS(G6<8,"small",G6<16,"medium",TRUE,"large")`. In an older Excel:
   `=IF(G6<8,"small",IF(G6<16,"medium","large"))`.
2. Filter the `size` column to each label and read `=SUBTOTAL(103,A6:A1805)` in C1: **519** small, **790**
   medium, **491** large. 519 + 790 + 491 = **1,800**: nothing is missing and nothing counts twice.
3. Check the edges. Filter `amount_usd` to 16.00: 17 orders, and all of them say `large`. Filter it to
   8.00: 33 orders, all `medium`. That is what "8 up to but not including 16" means.

Answer: **519 small, 790 medium and 491 large orders.**

</Solution>

</Exercise>

## On your own

<Exercise id="excel-if-logic-2">

**Which orders earn no loyalty points?** The rule: an order earns no points when it has **no customer**
(a walk-in, so `customer_id` is empty) **or** it was **canceled**. Use `raw_orders`. Do not change it:
work on the blank sheet `calc_ex2`, with the data pasted at A5 as in the worked example.

Before you type anything, write on `calc_ex2` what your result should look like: which column, which two
labels, and roughly how many rows you expect to carry the "no points" label. Then build it from a
blank start, and check it by eye.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

The word in the rule is *or*, not *and*. An empty cell is equal to `""`, two quotation marks with
nothing between them. Filter on your flag and look at the rows.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Half-solution</summary>

Expected output: *"A new column on every row: `no points` for walk-ins and canceled orders, `points` for
everything else. I expect only a few rows."* The test has two parts joined with `OR`: `B6=""` and
`F6="canceled"`.

</details>

<Solution>

In **H5** type `points_flag` and in **H6**:
`=IF(OR(B6="",F6="canceled"),"no points","points")`.

Filter the column to `no points`: exactly **2** rows.

| row | order_id | customer_id | store | ordered_on | status | amount_usd |
|---:|---:|---|---|---|---|---:|
| 340 | 1335 | (empty) | Old Town | 2026-02-14 | completed | 7.00 |
| 466 | 1461 | 42 | Campus | 2026-03-01 | canceled | 5.00 |

Order 1335 is the walk-in who had no loyalty card, and order 1461 is the canceled one. The other
**1,798** orders say `points`. Check: 2 + 1,798 = 1,800.

Why `OR` and not `AND`: no order is both a walk-in and canceled, so `AND` would flag **0** rows. When
you expect "a few" and get none, suspect the connecting word first.

</Solution>

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="excel-if-logic-3">

**Spot the flaw.** A colleague labels the sizes from the exercise above with
`=IF(G6>=8,"medium",IF(G6>=16,"large","small"))` (their file is the sheet `calc_ex3`) and says: *"It works, I looked at the first rows and they
are right."* The filter on `size` shows 519 `small`, 1,281 `medium` and no `large` at all. What went wrong, and
how would you find the cause?

<Solution>

An `IF` stops at the first test that is true. Any amount of 16 or more is also 8 or more, so it
is caught by the first test and labeled `medium` before the `large` test is ever reached. The 491 large orders
are all counted among the 1,281 mediums (790 + 491).

The first rows hide the flaw because small and medium amounts come out right. Look at the extremes:
filter `amount_usd` to a big order, or sort by amount, or count the three labels and notice that one is
missing. Fix it by testing the largest first, `=IF(G6>=16,"large",IF(G6>=8,"medium","small"))`, or by
using `IFS` with the bounds in order.

</Solution>

</Exercise>
