## Warm-up

Answer from memory before you open anything. Then click to check.

1. E2 holds `=D2*C$1`. You fill it down to E3. What does E3 contain?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   `=D3*C$1`. `D2` is relative, so it moved down to `D3`. In `C$1` the row is locked, so it stayed
   `C$1`.

   </details>

2. In a column of amounts some cells sit on the left and some on the right. What do you suspect,
   and which formula tests the cell A2?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   Some amounts are text, not numbers. `=ISNUMBER(A2)` gives TRUE for a number and FALSE for text.
   Comparing `COUNT` with `COUNTA` on the column counts them all at once.

   </details>

A **Table** is a range that Excel treats as one object: one header row with filter buttons on every
column, and sorts and filters that always move whole rows. Make one from your data and it is also the
tidy layout from the first lesson: one header row, one record per row.

- **Sort** reorders rows. A sort must carry the whole row along. If you select one column of a plain
  range and sort only that, the order id of one row ends up next to the amount of another. Excel warns
  you before it does that and offers **Expand the selection**. Sort from a Table or from a filter's
  header menu and you do not have to think about it.
- **Filter** hides the rows that do not match. It deletes nothing, and the hidden rows come back when
  you clear the filter. The row numbers skip, as in the picture. Filtering several columns narrows
  step by step: each filter works on what the previous one left.
- **Totals** are where people go wrong. `SUM` adds every cell in its range, hidden or not.
  `SUBTOTAL(109, range)` adds only the rows the filter leaves visible, and `SUBTOTAL(103, range)`
  counts them. A Table's Total Row writes this `SUBTOTAL` for you.

## Worked example

Question: **how much cash did Campus take in March?** Open the practice file and follow along.

1. **Copy the data to `calc` and make it a Table.** Copy all of `raw_orders` to cell **A5** of `calc`,
   so that rows 1 to 4 stay free for your totals. The header is now in row 5 and the 1,800 records run
   from row 6 to row 1805. Click any cell in the data and press **Ctrl+T** (Mac: **Cmd+T**), or use **Home > Format as
   Table**, and tick that the table has headers. In Google Sheets select the data and choose
   **Format > Convert to table**.
   *Why:* the Table finds the header row, adds a filter button to every column, and keeps rows
   together when you sort.

2. **Write your totals above the table, before you filter.** Type the labels in **A1:A3** and the
   formulas in **C1:C3**:

   | A | C | Result |
   |---|---|---:|
   | `records_visible` | `=SUBTOTAL(103,A6:A1805)` | 1800 |
   | `amount_visible_usd` | `=SUBTOTAL(109,G6:G1805)` | 22754.25 |
   | `amount_all_usd` | `=SUM(G6:G1805)` | 22754.25 |

   *Why above the table:* a filter hides whole rows. A total typed beside the table would sit in a row
   the filter can hide, and it would vanish with it. Rows 1 to 4 are not part of the table, so they
   always stay in view. With no filter the three results agree: that is your baseline. If you click
   the cells instead of typing, Excel may write a name such as `Table1[amount_usd]` instead of
   `G6:G1805`. It means the same column.

3. **Say the question as conditions.** Cash at Campus in March means four conditions: `store` is
   Campus, `payment_method` is cash, `ordered_on` is in March, and `status` is completed.
   *Why:* every filter must say one condition, and writing them first shows which columns you need.

4. **Filter the first three.** Use the arrow in each header:
   - `store`: tick only **Campus**. `records_visible` shows **716**.
   - `payment_method`: tick only **cash**. It shows **107**.
   - `ordered_on`: in Excel choose **Date Filters**, then **Between**, and enter 2026-03-01 and
     2026-03-31. In Google Sheets open the filter's **Filter by condition**, choose **Custom
     formula is** and type `=AND(D6>=DATE(2026,3,1),D6<=DATE(2026,3,31))`, where D6 is the first date
     under the header. It shows **18**.

   `amount_visible_usd` now shows **216.25**, but `amount_all_usd` still shows 22754.25: `SUM` ignores
   the filter, `SUBTOTAL` follows it.

5. **Filter the status.** On `status`, tick only **completed**. `records_visible` shows **17** and
   `amount_visible_usd` shows **211.25**.
   *Why:* an order that was canceled took no money, and the owner asked how much cash was taken.

6. **Verify.**
   - The total fell from 216.25 to 211.25: 5.00. Tick **canceled** instead of completed: exactly one row
     appears, order 1461 on March 1, with an amount of 5.00. That explains the difference.
   - Clear all the filters: `amount_visible_usd` goes back to 22754.25 and agrees with `amount_all_usd`.
     If it does not, a filter is still on.

7. **Sort, with the filters cleared.** Use the `amount_usd` arrow to sort largest to smallest. The first
   row is order **2075**, 197.00, at Old Town on May 1: the day with the 20 sandwiches from the formulas
   lesson. Check that the row stayed whole: it still says Old Town, mobile, completed. Now sort by two
   columns: **Data > Sort**, then **Add Level** (in Google Sheets open the sort options in the **Data**
   menu and choose **Add another sort column**). Sort `store` A to Z, then `amount_usd` largest to smallest. The first row is
   Campus's biggest order, 2348, at 68.25.

Answer: **Campus took \$211.25 in cash in March, from 17 completed orders.**

<details>
<summary>Explain it in your own words: why use SUBTOTAL(109) and not SUM for the total?</summary>

Because `SUM` adds all 1,800 rows whether or not a filter hides them, so it never changes. The
`SUBTOTAL` total follows the filter: change the conditions and it recalculates for the rows you can
see, which is what a question about "Campus in March" needs.

</details>

**Harbor's April: what did card payments bring in, and which were the three biggest card orders?** Use

orders and \$1,596.50. Do not change the raw sheet: work on the blank sheet `calc_ex2`.

Before you type anything, write on `calc_ex2` what your result should look like. Then build it from a
blank start. The list is messy.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Check the list before you make a Table: how many rows does it have, and does every row hold a record?
Then run the checks from the numbers lesson on the `amount_usd` column.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Half-solution</summary>

Expected output: *"One total for card payments, and three card order ids with their amounts."* There are
two faults. One stops a Table from covering the whole list; the other makes some amounts invisible to
`SUBTOTAL` and to a sort. Fix both in your copy, then filter, total and sort. Your total
before the filter must equal the manager's \$1,596.50.

</details>

<Solution>

The two faults:

- **A blank row** after the 40th record (row 42 of the raw sheet). A Table made from the top of the
  list stops there, so it covers only 40 of the 123 records. Count them: `COUNTA` of `order_id` over
  the whole list is 123, but the Table has 40. In your copy, delete the blank row.
- **One amount is text**: order 1864, the biggest of the month. `=COUNT` of `amount_usd` gives 122 and
  `=COUNTA` gives 123. `SUBTOTAL` skips the text, so the card total would be 823.25 instead of
  861.50. Add a column `amount` with `=VALUE(G2)` (G is `amount_usd`, and row 2 is the first record if you pasted at A1) and use that column.

Then: make a Table of all 123 records. Check the baseline: the total of `amount` with no filter is
**1596.50**, the manager's figure. Filter `payment_method` = card: **62 orders, \$861.50**. Sort `amount`
largest first.

Answer: **card payments brought in \$861.50 (62 orders). The three biggest card orders were 1864
(\$38.25), 1945 (\$36.75) and 1861 (\$32.00).**

The third biggest order of the month overall is 2006 (\$35.75), but it was paid by mobile, so it is
not a card order: filter first, then sort.

What the faults would have cost you. Make a Table from the top of the list without deleting the blank
row, with a `SUBTOTAL` that points at the Table's own column (which is what you get when you click
the cells), and the card total is \$269.75: the card orders among the first 40 records only. The 83
records below the blank row are not part of the Table, so its filter never touches them. A
`SUBTOTAL` over a fixed range that runs to the bottom of the list would give a different wrong number,
because those 83 rows would be added unfiltered. And if you delete the blank row but leave the text
amount, the card total is \$823.25 and order 1864 does not take its place in the sort.

</Solution>

</Exercise>

## Check yourself

   `=SUBTOTAL(109,G6:G1805)` when a filter is on?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

</Solution>
