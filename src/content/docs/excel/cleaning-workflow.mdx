## Warm-up

Answer from memory before you open anything. Then click to check.

1. A code cell holds `·S2-1001-CARD·` (a **·** is a space). Write the formula that returns the store digit,
   `2`, as a number.

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   **`=VALUE(MID(TRIM(A2),2,1))`**. `TRIM` first, so the position is the same on every row; `MID` cuts one
   character at position 2; `VALUE` turns the text `"1"` into the number 1.

   </details>

2. A lookup should return `UNMAPPED` instead of `#N/A` when the key is not in the table. Which argument of
   `XLOOKUP` does that, and what does the full formula look like for a key in E2 and a table in
   `mapping!A2:B5`?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   The fourth argument, `if_not_found`: **`=XLOOKUP(E2,mapping!$A$2:$A$5,mapping!$B$2:$B$5,"UNMAPPED")`**.
   The `$` signs keep the table in place when you fill down.

   </details>

3. A column of dates sorts oddly and `=MIN()` over it returns 0. What is the cause?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   The dates are text, not numbers. Check with `ISNUMBER`, then rebuild them with `DATE`.

   </details>

Hadley Wickham opens his paper on tidy data with the observation that "a huge amount of effort is spent
cleaning data to get it ready for analysis". The way to make that effort safe is a fixed workflow:

1. **Keep raw untouched.** The export stays on `raw_orders_export`, exactly as it arrived.
2. **Profile before you fix.** Count rows, blanks, duplicates and the different spellings. You cannot
   decide what to change until you know what is there.
3. **Fix on a copy, one step at a time.** Each fix is a decision (what counts as a duplicate, which
   spellings mean the same thing), so each one is written down.
4. **Reconcile.** After every step the row count must be the one you expect, and the end totals must match
   a number from outside the file (here, the till system's report).

## Worked example

Question: **turn the 1,830-row export into clean orders that match the till system's 1,800 orders and
1,799 completed, and log every change.** Open the practice file and follow along.

1. **Say what one row is, and what its key is.** One row should be one order, and the key is `order_id`:
   two rows with the same `order_id` are the same order. The control totals from the till system are
   1,800 orders and 1,799 completed.
   *Why decide now:* "duplicate" has no meaning until you say which columns identify a row.

2. **Copy, do not edit.** Select `raw_orders_export!A1:F1831`, copy, and paste **values** into `calc!A1`.
   The data on `calc` has **1,830 rows** under the header. *Why:* raw stays as it arrived. If you spoil
   the copy, copy it again.

3. **Profile.** On `calc` (to the right of the data, in columns I and up) write down what you find:
   - Rows: `=COUNTA(A2:A1831)` gives **1830**.
   - Blanks: `=COUNTBLANK(B2:B1831)` gives **1**. One order has no `customer_id`: a walk-in without a
     loyalty card. We will leave it blank on purpose (step 8).
   - Duplicate keys: copy `A2:A1831` into `I2`, select that column and run **Remove Duplicates** on it, then
     `=COUNT(I2:I1831)` gives **1800**. So **30 rows are repeats** of an order that already appeared.
   - Spellings: copy `E2:E1831` into `K2` and run Remove Duplicates on it. Five lines remain:
     `completed`, `completed·`, `Complete`, `DONE`, `cancelled·`. Two of them look identical on screen;
     one has a trailing space.
   *Why profile first:* the five spellings and the 30 repeats are the work. Without the profile you would
   clean what you happened to notice.

4. **Remove duplicates on the key.** Select `calc!A1:F1831`. In **Excel**, **Data > Remove Duplicates**; in
   **Google Sheets**, **Data > Data cleanup > Remove duplicates**. Say that your data has a header row and
   tick **only `order_id`**. Excel reports 30 duplicates removed and 1,800 unique values remaining.
   Excel keeps the first row it finds for each value and deletes the later ones (Microsoft's page describes
   it that way); in Excel, **Ctrl+Z** undoes it if you made a mistake.
   *Why only `order_id`:* the key says what an order is. Try it with all six columns ticked (then undo): it
   removes only **20**, leaving **1,810** rows, because the 10 re-sent rows, like order 1173 above, are
   spelled differently and do not match as whole rows.
   *Why now:* do this before you add formula columns next to the data, because Remove Duplicates works
   inside the block you selected.

5. **Write the mapping table.** On `mapping`, type `status_key` in A1 and `status_clean` in B1, then
   four rows: `completed → completed`, `complete → completed`, `done → completed`,
   `cancelled → canceled`. The keys are **lowercase, with no spaces**.
   *Why a table:* "complete" and "done" meaning completed is a decision. In a table anyone can read and
   change it; inside a long formula nobody can.

6. **Apply it.** In `calc!G2` type
   `=XLOOKUP(LOWER(TRIM(E2)),mapping!$A$2:$A$5,mapping!$B$2:$B$5,"UNMAPPED")` and fill down to row 1801.
   *Why `LOWER(TRIM())`:* it standardizes the key before the lookup, so the trailing space and the case
   stop mattering. *Why `"UNMAPPED"`:* a spelling you did not list shows up as a word you can count, not as
   an error or a silent guess.

7. **Reconcile.** `=COUNTIFS(G2:G1801,"UNMAPPED")` is **0**. `=COUNTIFS(G2:G1801,"completed")` is
   **1799** and `=COUNTIFS(G2:G1801,"canceled")` is **1**. `=COUNTA(A2:A1801)` is **1800**. All four match
   the till system.

8. **Decide the blank, then write the log.** The one blank `customer_id` is a walk-in. Putting a 0 there
   would invent a customer, so it stays blank. On `change_log` write one line per step:

   | step | what changed | rows before | rows after | why |
   |---|---|---|---|---|
   | 1 | Copied raw_orders_export to calc as values | 1,830 | 1,830 | Raw stays untouched |
   | 2 | Remove Duplicates on order_id, first row kept | 1,830 | 1,800 | 20 exact copies and 10 re-sends spelled differently |
   | 3 | Mapped status: `completed`, `complete`, `done` → `completed`; `cancelled` → `canceled` | 1,800 | 1,800 | Matches the till system: 1,799 completed, 1 canceled |
   | 4 | Left the blank customer_id blank | 1,800 | 1,800 | One walk-in order, no card |

That answers the owner: **1,800 orders, 1,799 completed and 1 canceled, after removing 30 repeated rows.
Every change is on the log, and the raw sheet is as it arrived.**

<details>
<summary>Explain it in your own words: why did we copy the data before removing duplicates?</summary>

Because Remove Duplicates deletes rows, and Microsoft's page advises copying the original range to another
sheet or workbook first. On a copy, a mistake costs you one more copy and paste. On the raw sheet it can
cost you the only record of what the export said.

</details>

<Aside type="note" title="Excel and Google Sheets">
Remove Duplicates exists in both: in Excel it compares the text you can see, and Google's help says that
in Sheets "cells with identical values but different letter cases, formatting, or formulas are considered
to be duplicates". `XLOOKUP` is in Google Sheets' function list, but Microsoft says it "is not available in Excel 2016
and Excel 2019"; there, use the `INDEX` with `MATCH` pattern from the lesson on reading VLOOKUP and
INDEX with MATCH.
</Aside>

## Check yourself

1. Without looking back: why does a cleaning workflow end with a reconciliation against a number from
   outside the file?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   Because a file can be internally consistent and still wrong. Row counts that match the till system's
   totals prove nothing was dropped or doubled; a total you computed from the same file proves very little.

   </details>

<Exercise id="excel-cleaning-workflow-3">

**Spot the flaw.** Open `calc_ex3`: it holds a colleague's result, with her two totals in M2 and N2. She
cleaned the order export like this: *"I selected all six columns on
`raw_orders_export`, ran Remove Duplicates, and saved. Now it has 1,810 rows. I mapped the status labels,
and I get 1,809 completed orders. Done."* The till system says 1,800 orders and 1,799 completed. Name every
problem you can find.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

There are three. One is about where she worked, one about what she compared, one about what she did not
write down. Which of her numbers disagree with the till system, and by how much?

</details>

<details>
<summary>Half-solution</summary>

Her counts are 10 too high. Ten rows are orders that arrived twice with different spellings. Why did
Remove Duplicates keep them when she ticked every column?

</details>

<Solution>

1. **She edited the raw sheet.** Remove Duplicates deletes rows, and once the file is saved and closed there
   is no undo. The export as it arrived no longer exists, and nobody can check what she removed.
2. **She compared whole rows, not the key.** Ticking all six columns removed only the 20 exact copies. The
   10 re-sent orders, spelled differently, survived: 1,810 rows instead of 1,800. Her completed count of
   **1,809** is **10 too high** compared with the till system's 1,799: those ten orders are counted twice.
   The mismatch with the control total was the clue, and she did not compare.
3. **No log.** Nobody can tell which rows she removed, which rule she used, or that the 10 are still
   there.

The fix: copy the raw data, remove duplicates on `order_id` only (1,800 rows), map the labels, reconcile
to 1,800 and 1,799, and log each step.

</Solution>

</Exercise>
