## Warm-up

Answer from memory before you open anything. Then click to check.

1. In E2 you multiply the amount in D2 by a tax rate that sits in H1. Write the formula so it still
   works when you fill it down to E3, E4 and so on.

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   `=D2*$H$1`. The dollar signs lock H1 on both the row and the column, so every filled-down copy still
   points at the tax rate. D2 is free to move to D3, D4 and so on.

   </details>

2. You sort only column B of a list and leave the other columns where they are. What have you done to
   the list?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   You broke its rows. Every value in B now sits next to the wrong values from the other columns.
   Sort the whole table, and the rows move together. A lookup depends on this: the code and the value
   you want must stay in the same row.

   </details>

3. What must be true of the first row of a list before you can turn it into a Table?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   It holds one header row with a name for each column, and below it there are no blank rows, merged
   cells or totals mixed into the data.

   </details>

## The question

The owner of the cafes asks:

> What did we sell in total from January to June? I only trust the number if it matches the till's
> own report.

The till gives you two tables. First, `raw_order_items`: one row per product line, with the product
**code** but no price. Second, `raw_products`: one row per product, with its code and its price.

| order_item_id | order_id | product_id | quantity | discount_usd |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 5001 | 1001 | 10 | 1 | 0.00 |
| 5002 | 1001 | 6 | 1 | 0.00 |
| 5003 | 1001 | 1 | 2 | 0.00 |

| product_id | product_name | category | unit_price_usd |
|---:|---|---|---:|
| 1 | Espresso | coffee | 3.00 |
| 2 | Americano | coffee | 3.50 |
| 3 | Latte | coffee | 4.50 |

The amount of a line is `quantity × unit_price_usd − discount_usd`, but the price lives in the other
table. By the end of this lesson you will bring it into every line by its code, and you will prove the
total against the `raw_till_report` sheet instead of trusting your formulas.

A **lookup** takes a **key** (here the product code), finds the row that has the same key in another
table, and brings back a value from that row. It pairs rows by the key, not by position: line 5001 is
the first line, but its product, code 10, sits far below code 1 in the products table. This is the
spreadsheet version of a **join**, which you will meet again in SQL: two tables, one key, and some
rows that may find no partner. The second picture shows such a row, and the two things a formula can
do about it.

## Worked example

Question: **what do all the order lines add up to, and does it match the till report?** Open the
practice file and follow along.

1. **Say what one row is, and write the expected output.** On `calc`, click **I1** and type: *"One row
   per order line, a unit price and an amount on every row, and one total that equals the
   `raw_till_report` total."* *Why:* one row is one product line, and a total that is computed from
   a lookup is only worth something if you know in advance what it must equal.

2. **Copy the lines to `calc`.** Go to `raw_order_items`, click the small square at the top left of the
   grid to select everything, copy, click **A1** on `calc`, and paste. *Why:* the raw sheet is never
   changed. The new columns go next to the lines they belong to, on your copy.

3. **Name the key and the two lists.** The key is `product_id`, column **C** of the lines. Where do
   you look for it? In `raw_products`, column **A**, rows 2 to 17. What do you want back? The price,
   column **D**, same rows. *Why exact:* one product code has exactly one price. A near match would
   bring back the price of another product and still look like a price. That is why `XLOOKUP`
   matches exactly unless you ask otherwise (its `match_mode` argument defaults to 0, exact).

4. **Write the lookup.** In **F1** type `unit_price_usd`. In **F2** type:

   ```text
   =XLOOKUP(C2,raw_products!$A$2:$A$17,raw_products!$D$2:$D$17)
   ```

   Read it as: *find C2 in the codes, bring back the price at the same position.* The result for the
   first line is **3.25** (a croissant). *Why the dollar signs:* when you fill the formula down,
   `C2` should become `C3`, `C4` and so on, but the products table must stay where it is. The dollar
   signs lock it. Without them the lookup table slides down with the formula (see *Check yourself*).

5. **Fill it down.** Double-click the small square at the bottom right corner of F2: the formula
   fills down to row **3288**, the last line. *Why:* the whole column needs the formula, not only the
   cells you looked at.

6. **Work out the amount.** In **G1** type `amount_usd` and in **G2** type `=D2*F2-E2`, then fill down.
   *Why:* that is the rule from the dataset: quantity times price, minus the discount on the line.

7. **Verify.** Do three checks, in this order.
   - *By eye:* line 5002 has code 6. In `raw_products`, code 6 is Cold Brew at 4.75, so the amount is
     4.75. Line 5003 has code 1 (Espresso, 3.00) and quantity 2, so its amount is **6.00**. Both match
     your column. The three lines of order 1001 add up to 3.25 + 4.75 + 6.00 = **14.00**.
   - *Every row has a price:* `=COUNT(F2:F3288)` gives **3287**, the number of lines.
   - *The total:* `=SUM(G2:G3288)` gives **22,754.25**. On `raw_till_report` the till says the same:
     3287 lines and a total of 22754.25.

   *Why reconcile:* a lookup that picks the wrong row, or misses a row, still fills the column with
   numbers. A total that equals an independent control total is the proof.

   **Excel and Google Sheets:** the formula is the same in both, and its fourth argument has a
   different name. Excel calls it `if_not_found`, Google Sheets calls it `missing_value`. Both default
   to `#N/A`. Google's `lookup_range` must be a single row or a single column.

<details>
<summary>Explain it in your own words: why did we lock the products range but not C2?</summary>

Because the two ranges play different roles. C2 is the code of *this* line, so it must move to the next
line when you fill down. The products table is the same for every line, so it must stay put. Locked
means "the same for every row", free means "one per row".

</details>

<Aside type="caution" title="XLOOKUP needs a recent Excel">
Microsoft's help page says `XLOOKUP` is not available in Excel 2016 and Excel 2019. If you type it
there and get `#NAME?`, your version is too old. The next lesson, *Reading VLOOKUP and INDEX with
MATCH*, shows the older formulas that do the same job. Excel 2021, Excel 2024, Microsoft 365 and
Google Sheets all have `XLOOKUP`.
</Aside>

<Aside type="tip" title="One line to look at">
One line in the file has a quantity of 20, where 2 was meant: line 6952, 20 turkey sandwiches in
order 2075. It is included in the total of 22,754.25, so the till total and yours agree, and the line
is still worth a second look. Matching a control total proves your formula, not the data.
</Aside>

## Finish it

<Exercise id="excel-xlookup-1">

**What loyalty tier does each order belong to, and how many orders are in each tier?** `calc` already holds the worked example. Start from a clean one: click the small square at the top left of the grid, press Delete, or add a new sheet and use that. On `calc`, copy
`raw_orders` (1,800 orders) and add a `loyalty_tier` column with the tier from `raw_customers`.
Expected output: one row per order, a tier on every row, and a visible flag, not an error, on any
order without a customer, plus a check that the tiers add up to the number of orders.

The first steps are done for you:

1. One row is one order. The key is `customer_id`, column **B** of the orders.
2. The key list is `raw_customers`, column **A**, rows 2 to 151, and the value to bring back is the
   tier, column **D**. `raw_orders` is copied to `calc` and the new column is **G**.

Now finish it: write the lookup in **G2**, fill it down, and check it.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

One order has no customer. What should its row show? Decide that first, then look at what the fourth
argument of `XLOOKUP` is for.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Half-solution</summary>

The formula has the shape `=XLOOKUP(B2, key list, tier list, "no card")` with both lists locked. To
check, count how many cells equal each tier with `=COUNTIF(G2:G1801,"bronze")` and so on, and add
the counts up.

</details>

<Solution>

In **G2**:

```text
=XLOOKUP(B2,raw_customers!$A$2:$A$151,raw_customers!$D$2:$D$151,"no card")
```

Fill down to row **1801**. The tiers are **bronze 1,044**, **silver 582**, **gold 173** and **no card 1**:
1,044 + 582 + 173 + 1 = **1,800**, the number of orders.

The one unmatched row is order **1335** (row 336): a walk-in without a loyalty card, so `customer_id`
is empty and there is nothing to find. Without the fourth argument that one cell shows `#N/A`, and
any `SUM` or `AVERAGE` over a column built from it would fail too. With `"no card"` the row is flagged
in words, and you can count it.

</Solution>

</Exercise>

## On your own

<Exercise id="excel-xlookup-2">

**Price a delivery partner's list.** A delivery partner sent `raw_delivery_lines`: one row per line
(`delivery_ref`, `item_code`, `quantity`) for Harbor, June 8 to 14. Prices are in `raw_products`. Do not
change the raw sheet. `calc` already holds the worked example. Start from a clean one: click the small square at the top left of the grid, press Delete, or add a new sheet and use that. Build the result on `calc`.

Before you type a formula, write one sentence on `calc` saying what the result should look like: how many
rows, what each row shows, and what happens to a line whose code is not on our menu. Then build it.
The partner's note says that 41 of the 43 lines are on our menu, and that they come to **\$364.75**.
Your total must match it, and you should be able to say why two lines are left out.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Give every code a "not on menu" flag first and see which lines it flags. Is the count what you expected?
Compare a flagged code with the same number in the products table: what is different about the cell
itself, not about the number?

</details>

<details>
<summary>Half-solution</summary>

Expected output: *"43 rows, one per delivery line, with a price and an amount on every line that is on
our menu, the words `not on menu` on the others, and a total for the lines on the menu."* Eight of the
flagged lines are not really missing. Their codes were typed as text, or with a trailing space, and a
lookup treats the text `"6"` and the number 6 as different. `VALUE` turns text into a number and `TRIM`
removes stray spaces.

</details>

<Solution>

1. **First try:** `=XLOOKUP(raw_delivery_lines!B2,raw_products!$A$2:$A$17,raw_products!$D$2:$D$17,"not on menu")`
   flags **10** lines, not the 2 you would expect. Their total on the other 33 lines is only
   **297.00**.
2. **Why:** `item_code` is a number on most rows, but on 6 rows it is text (`"14"`, `"3"`, `"5"`, `"11"`,
   `"8"`, `"2"`) and on 2 rows it is text with a trailing space (`"3 "` and `"4 "`). These sit on the left
   of their cells. `=ISNUMBER(raw_delivery_lines!B5)` is `FALSE` for them. The other 2 flagged
   lines are real: code **17** is not on the menu (lines D-013 and D-034).
3. **Fix on `calc`**, in row 2 and then filled down to row 44:
   - **A2:** `=raw_delivery_lines!A2`
   - **B2:** `=VALUE(TRIM(raw_delivery_lines!B2))`, the cleaned code as a real number
   - **C2:** `=XLOOKUP(B2,raw_products!$A$2:$A$17,raw_products!$D$2:$D$17,"not on menu")`
   - **D2:** `=IF(ISNUMBER(C2),raw_delivery_lines!C2*C2,0)`, an amount only where there is a price
4. **Check.** `=COUNT(C2:C44)` gives **41** lines with a price, `=COUNTIF(C2:C44,"not on menu")` gives
   **2**, and 41 + 2 = **43**. `=SUM(D2:D44)` gives **364.75**, equal to the partner's note.

The first try was **67.75 too low**, with 8 lines wrongly flagged. The `IF(ISNUMBER(...))` in D keeps the text
`not on menu` from breaking the multiplication.

</Solution>

</Exercise>

## Check yourself

1. Without looking back: write the formula that brings the product name for the code in C2, from
   `raw_products` (codes in A2:A17, names in B2:B17), so that it survives being filled down.

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   `=XLOOKUP(C2,raw_products!$A$2:$A$17,raw_products!$B$2:$B$17)`. The code is free to move, the two

<Exercise id="excel-xlookup-3">

**Spot the flaw.** A colleague writes the price lookup in F2 without dollar signs:

```text
=XLOOKUP(C2,raw_products!A2:A17,raw_products!D2:D17)
```

They fill it down and check the first two lines by eye: 3.25 and 4.75, both right. But `=SUM(G2:G3288)`
shows `#N/A`. "The data must be dirty," they say. What went wrong?

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Click a cell far down column F and read its formula. Is it the same as F2 apart from the row number?

</details>

<details>
<summary>Half-solution</summary>

In row 3 the lookup range is A3:A18 instead of A2:A17. Which product code is no longer inside it?

</details>

<Solution>

The data is fine, the formula is not. Without dollar signs the two ranges move down with the formula.
In row 3 the lookup table is A3:A18, so product 1 is no longer in it, and the third line (code 1,
Espresso) shows `#N/A`. By row 18 the ranges point at empty cells below the table. Only **6 of 3,287**
lines get a price, and the other **3,281** show `#N/A`, and `SUM` passes the error on to the total.

The first two rows were right only by luck: their codes were still inside the sliding range. Checking
the first rows by eye is not enough; check one row far down too.

To find it, click a cell far down column F and press **F2** (Excel): the colored outlines show the
lookup range, far from the products table. The fix is `$A$2:$A$17` and `$D$2:$D$17`.

</Solution>

</Exercise>

**Can you do it?** *I can bring a value into one table from another by its key, show a readable flag
where the key is missing, and prove the lookup column by reconciling a total.*
Test: Exercise excel-xlookup-2, done from a blank `calc` sheet without opening the hints.

The pattern to remember: **find the key, match it exactly, decide what an unmatched row shows, then
reconcile a total.** In SQL this becomes a `JOIN`, and the unmatched rows are what a `LEFT JOIN` keeps.
