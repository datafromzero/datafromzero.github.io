## Warm-up

Try each one from memory, then open the answer. Forgot? Follow the link back to that part of the lesson: looking back is part of the review.

1. The date of an order line is in cell C2. Write the formula that gives its month number (from [*Dates as numbers*](/excel/date-functions/#worked-example), E11).

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   `=MONTH(C2)`. For 2026-01-01 it returns 1. A date is a number with a date format, so `MONTH`,
   `YEAR` and `DAY` can take it apart.

   </details>

2. A cell shows 2026-04-01 and sits on the left of its cell. How do you check whether it is a real
   date (from [*Numbers are not numbers until you check*](/excel/numbers-dates-text-types/#the-picture), E4)?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   `=ISNUMBER(C2)`. If it returns `FALSE`, the cell holds text that only looks like a date. A real
   date lines up on the right.

   </details>

3. After you build a pivot, what must its grand total equal (from [*Summarize with a PivotTable*](/excel/pivottables-build/#worked-example), E15)?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   The total of the source column (the status-bar Sum), less any rows you filtered out on purpose.
   If it does not, the pivot is missing rows or counting some twice.

   </details>

## The question

The owner of the cafes asks:

> How did sales move month by month? And in each month, how much of the sales did each store bring?

A pivot of sales by day answers neither: it has 181 rows, one for every day from January 1 to June 30.
Here are its first rows (completed orders, all stores):

| date | sales_usd |
|---|---:|
| 2026-01-01 | 45.50 |
| 2026-01-02 | 75.75 |
| 2026-01-03 | 65.50 |

By the end of this lesson you will turn that list into six month rows, show each store's share of each
month, and be able to say exactly which total every percentage divides by.

**Grouping** gathers many source values into one label: here, 181 dates into six months, with the
sums adding up to the same grand total. **Show values as** keeps the pivot's cells but changes what
they display. The percentage options differ only in the number they divide by (Microsoft's wording):

- **% of Grand Total**: "the grand total of all the values or data points in the report";
- **% of Column Total**: "the total for the column or series";
- **% of Row Total**: "the total for the row or category";
- **Running Total In**: "the value for successive items in the Base field as a running total".

The same cell can show three different percentages. Take Harbor in April, 1,596.50 of sales, with
months in the rows and stores in the columns:

| Show values as | Divides 1,596.50 by | Harbor in April shows |
|---|---:|---:|
| % of Grand Total | 22,749.25 (all stores, six months) | 7.0% |
| % of Column Total | 4,606.25 (Harbor's three months) | 34.7% |
| % of Row Total | 4,390.75 (all stores, April) | 36.4% |

All three are correct. They answer three different questions, so the sentence you write must say
which one you asked.

## Worked example

Question: **in each month, what share of the sales did each store bring?** Open the practice file and
follow along.

1. **Name the denominator first.** The question is "share of the month", with months in the rows. So
   each cell must be divided by its *row total*, and each row will add up to 100%. *Why:* if you
   click first, you will pick whichever percentage looks nicest. Choosing the denominator in words
   comes before choosing it in a menu.

2. **Copy the data to `calc` and check the dates.** Copy `raw_order_lines` to `calc` as in the last
   lesson (in Excel, make it a Table). Then type `=ISNUMBER(C2)` in a spare cell: it must return
   `TRUE`. *Why:* only real dates can be grouped. Delete the spare cell afterwards.

3. **Build the base pivot.** Put `date` in Rows, `store` in Columns, `amount_usd` in Values (Sum), and
   `status` in Filters, set to **completed**.
   - **Excel:** **Insert > PivotTable**, then drag the fields in the field list.
   - **Google Sheets:** **Insert > Pivot table**, then **Add** next to Rows, Columns, Values and
     Filters.

   In Google Sheets you get one row per day: 181 rows. Current Excel may already show the dates
   grouped into months when you drop the field in Rows; if it does, right-click a date and choose
   **Ungroup** to see the 181 days. The grand total is **22,749.25** either way. *Why:* this is the
   same total as in the last lesson, and it is the number grouping must not change.

4. **Group the dates into months.**
   - **Excel:** right-click a date in the pivot and choose **Group**. In the box, under **By**, choose
     **Months** and select **OK**.
   - **Google Sheets:** right-click a date cell, choose **Create pivot date group**, then choose the
     period to group by: month.

   There are now **6 rows** (in Excel, if the dates were already months, check that you see them), and the grand total is still **22,749.25**. April reads **4,390.75**.
   *Why:* 181 daily rows hide the pattern; six month rows answer the question. Grouping only changes
   the labels, so any change in the grand total means a mistake.

5. **Add a slicer to filter the pivot.**
   - **Excel:** click inside the pivot, choose **Insert > Slicer**, tick `store` and select **OK**.
     Click **Harbor** in the slicer: the pivot shows only Harbor, with a grand total of **4,606.25**
     (April 1,596.50, May 1,546.50, June 1,463.25). Select **Clear Filter** in the slicer to bring
     the other stores back. For dates, **PivotTable Analyze > Insert Timeline** adds a slider over years,
     quarters, months or days.
   - **Google Sheets:** click the pivot table, choose **Data > Add a slicer**, then pick `store` as
     the column. Sheets has a slicer; this lesson does not use a timeline there.

   *Why:* a slicer shows on the sheet what is filtered, and anyone can change it with a click. The
   Harbor total matches the 4,606.25 you saw in the last lesson.

6. **Show the values as a share of the row total.**
   - **Excel:** right-click a number in the pivot, choose **Show Values As**, then **% of Row Total**.
   - **Google Sheets:** click **Edit** under the pivot table. In the **Values** card, open **Show as**
     (it says **Default**) and choose **% of row**. The other choices are **% of column** and
     **% of grand total**.

   April now reads: Old Town **35.1%**, Campus **28.5%**, Harbor **36.4%**. Check by hand:
   1,596.50 ÷ 4,390.75 = 0.3636, so **36.4%**. Every row adds to 100%. *Why:* each cell is divided by its row's total, which is exactly the "share of the month"
   you named in step 1. Now try **% of Column Total** and **% of Grand Total** on the same cell, and
   compare with the table in *The picture*.

7. **Add a running total (Excel).** Put `amount_usd` in Values a second time. Right-click one of its
   numbers, choose **Show Values As > Running Total In**, and pick `date` as the base field. The
   April row shows **13,166.00**, and the last row, June, shows **22,749.25**. *Why:* the running
   total answers "how much have we sold so far". Check: 3,109.75 + 2,670.25 + 2,995.25 + 4,390.75 =
   13,166.00, and the last row must equal the grand total.
   **Google Sheets** pivot tables have no running-total option in **Show as**. Put a running sum beside
   the pivot instead: each month is the row above plus this month.

8. **Write the finding in one sentence.** Switch back to sums and read the table:

   > *Monthly sales rose from 3,109.75 in January to 5,103.25 in May. In April the new Harbor store
   > brought 36.4% of the month's sales in its first month, while Old Town and Campus together sold
   > less (2,794.25) than in March (2,995.25).*

That answers the owner: **the rise starts in April, and it is the new store.**
(May's 5,103.25 includes the 20-sandwich line of 185.00 from the last lesson. With 2 sandwiches it
would be 4,936.75, still the highest month.)

<details>
<summary>Explain it in your own words: why did step 1 come before step 6?</summary>

Because the menu offers three percentages that look alike and give different numbers for the same
cell. The question, "a store's share of the month", already says which total to divide by: the
month's. If you only decide after seeing the numbers, you can end up with 7.0% or 34.7% and a
sentence that does not match them.

</details>

**Spot the flaw.** A colleague builds the pivot from the worked example (months in the rows, stores in

<Solution>

The 7.0% is Harbor's April sales (1,596.50) divided by the **grand total** of all six months and all
stores (22,749.25). It is the share of the whole half year, not of April. April's own total is
4,390.75, so Harbor made up 1,596.50 ÷ 4,390.75 = **36.4% of April**, the biggest share of any store
that month. Her sentence reads a grand-total percentage as a row percentage.

To check, switch the value to a plain sum and divide by hand: the number you divide by tells you which
percentage you are looking at. The fix is **% of Row Total**, and a sentence that names the
denominator: *"Harbor brought 36.4% of April's sales."*
