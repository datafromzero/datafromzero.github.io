## Warm-up

Answer from memory before you open anything. Then click to check.

1. Write a formula that adds the values in B2 to B17.

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   `=SUM(B2:B17)`. The colon joins the first and the last cell of the range.

   </details>

2. You type `=B2*C2` in D2 and fill it down. What does D3 contain, and why?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   `=B3*C3`. The formula moved down one row, so every address in it moved down one row too. This
   lesson is about that move: when it helps, and when it breaks things.

   </details>

By default a reference is **relative**: it means "the cell this far away from me". When you copy a
formula, every relative address moves by the same number of rows and columns as the formula did.
That is what made filling down work in the last lesson, and it is what broke the picture above. A
`$` in front of a column letter or a row number **locks** it, so it stays where it is.

| Written | Copied sideways | Copied down | Name |
|---|---|---|---|
| `F2` | column moves | row moves | relative |
| `$F$2` | column stays | row stays | absolute |
| `F$2` | column moves | row stays | mixed: row locked |
| `$F2` | column stays | row moves | mixed: column locked |

Read the `$` as "the part right after me stays". You do not have to type it: in Excel on Windows,
select the address in the formula bar and press **F4**, and each press cycles through the four
types. In Excel on a Mac, use **Cmd+T** (or **F4**). In Google Sheets press **F4** while you type the
formula (on a Mac **Fn+F4**). Or just type the dollar signs yourself.

## Worked example

Question: **what share of the six months' sales did each product bring in?** Open the practice file
and follow along.

1. **Copy the data to `calc`.** Select everything on `raw_product_sales`, copy it, and paste it in
   cell **A1** of `calc`.
   *Why:* the raw sheet stays as it arrived.

2. **Put the total in one cell of its own.** In **F1** type `total_sales_usd`. In **F2** type
   `=SUM(C2:C17)`. It shows **22749.25**.
   *Why:* every share divides by the same number, so that number gets one cell. Keeping it beside
   the table, not inside it, keeps the table tidy.

3. **Say what the new column should look like, then write its first formula.** In **D1** type
   `share`. The expected result: one share per product, each a fraction of 22,749.25, all 16
   adding up to 100%. In **D2** type `=C2/F2` and press Enter. Format the column as a percentage (the
   % button in the toolbar). D2 shows **3.8%**: Espresso, 855.00 out of 22,749.25.

4. **Predict, then fill down.** Before you copy it, write down what D3 will contain. Then select
   **D2:D17** and press **Ctrl+D** (Mac: **Cmd+D**).

5. **Read what happened.** D3 shows **#DIV/0!**. Click D3: it holds `=C3/F3`. Click D4: `=C4/F4`.
   *Why:* `C2` moved down to `C3`, which is what you wanted. But `F2` moved down to `F3` too, and F3
   is empty, so the division fails. One correct cell was not yet a correct column.

6. **Lock the total.** Click D2. In the formula bar select `F2` and press **F4** (or type the
   dollar signs): the formula becomes `=C2/$F$2`. Fill it down again over **D2:D17**.
   *Why:* `$F$2` is locked in both directions, so it ignores the copy. `C2` has no `$`, so it still
   moves, one row per row.

7. **Verify.**
   - Click D3 and D17. They hold `=C3/$F$2` and `=C17/$F$2`: the moving part moved, the locked part
     did not.
   - Check one row by hand: Latte, 2927.00 / 22749.25 = 0.1287, which shows as **12.9%**.
   - In **H2** type `=SUM(D2:D17)`. It must be **100%**.
   - Row 17, Pumpkin Spice Latte, shows **0.0%**. That is correct: it was never sold, so its sales
     are 0, and the cell is a true zero, not an error.

Answer: **Latte leads with 12.9% of sales, then Cappuccino with 9.3% and Turkey Sandwich with 8.7%.**

These shares use the data **as recorded**, including order 2075 with its 20 Turkey Sandwiches, the
typo from the formulas lesson (2 was meant). That is fine here, because this lesson is about
references and the checks are the same whatever the data says. It does move the answer, though: with 2
sandwiches, Latte would be 13.0%, Cappuccino 9.4%, Cold Brew 8.2% and Turkey Sandwich only 8.0%, in
fourth place. In real work you would ask the manager before you report either set.

<details>
<summary>Explain it in your own words: why did the formula work in row 2 and fail in row 3?</summary>

In row 2 the total happens to sit in F2, right where the unlocked formula points. Copying moves
every unlocked address by one row, so in row 3 the formula looked at F3, which holds nothing. The
first row was right by luck of position, not because the formula was right. That is why you check a
later row, not just the first.

</details>

## Finish it

<Exercise id="excel-cell-references-1">

**What share of the units sold did each product account for?** Use the `units_sold` column. Expected
output: a column of 16 shares that adds up to 100%, the product with the largest share, and the
share of Avocado Toast. As in the worked example, the figures are the data as recorded.

The first steps are done for you on the sheet `calc_ex1`:

1. One row is one product; the data is copied in.
2. The total of the units is in **G2**: `=SUM(B2:B17)`, which shows 5161.
3. The first formula is in **E2**: `=B2/G2`, which shows 5.5%.

Now finish it: fill the column down, find out whether it is right, fix it if it is not, and check
the total.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Click E3 after you fill down. Which address moved that should have stayed?

</details>

<details>
<summary>Half-solution</summary>

`G2` has to stay put when the formula moves down. Lock it, then fill down again and add up column E.

</details>

<Solution>

1. Fill down **E2:E17**: from E3 on, every cell shows **#DIV/0!**, because `G2` slid to `G3`, which is
   empty.
2. Fix E2: `=B2/$G$2`. Fill down again over **E2:E17**. (For filling down only, `G$2` works as well: the
   row is the part that would move. When you are unsure, lock both.)
3. Check: `=SUM(E2:E17)` gives **100%**. Latte, 653 / 5161, shows **12.7%**.

Answer: **Latte has the largest share of units (12.7%), Avocado Toast has 4.0%,** and Pumpkin Spice
Latte is 0.0%.

Compare with the worked example: Avocado Toast is only 4.0% of units but 7.7% of sales, because it
costs 8.50 while every coffee costs between 3.00 and 5.50. A share of units and a share of sales are two different
questions. Both of those Avocado Toast figures, and Latte's 12.7%, are the same with or without the
order 2075 typo; only the Turkey Sandwich row changes (4.1% of units as recorded, 3.8% with 2 instead
of 20).

</Solution>

</Exercise>

## On your own

<Exercise id="excel-cell-references-2">

**Each store's share of each month's sales.** Open `raw_store_month`: sales in USD for the three
stores (rows) and six months (columns). Do not change it: it is raw data. Build the answer on the
blank sheet `calc_ex2`.

Before you type anything, write one sentence on `calc_ex2` saying what the result should look like: how
many rows and columns, what each cell is, and what each column should add up to. Then build it with
**one formula** copied across and down, and check it.

Note that Harbor opened on April 1, so its cells for January to March are empty.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

You need the total of each month first. Where does that total sit relative to the cell you are
dividing, and when you copy the formula across and down, which of the two addresses must not move
down?

</details>

<details>
<summary>Half-solution</summary>

Expected output: *"A grid of 3 rows by 6 columns, one cell per store and month, each the store's share
of that month's sales; every month column adds up to 100%."* Add a total row with `=SUM(B2:B4)`
under the data and copy it across. The divisor must keep its row: `B$5`.

</details>

<Solution>

1. On `calc_ex2`, copy `raw_store_month` to A1. In **A5** type `total`, in **B5** `=SUM(B2:B4)` and fill
   it right to **G5**. `SUM` skips Harbor's empty cells.
2. Repeat the headers in row 7. Put the store names in **A8:A10**.
3. In **B8** type `=B2/B$5`. Fill it right to **G8**, then down to row 10, and format as a percentage.
   *Why `B$5`:* copied across, the column moves (B, C, D...) so each month divides by its own total.
   Copied down, the row must stay at 5, because the totals are in one row. Row locked, column free.
4. Check: each month column adds up to 100%, and the totals in row 5 are 3109.75, 2670.25, 2995.25,
   4390.75, 5103.25 and 4480.00.

| store | Jan | Feb | Mar | Apr | May | Jun |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Old Town | 51.2% | 43.5% | 51.9% | 35.1% | 36.6% | 31.8% |
| Campus | 48.8% | 56.5% | 48.1% | 28.5% | 33.1% | 35.5% |
| Harbor | 0.0% | 0.0% | 0.0% | 36.4% | 30.3% | 32.7% |

Harbor shows 0.0%, not an error, for January to March: an empty cell counts as 0, and the divisor is
the month's total, which is never zero. Look at what the shares say: Old Town's share fell from
51.2% in January to 31.8% in June, yet its sales went from \$1,591.00 to \$1,426.00. A share can fall
because the total grew: Harbor opened. (Old Town's May figure includes order 2075 as recorded; January and June, the months compared here, are not affected by it.)

If you wanted each store's months as a share of that store's own six-month total, kept in a column H,
the lock would move to the column: `=B2/$H2`.

</Solution>

</Exercise>

## Check yourself

1. Without looking back: in D2 you write `=$A2*B$1`. You copy D2 to E3, one column to the right and one
   row down. What does E3 contain?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   `=$A3*C$1`. `$A` is column-locked, so it stays A, but its row moved from 2 to 3. `B` is free, so it
   moved to C, but `$1` is row-locked, so it stayed 1.

   </details>

<Exercise id="excel-cell-references-3">

**Spot the flaw.** A colleague builds the share column like this: *"In D2 I wrote `=$C$2/$F$2`. D2
shows 3.8%, which is right: 855.00 out of 22,749.25. I filled it down and every row shows 3.8%, so
the shares are consistent."* The file is `calc_ex3`. What went wrong, and how would you find the cause?

<Solution>

They locked too much. `$C$2` is locked in both directions, so every row divides Espresso's sales by
the total. Every row shows Espresso's 3.8%, not "consistent" but identical. The column adds up to
16 × 3.8% = **60.1%**, not 100%.

They checked only the first row, and the first row is right under either formula. To find it, click a
cell further down and read its formula (D5 holds `=$C$2/$F$2`, not `=C5/$F$2`), or add up the column
and notice it is not 100%. The fix is to lock only what must not move: `=C2/$F$2`.

</Solution>

</Exercise>

must reconcile.*
