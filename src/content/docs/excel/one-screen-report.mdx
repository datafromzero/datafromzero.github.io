This lesson works the same in Microsoft Excel and in Google Sheets. Where the two apps differ, both
ways are shown. It uses the dynamic-array formulas `FILTER` and `UNIQUE`, so it needs Microsoft 365 or
current Google Sheets.

**Your practice file:** download
[`excel-one-screen-report.xlsx`](https://github.com/datafromzero/datafromzero.github.io/raw/main/sheets/excel/excel-one-screen-report.xlsx)
and open it in Excel, or upload it to Google Drive and open it with Google Sheets.
It holds real-looking (but invented) data from **cafe-sales**, a small chain of three cafes that we
use through the whole roadmap. The main sheet, `raw_order_lines`, has one row per order line, as in the
pivot lessons.

## Warm-up

Answer from memory before you open anything. Then click to check.

1. On `raw_order_lines`, `amount_usd` is in column J and `status` in column K. Write the formula for the
   total sales of completed orders.

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   `=SUMIFS(J:J,K:K,"completed")`. It gives 22,749.25.

   </details>

2. You are about to chart monthly sales as columns. Where should the value axis start, and what goes in
   the title?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   The axis starts at 0, so heights match values. The title states the takeaway, for example "Sales rose in
   April, when Harbor opened", not "Sum of amount_usd".

   </details>

3. In a pivot of months by store, a cell shows 36.4%. What do you have to know before you can say what
   it means?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   Which total it divides by: the grand total, the column total or the row total. The same cell can
   show 7.0%, 34.7% or 36.4%.

   </details>

## The question

The owner of the cafes asks:

> I want one screen I can open on Monday: total sales, number of orders, average order, and a chart by
> month that I can filter by store. And my accountant must be able to check every number on it.

The last sentence is the hard part. Anyone can paste numbers onto a screen; this lesson is about a
screen that **proves itself**. By the end you will have built it, and a stranger will be able to click
any number and see where it came from.

A **report you can hand over** has four properties:

- **Every number is a formula or a pivot.** A number typed onto the screen is a number nobody can check,
  and it goes stale the day the data changes.
- **Three sheets, one direction.** `raw_order_lines` holds the data and is never edited; `calc` holds the
  checks; `output` is the one screen.
- **One pivot feeds the chart and the slicer.** They stay in step because they read the same table.
- **The screen says how to check it:** its source, its assumptions and the day it was refreshed.

This is the craft that Microsoft's study guide for the Power BI Data Analyst exam lists under "Create
reports": select an appropriate visual, apply slicing and filtering, configure the report page. A
spreadsheet is the place to learn it before you meet a BI tool.

## Worked example

Question: **what does the owner's one screen contain, and where does each number come from?** Open the
practice file and follow along.

1. **Write the expected output first.** *One screen on `output`: three KPI cells (sales, orders, average
   order), one monthly chart with a store slicer, and a three-line footer. Every number is a formula or a
   pivot, and a check on `calc` ties it to the source.* *Why:* it is the framing line for the whole
   build, and the audit at the end is a check against it.

2. **Build the KPI cells.** On `output`, write the labels in A3:A5 and these formulas in B3:B5:

   | Cell | Formula | Result |
   |---|---|---:|
   | B3, Sales | `=SUMIFS(raw_order_lines!J:J,raw_order_lines!K:K,"completed")` | 22,749.25 |
   | B4, Orders | `=COUNTA(UNIQUE(FILTER(raw_order_lines!B:B,raw_order_lines!K:K="completed")))` | 1,799 |
   | B5, Average order | `=B3/B4` | 12.65 |

   *Why `UNIQUE` for orders:* an order has several lines, so counting rows would count lines (3,286),
   not orders. `FILTER` keeps the completed lines' `order_id`, `UNIQUE` keeps one of each, `COUNTA`
   counts them. Check: the dataset has 1,800 orders and one is canceled, so **1,799**. *Why `B3/B4`:*
   the average order must use the same two numbers the screen shows, so the three cells never
   disagree. Format B5 with two decimals. The ranges are whole columns (`J:J`, `B:B`), so lines added later are
   included; the header and any blank cells fail the `"completed"` condition and drop out.

3. **Build the pivot the chart will use.** On `output`, below the KPIs, insert a pivot from
   `raw_order_lines` (select the whole columns A:K, so lines added later are included): `date` in Rows, grouped by month; `amount_usd` in
   Values (Sum); `status` in Filters set to **completed**. Its grand total is **22,749.25**.
   - **Excel:** **Insert > PivotTable**, choose **Existing Worksheet** and a cell on `output`.
   - **Google Sheets:** **Insert > Pivot table**, select the range and a location, then **Add** the fields
     in the editor.

   *Why:* the pivot is the table the chart draws. Reconcile it before charting: the grand total must
   equal the Sales KPI in B3.

4. **Add a check on `calc`.** In `calc!A1` type `=output!B3` and in `calc!A2` the pivot's grand total
   (click the grand total cell and the sheet writes the reference for you). In A3 type
   `=IF(ROUND(A1-A2,2)=0,"OK","CHECK")`. *Why:* two independent routes to the same total, one from a
   formula on the raw sheet and one from the pivot. The cell says "OK" now. If a range is cut short, or
   a row is dropped, it will say "CHECK" before your reader finds out.

5. **Link a chart and a slicer to the same pivot.**
   - **Excel:** click a cell in the pivot, choose **Insert > PivotChart** and pick a column chart. A
     chart made this way follows the pivot: when the pivot is filtered or a slicer is used, "the chart
     will also be filtered" (Microsoft). Then, with the pivot selected, choose **Insert > Slicer** and
     tick `store`.
   - **Google Sheets:** select the pivot table and choose **Insert > Chart**, then click the pivot
     and choose **Data > Add a slicer** with `store` as the column. Google's help page says
     slicers "apply to all charts and pivot tables in a sheet that use the same data set".

   Click **Harbor** in the slicer: the pivot and the chart show only Harbor (three months, total
   **4,606.25**). The check cell on `calc` now says "CHECK": the pivot is filtered and the KPI is not, which is
   why a slicer must be cleared before you hand the file over. Clear the filter, and it says "OK" again.
   *Why:* one pivot behind both means there is nothing to keep in sync by hand.

   Look at the KPI cells: they did **not** change. They read `raw_order_lines` directly, and a slicer
   filters pivots and charts. Google's page says slicers "don't apply to formulas". So label the KPI
   cells **All stores**, and keep the chart's title honest: **Sales by month**, filtered by the slicer.

6. **Write the footer.** Three lines under the chart, with formulas where a number appears:
   - Source: `="Source: raw_order_lines, "&TEXT(COUNTA(raw_order_lines!A:A)-1,"#,##0")&" order lines, Jan 1 to Jun 30, 2026"`,
     which reads "3,287 order lines"
   - Assumptions: `="Completed orders only. Left out: "&COUNTIFS(raw_order_lines!K:K,"canceled")&" canceled line, "&TEXT(SUMIFS(raw_order_lines!J:J,raw_order_lines!K:K,"canceled"),"0.00")&" USD"`,
     which reads "1 canceled line, 5.00 USD".
   - Refreshed: type the date you refreshed the data, for example `2026-11-17`.

   *Why:* a reader can only trust the screen if it says what it contains and what it left out. *Why the
   date is typed:* `=TODAY()` would show today's date every time the file opens, even if the data was last
   refreshed in June.

7. **Audit it like a stranger.** Click each KPI and read the formula bar: no typed numbers. Select the
   `amount_usd` column on `raw_order_lines`: the status bar Sum is **22,754.25**, and
   22,754.25 − 5.00 (canceled) = **22,749.25**, the Sales KPI. The check cell on `calc` says "OK".
   *Why:* if you cannot defend a number in thirty seconds, it is not ready to hand over.

<details>
<summary>Explain it in your own words: why is the check in step 4 a different route to the same total?</summary>

Because a check that repeats the same formula can only repeat the same mistake. The Sales KPI reads the
raw sheet with `SUMIFS`; the pivot reads the same data through its own filters and grouping. If a range
is too short or a filter is wrong, the two routes disagree, and the check cell shows it.

</details>

<Aside type="tip" title="One screen is a limit, not a goal">
Three KPIs, one chart and one slicer fit on one screen; more would push the footer out of sight. Every
element has to earn its place by answering the question you wrote in step 1.
</Aside>

## Finish it

<Exercise id="excel-one-screen-report-1">

**Build the KPI block for one month: June.** The owner wants the same three KPIs for June 2026 only
(completed orders, all stores). Work on the empty sheet `output_ex1`, so the worked example stays
intact. Expected output: **Sales**, **Orders** and **Average order** for June, each a formula.

The first steps are done for you:

1. The block has the same shape as in step 2 of the worked example: labels in A3:A5, formulas in B3:B5.
2. B3 holds the June sales, with two date conditions:
   `=SUMIFS(raw_order_lines!J:J,raw_order_lines!K:K,"completed",raw_order_lines!C:C,">="&DATE(2026,6,1),raw_order_lines!C:C,"<"&DATE(2026,7,1))`
   which shows **4,480.00**.

Now finish it: write the Orders and Average order cells, and check them.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Orders still counts distinct `order_id`s, but the `FILTER` now needs three conditions at once: completed,
on or after June 1, before July 1. Conditions that must all hold are multiplied inside `FILTER`.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Half-solution</summary>

Orders: `COUNTA(UNIQUE(FILTER(raw_order_lines!B:B, …)))` with the three conditions, each in brackets and
joined with `*`. Average order: the sales cell divided by the orders cell.

</details>

<Solution>

1. **Orders (B4):**
   `=COUNTA(UNIQUE(FILTER(raw_order_lines!B:B,(raw_order_lines!K:K="completed")*(raw_order_lines!C:C>=DATE(2026,6,1))*(raw_order_lines!C:C<DATE(2026,7,1)))))`
   shows **353**.
2. **Average order (B5):** `=B3/B4` shows **12.69** (4,480.00 ÷ 353 = 12.6912).
3. **Check:** change the two `DATE` months to each of January to May in turn and write down the results. The
   six months of sales add up to **22,749.25**, and the six order counts (242, 218, 257, 349, 380 and 353)
   add up to **1,799**, the Orders KPI of the whole-period screen. If either sum is off, a month's
   boundary is wrong. June's 4,480.00 also matches the June row of the monthly pivot.

Note the second date condition: `"<"&DATE(2026,7,1)` stops before July 1, so no day belongs to two months.

</Solution>

</Exercise>

## On your own

<Exercise id="excel-one-screen-report-2">

**Build a one-screen report for the Old Town manager.** She wants Old Town's sales, orders and average
order for January to June, a monthly chart, and a slicer to look at one product category at a time.
Use `raw_order_lines`, put the screen on the empty sheet `output_ex2` and the checks on `calc_ex2`.

Before you build anything, write one sentence on `calc_ex2` saying what the result should look like:
which KPI cells, what chart, what slicer, and what the footer must say. Then build it. Everything on
the screen is a formula or a pivot.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

A KPI for one store needs one more condition in each formula. The data has a line that you should not
quietly fix or delete: find the largest `amount_usd` for Old Town and decide what the footer says about it.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Half-solution</summary>

Expected output: *"Three KPI cells for Old Town, one column chart of monthly sales with a category
slicer, and a footer with source, assumptions and refresh date."* For Orders, filter on both store and
status inside `FILTER`.

</details>

<Solution>

1. **KPIs, on `output_ex2`.**
   - Sales: `=SUMIFS(raw_order_lines!J:J,raw_order_lines!D:D,"Old Town",raw_order_lines!K:K,"completed")`
     = **9,144.25**.
   - Orders: `=COUNTA(UNIQUE(FILTER(raw_order_lines!B:B,(raw_order_lines!D:D="Old Town")*(raw_order_lines!K:K="completed"))))`
     = **729**.
   - Average order: `=B3/B4` = **12.54** (9,144.25 ÷ 729 = 12.5436).
2. **Pivot, chart and slicer.** Pivot: `date` grouped by month in Rows, Sum of `amount_usd` in Values,
   `store` = Old Town and `status` = completed in Filters. Column chart from it, axis from 0. A slicer
   on `category`, connected to the same pivot. The months are Jan 1,591.00, Feb 1,162.00, Mar 1,554.50,
   Apr 1,542.25, May 1,868.50, Jun 1,426.00, which add to **9,144.25**, equal to the Sales KPI. Clicking
   the coffee button shows coffee only: 4,507.75.
3. **Check on `calc_ex2`:** the Sales KPI minus the pivot's grand total, expected 0, shown as "OK".
4. **Footer.** Source (a formula with the line count); assumptions; refresh date typed.
   The assumption you must write is about the May 1 line: *"Includes one line of 20 Turkey Sandwiches
   (185.00) on May 1, kept as recorded. If 2 were meant, May would be 1,702.00 and Old Town's total
   8,977.75."* The raw data stays as it arrived, and the reader can see the effect of the doubt.

The KPI cells say **Old Town, all categories**: the category slicer filters the pivot and chart, not the
formulas.

</Solution>

</Exercise>

## Check yourself

1. Without looking back: besides the numbers, what three things must a handed-over report state on the
   sheet?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   Its source (which data and how many rows), its assumptions (what it includes and leaves out), and the

<Exercise id="excel-one-screen-report-3">

**Spot the flaw.** You inherit the sheet `raw_inherited_report`. It shows **Sales 19,320.00**, **Orders
1,532**, **Average order 12.61** and **Data through June 30, 2026**. The pivot from
the worked example says **22,749.25**. The colleague who sent it says: *"The report uses formulas, so it's the
pivot that is wrong."* Who is right, and how do you find out?

<Solution>

The report is wrong; the pivot is right. Click cell B3: the formula is
`=SUMIFS(raw_order_lines!J2:J2800, raw_order_lines!K2:K2800, "completed")`. The data runs to **row 3288**,
so the formula stops 488 lines early: it covers the lines up to June 8 only, and leaves out
**3,429.25** of completed sales (19,320.00 + 3,429.25 = 22,749.25).

Three more problems:

- **Orders** (B4) is a typed 1,532: that is the number of completed orders in the first 2,799 lines,
  typed once and never updated. The real count is **1,799**.
- **Average order** divides one wrong number by another, so it looks plausible: 12.61, against 12.65.
- **Data through June 30** is typed, and it is false: the formulas stop on June 8.

How to find out: select the `amount_usd` column on `raw_order_lines`. The status-bar Sum is 22,754.25;
less the 5.00 canceled line, 22,749.25: the pivot's number. A report that cannot reproduce the source total
is wrong, however formula-driven it looks. Fix: whole-column ranges (`J:J`, `K:K`) or ranges that
cover every row, the orders formula from step 2, a footer built from formulas, and the check cell on `calc`.

</Solution>

</Exercise>
