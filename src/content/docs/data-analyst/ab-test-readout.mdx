## The question

Priya, who runs the budgeting app `app-events`, writes:

> Three tests finished. Which of them can I ship? One line each, and tell me what it is worth in money.

In each test a coin put every new signup into control or the variant. "Bought" means a Plus purchase within 14 days of signing up.

| test | what changed | people | bought, control | bought, variant |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|
| `paywall_v2` | a redesigned paywall | 5,419 | 343 of 2,706 | 415 of 2,713 |
| `welcome_tips` | tips on the first screens | 4,968 | 304 of 2,449 | 306 of 2,519 |

## Warm-up

From memory first, then click to check.

   <details>

   Count the people in each group against the planned split. A sample-ratio test with p below 0.0005 voids the test.

## The big idea

Read a test in a fixed order: the checks that can void it, the effect in the units the decision uses, the rule written before launch, then a memo that ends in a decision.

## Learn by doing

About 30 minutes, 3 ideas: the pre-flight, the effect in people and dollars, the memo. The extractions are read-only (run them on `app-events` in DuckDB); the editors run on their results.

### 1. Check before you read

**Idea.** A *pre-flight check* is a test of the test, run before any effect is read. Three can void a result: the split (C14), the *balance* (do the groups hold the same kinds of people?) and the *window* (has every person had the whole follow-up?). The Microsoft Research page says that every test should pass the sample-ratio check before it is analyzed.

Split p-values, from C14: 0.92 for `paywall_v2`, 0.32 for `welcome_tips`, 0.0002 for `signup_flow_v2`. Window: the last signup of each test plus 14 days ended before the export on July 1.

| test | last signup | follow-up complete by |
|---|---|---|
| `paywall_v2` | March 8 | March 22 |
| `welcome_tips` | April 26 | May 10 |
| `signup_flow_v2` | June 7 | June 21 |

The balance check compares each group's mix of signup platforms. The extraction counts the people of each platform in each group.

```sql
-- read-only: runs on app-events (the editors of this page hold only cafe-sales)
SELECT a.experiment, a.variant AS arm, e.platform, COUNT(*) AS people
FROM experiment_assignments a
JOIN events e ON e.user_id = a.user_id AND e.event_name = 'signup_complete'
GROUP BY a.experiment, a.variant, e.platform
ORDER BY a.experiment, a.variant, e.platform;
```

**Try it:** the largest gap, in points, between the platform shares of the two groups.

<RunSQL query={`WITH c(experiment, arm, platform, people) AS (
  VALUES ('paywall_v2', 'control', 'android', 827), ('paywall_v2', 'control', 'ios', 921), ('paywall_v2', 'control', 'web', 958),
         ('paywall_v2', 'variant', 'android', 818), ('paywall_v2', 'variant', 'ios', 895), ('paywall_v2', 'variant', 'web', 1000),
         ('welcome_tips', 'control', 'android', 815), ('welcome_tips', 'control', 'ios', 864), ('welcome_tips', 'control', 'web', 770),
         ('welcome_tips', 'variant', 'android', 802), ('welcome_tips', 'variant', 'ios', 895), ('welcome_tips', 'variant', 'web', 822),
         ('signup_flow_v2', 'control', 'android', 1020), ('signup_flow_v2', 'control', 'ios', 1070), ('signup_flow_v2', 'control', 'web', 788),
         ('signup_flow_v2', 'variant', 'android', 656), ('signup_flow_v2', 'variant', 'ios', 1187), ('signup_flow_v2', 'variant', 'web', 760)

s AS (
  SELECT experiment, arm, platform, 100.0 * people / SUM(people) OVER (PARTITION BY experiment, arm) AS share
  FROM c
),
d AS (
  SELECT experiment, platform,
         MAX(share) FILTER (WHERE arm = 'variant') - MAX(share) FILTER (WHERE arm = 'control') AS gap
  FROM s
  GROUP BY experiment, platform
)
SELECT experiment, ROUND(MAX(ABS(gap)), 1) AS largest_gap_points
FROM d

ORDER BY experiment;`} />

**Predict:** two tests differ by about a point or less, because chance moves a share by that much. How large is the gap of the third: about 3, 5 or 10 points?

Expected: 3 rows, 2 columns.

*Why:* `signup_flow_v2` differs by 10.2 points; the other two by 1.4 and 1.5. The balance check says where the split broke: Android is 35.4 percent of control and 25.2 percent of the variant. A test that fails a pre-flight check gets no effect read at all; Exercise 2 writes the memo for it.

### 2. The effect in the units of the decision

**Idea.** A gap in points is a statistician's unit. A decision uses people and money, so convert: *per 1,000 signups*, with the same interval. For money, define the metric first: here *revenue per signup* is the list price of the plan a person bought within 14 days, \$7.99 monthly or \$59.99 annual, and \$0 for no purchase. Few people buy the annual plan, but each is worth about eight monthly buyers, so the dollar figure is noisier than the buyer count.

The extraction for `paywall_v2` gives the people and, for the buyers, the plan: control 2,706 people, 262 monthly buyers, 81 annual; variant 2,713 people, 312 monthly, 103 annual.

```sql
-- read-only: runs on app-events
WITH first_plan AS (
  SELECT subscription_id, user_id, MIN(started_on) AS started_on, MIN_BY(plan, updated_at) AS plan_at_start
  FROM subscriptions
  GROUP BY subscription_id, user_id
),
bought AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT ON (a.user_id) a.user_id, f.plan_at_start
  FROM experiment_assignments a
  JOIN events p ON p.user_id = a.user_id AND p.event_name = 'purchase'
   AND p.event_ts >= a.assigned_at AND p.event_ts < a.assigned_at + INTERVAL 14 DAY
  JOIN first_plan f ON f.user_id = a.user_id AND f.started_on = CAST(p.event_ts AS DATE)
  WHERE a.experiment = 'paywall_v2'
  ORDER BY a.user_id, p.event_ts
)
SELECT a.variant, COUNT(*) AS n,
       COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE b.plan_at_start = 'plus_monthly') AS monthly,
       COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE b.plan_at_start = 'plus_annual') AS annual
FROM experiment_assignments a
LEFT JOIN bought b USING (user_id)
WHERE a.experiment = 'paywall_v2'
GROUP BY a.variant
ORDER BY a.variant;
```

**Try it:** the gap in buyers per 1,000 signups, with its 95 percent interval (the gap plus or minus 1.96 standard errors).

<RunSQL query={`WITH g(arm, n, buyers) AS (
  VALUES ('control', 2706, 343), ('variant', 2713, 415)
),
r AS (
  SELECT c.buyers * 1.0 / c.n AS p0, t.buyers * 1.0 / t.n AS p1, c.n AS n0, t.n AS n1
  FROM g c JOIN g t ON c.arm = 'control' AND t.arm = 'variant'
)
SELECT ROUND(1000 * (p1 - p0)) AS extra_buyers_per_1000,
       ROUND(1000 * (p1 - p0 - 1.96 * SQRT(p0 * (1 - p0) / n0 + p1 * (1 - p1) / n1))) AS low,
       ROUND(1000 * (p1 - p0 + 1.96 * SQRT(p0 * (1 - p0) / n0 + p1 * (1 - p1) / n1))) AS high
FROM r;`} />

Expected: 1 row, 3 columns.

**Try it.** Now the money. Each person's revenue is 0, 7.99 or 59.99, so the mean and the variance follow from the three counts.

<RunSQL query={`WITH arms(arm, n, monthly, annual) AS (
  VALUES ('control', 2706, 262, 81), ('variant', 2713, 312, 103)
),
m AS (
  SELECT arm, n,
         (7.99 * monthly + 59.99 * annual) / n AS mean,
         (7.99 * 7.99 * monthly + 59.99 * 59.99 * annual) / n AS mean_sq
  FROM arms
),
v AS (
  SELECT arm, n, mean, (mean_sq - mean * mean) * n / (n - 1) AS var FROM m
)
SELECT ROUND(c.mean, 2) AS control, ROUND(t.mean, 2) AS variant,
       ROUND(t.mean - c.mean, 2) AS gap,
       ROUND(t.mean - c.mean - 1.96 * SQRT(c.var / c.n + t.var / t.n), 2) AS low,
       ROUND(t.mean - c.mean + 1.96 * SQRT(c.var / c.n + t.var / t.n), 2) AS high
FROM v c JOIN v t ON c.arm = 'control' AND t.arm = 'variant';`} />

**Predict:** the buyer gap has an interval clear of zero. Will the interval of the dollar gap per signup sit as clearly above zero?

Expected: 1 row, 5 columns.

*Why:* 26 more buyers per 1,000 signups (8 to 45), but \$0.63 more revenue per signup (\$0.04 to \$1.21): its low end is 4 cents, so the money gain barely clears zero. Per 1,000 signups the dollar gap is \$627, from \$40 to \$1,210. Nothing is wrong with the data: a lumpy value (two prices, one rare) spreads the estimate.

### 3. The rule, then the memo

**Idea.** The rule of C14 was written before launch: ship if the lift is at least 2 points, its interval stays above 0 and the cancel guardrail's interval stays below +5 points; drop if even the top of the lift's interval is below 2 points or the bottom of the guardrail's interval is above +5; otherwise keep running. The readout puts the evidence beside it, and the memo (C15's five parts) ends in a decision. The guardrail numbers are C14's: of the buyers, 48 of 343 in control and 75 of 415 in the variant canceled within 30 days, a gap of +4.1 points with an interval of -1.1 to +9.3.

> **Decision:** keep `paywall_v2` running; do not ship it yet.
> **Context:** you asked whether to ship the redesigned paywall that ran from January 12 to March 8 for 5,419 new signups.
> **Tension:** more people bought, but the money gain barely clears zero and the guardrail cannot rule out a rise in cancellations that the plan says is too high.
> **Evidence:** the split was fair (p = 0.92) and the groups matched within 1.5 points. Per 1,000 signups, 26 more people bought Plus within 14 days (8 to 45) and first-purchase revenue rose \$627 (\$40 to \$1,210). Among buyers, 4.1 points more canceled within 30 days (-1.1 to +9.3); the limit is +5.
> **Recommendation:** extend the test until the top of the guardrail interval is below +5 points; then ship if the lift still clears 2 points, and drop if the bottom of the guardrail interval passes +5.

<Aside type="tip" title="Remember">

- Run the pre-flight first: split, balance and window. A failed check voids the test.
- Give the effect in the units of the decision, with its interval.
- A noisy metric (dollars from two prices) has a wider interval than the buyer count.
- Read against the rule written before launch, never against the p-value alone.
- End with a memo: a decision, the evidence and what would change it.

**Common trap:** reading a win off the dashboard before counting the groups.

</Aside>

</details>

</details>

```sql
WITH r AS (
  SELECT 304.0 / 2449 AS p0, 306.0 / 2519 AS p1, 2449 AS n0, 2519 AS n1

SELECT ROUND(1000 * (p1 - p0)) AS extra_buyers_per_1000,
       ROUND(1000 * (p1 - p0 - 1.96 * SQRT(p0 * (1 - p0) / n0 + p1 * (1 - p1) / n1))) AS low,

FROM r;
```

The answer is -3 per 1,000, from -21 to +16. A lift of 2 points is 20 more buyers per 1,000, above the top of the interval, so this test rules it out: with a bar of 2 points the rule says drop. The data do not show that the tips do nothing: they allow anything from 21 fewer to 16 more buyers per 1,000 signups.

</Solution>

<Exercise id="data-analyst-ab-test-readout-3">

**Spot the flaw.** A colleague writes: "Everyone who bought saw the paywall, so I looked only at people who saw it in the first 14 days. In `paywall_v2`, 343 of 1,212 control viewers bought and 415 of 1,177 variant viewers. That is a 7-point lift, so shipping adds about 70 buyers per 1,000 signups."

<RunSQL query={`WITH v(arm, viewers, buyers) AS (
  VALUES ('control', 1212, 343), ('variant', 1177, 415)
)
SELECT ROUND(100.0 * MAX(buyers / viewers) FILTER (WHERE arm = 'variant')
           - 100.0 * MAX(buyers / viewers) FILTER (WHERE arm = 'control'), 1) AS lift_points
FROM v;`} />

Expected: 1 row, 1 column. Run it, then show with the same counts and the group sizes (2,706 and 2,713 signups) what the shipped change would add per 1,000 signups, and say what is wrong with the method.

<Solution>

The query gives 7.0 points among viewers. But a signup counts for the business whether or not they ever see the paywall, and only 44.8 percent of control and 43.4 percent of the variant did:

```sql
WITH v(arm, signups, buyers) AS (
  VALUES ('control', 2706, 343), ('variant', 2713, 415)
)
SELECT arm, ROUND(1000.0 * buyers / signups, 1) AS buyers_per_1000
FROM v;
```

It gives 126.8 and 153.0 per 1,000 signups: 26, not 70. A rate among viewers has the wrong denominator for a forecast per signup, and the groups of viewers are chosen after the coin, by what each person did. Forecast from everyone the coin was tossed for, and keep the viewer rate as a note on where the effect happens.
