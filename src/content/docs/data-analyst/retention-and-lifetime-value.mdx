### 1. Retention by age, and who signed up

**Idea.** *Retention by age* asks, for each person, whether they were active in the 30 days that start `age` months after signup. Age is measured from each person's own signup, so cohorts line up. Count a person in a window only when the whole window ended before the data did; otherwise a young cohort looks worse at every age. *Active* is a choice: here a `session_start` or an `add_expense`.

```sql
-- read-only: runs on app-events (the editors of this page hold only cafe-sales)
WITH activity AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT user_id, CAST(event_ts AS DATE) AS day
  FROM events
  WHERE event_name IN ('session_start', 'add_expense')
),
cells AS (
  SELECT u.channel, k.age,
         EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM activity a
                 WHERE a.user_id = u.user_id
                   AND a.day BETWEEN u.signup_date + 30 * k.age AND u.signup_date + 30 * k.age + 29) AS active
  FROM users u
  CROSS JOIN (SELECT UNNEST([0, 1, 2, 3]) AS age) k
  WHERE u.signup_date + 30 * (k.age + 1) <= DATE '2026-07-01'
)
SELECT age, COUNT(*) AS people, ROUND(100.0 * AVG(active::INT), 1) AS pct_active
FROM cells
GROUP BY age
ORDER BY age;
```

**Predict:** in days 0 to 29, 82.2 percent of people are active. In days 30 to 59, will the share fall by less than a quarter, by about half, or by more than three quarters?

### 2. Churn: logo or revenue

**Idea.** *Logo churn* is the share of customers who left; *revenue churn* is the share of the recurring revenue that left. They differ when customers pay different amounts, so choose by the decision: a discount or a price change needs revenue churn, support staffing and onboarding need logo churn. Measure only the subscriptions that have had the whole window, here those started by March 31, so 90 days have passed. Take the plan at the start, not the plan of the latest row, because some monthly subscribers switch to annual.

```sql
-- read-only: runs on app-events
WITH latest AS (
  SELECT * FROM (SELECT *, ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY subscription_id ORDER BY updated_at DESC) AS rn
                 FROM subscriptions) WHERE rn = 1
),
starts AS (
  SELECT subscription_id, MIN_BY(plan, updated_at) AS plan_at_start FROM subscriptions GROUP BY subscription_id
)
SELECT s.plan_at_start AS plan, COUNT(*) AS started,
       COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE l.ended_on IS NOT NULL AND l.ended_on <= l.started_on + 90) AS canceled_in_90_days
FROM latest l
JOIN starts s ON s.subscription_id = l.subscription_id
WHERE l.started_on <= DATE '2026-03-31'
GROUP BY s.plan_at_start
ORDER BY plan;
```

It returns `plus_annual` 290 started, 17 canceled, and `plus_monthly` 969 started, 373 canceled. Monthly revenue per customer is \$7.99 on the monthly plan and \$59.99 divided by 12 on the annual one.

**Try it.**

<RunSQL query={`WITH plans(plan, started, canceled, monthly_price) AS (
  VALUES ('plus_monthly', 969, 373, 7.99), ('plus_annual', 290, 17, 59.99 / 12)
)
SELECT ROUND(100.0 * SUM(canceled) / SUM(started), 1) AS logo_churn_pct,
       ROUND(100.0 * SUM(canceled * monthly_price) / SUM(started * monthly_price), 1) AS revenue_churn_pct
FROM plans;`} />

**Predict:** which is higher, logo churn or revenue churn?

Expected: 1 row, 2 columns.

*Why:* revenue churn, 33.3 against 31.0 percent: the monthly plan loses 38.5 percent of its subscriptions in 90 days and the annual plan 5.9, and a monthly subscription carries more revenue each month. A discount offer aimed at monthly subscribers would be judged on the 33.3.

### 3. Lifetime value, with its assumptions out loud

**Idea.** A simple *lifetime value* of a monthly subscriber is the monthly price divided by the monthly churn: a subscriber who leaves 1 time in 4 stays 4 months on average. It rests on two assumptions that you write down: churn is constant, and revenue is the price, not profit. Churn per renewal is the cancellations divided by the renewals faced, over renewals 1 to 5. Put it next to what it costs to win a buyer: the cost per signup divided by the share of signups who start a monthly plan within 14 days. The ad costs per signup are the fictional ones of the dataset README: \$4.50 paid search, \$2.00 paid social, \$3.00 referral reward, \$0 for organic search and the app store.

The extraction (a `GROUP BY channel` on the renewals each monthly subscription faced, and on the signups of January 1 to June 16) gives the counts pasted here.

**Try it.**

<RunSQL query={`CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW channel_value AS
WITH channels(channel, renewals_faced, canceled, signups, monthly_buyers, cost_per_signup) AS (
  VALUES ('app_store', 766, 137, 4256, 343, 0.00), ('organic_search', 1409, 217, 5263, 600, 0.00),
         ('paid_search', 395, 76, 2653, 179, 4.50), ('paid_social', 180, 65, 4519, 131, 2.00),
         ('referral', 1410, 170, 3486, 619, 3.00)
)
SELECT channel, cost_per_signup,
       CAST(canceled AS DOUBLE) / renewals_faced AS churn,
       7.99 / (CAST(canceled AS DOUBLE) / renewals_faced) AS ltv,
       CAST(monthly_buyers AS DOUBLE) / signups AS conv,
       cost_per_signup / (CAST(monthly_buyers AS DOUBLE) / signups) AS cost_per_buyer
FROM channels;

SELECT channel, ROUND(100 * churn, 1) AS churn_pct, ROUND(ltv, 2) AS ltv,
       ROUND(cost_per_buyer, 2) AS cost_per_buyer, ROUND(ltv / NULLIF(cost_per_buyer, 0), 2) AS ltv_to_cost
FROM channel_value
ORDER BY ltv_to_cost DESC NULLS LAST;`} />

**Predict:** which channels earn back what they cost to win a buyer, a value above 1?

Expected: 5 rows, 5 columns.

*Why:* only referral: 3.92, against 0.62 for paid search and 0.32 for paid social. Organic search and the app store have no ad cost, so their ratio is undefined, not infinite. Paid social's churn rests on 180 renewals: 36.1 percent, with a 95 percent interval of 29 to 43, so its ratio lies between 0.27 and 0.40, far below 1 whichever end is true. The constant-churn assumption is rough: across all channels the cancel rate is 19.3 percent at the first renewal and 11.4 at the fourth.

### 4. End in a memo

> **Decision:** do not renew paid social at the assumed cost per signup, and test a larger referral reward in July.
> **Context:** Priya asked whether to renew the ad budget; the data covers January 1 to June 30.
> **Tension:** the ad costs are assumptions, lifetime value assumes constant churn and counts revenue, not profit, and the newest cohorts look worse mostly because of the channel mix.
> **Evidence:** a month after signup, 5.1 percent of paid-social people are active against 17.1 percent of everyone else. A monthly-plan buyer is worth \$22 from paid social and costs \$69 to win (ratio 0.32); referral is worth \$66 and costs \$17 (3.92); paid search is 0.62.
> **Recommendation:** pause paid social, put its budget into the referral test, and recompute with real ad costs; paid social would break even only at about 64 cents a signup.

<Aside type="tip" title="Remember">

- Retention by age counts a person only when the whole window ended before the data.
- A newer cohort can look worse because of who it holds; compare the groups inside it.
- Logo churn counts customers, revenue churn counts money; pick by the decision.
- Lifetime value = monthly price ÷ monthly churn, with constant churn and revenue as assumptions you state.
- A ratio with a zero cost is undefined; give a range when the sample is small.

**Common trap:** calling a newer cohort worse without looking at who signed up.

</Aside>

```sql
SELECT channel, ROUND(ltv, 2) AS ltv, cost_per_signup,
```

```sql
ORDER BY channel;
```

The break-even costs per signup are \$3.60 for the app store, \$5.91 for organic search, \$2.80 for paid search, \$0.64 for paid social and \$11.77 for referral. Paid search would have to cost \$2.80 instead of the assumed \$4.50 to pay back, and paid social 64 cents instead of \$2.00. Referral could pay a reward of up to \$11.77 a signup before it stopped paying back.


**Spot the flaw.** A colleague estimates a monthly subscription's lifetime from the subscriptions of January to March that have ended: "the average life is 2.1 months, so a subscriber is worth \$17." The table holds the 969 monthly subscriptions started from January 1 to March 31, with the months paid so far, in 30-day blocks.

<RunSQL query={`WITH months(ended, subscriptions, months_paid) AS (
  VALUES (true, 441, 942), (false, 528, 2572)
)
SELECT ROUND(7.99 * months_paid / subscriptions, 2) AS ltv_estimate
FROM months
WHERE ended;`} />

Expected: 1 row, 1 column. Run it, then show with the same table that the estimate cannot be right, and say what is wrong with the method.

<Solution>

The query gives 17.07. But all 969 subscriptions have already paid 3,514 months between them, 3.63 months each, which is \$28.98 each so far:

```sql
SELECT ROUND(7.99 * SUM(months_paid) / SUM(subscriptions), 2) AS paid_so_far
FROM months;
```

An estimate below what people have already paid is impossible. The method counts only the subscriptions that ended, which are the short ones: the 528 still active are the longest lives and are left out. Use every subscription with the renewals each one faced, as in unit 3.

</Solution>
