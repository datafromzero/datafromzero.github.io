## The question

You have built seven chapters of one project. A hiring manager will open one link and give it two minutes. This capstone turns the project into that link, and asks you to show, in a log, that you checked what an AI told you.

There is no single right case study. Several questions, tools and stories earn full marks, and a number you cannot trace earns none.

## Warm-up

Try each one from memory, then open the answer. Forgot? Follow the link back to that part of the lesson: looking back is part of the review.

1. In [*Designing a dashboard people will use*](/data-analyst/dashboard-design/#1-start-from-the-decision) (chapter 2), what are the five lines of a brief? Write the last one for a dashboard that shows Dana whether Campus's weekdays carry more orders than its weekends.

   <summary>answer</summary>

   Decision, reader, questions, data, done when. For example: "Done when Dana can read, on her phone in two minutes, whether weekdays or weekends carry more of Campus's orders."

   </details>

2. In [*Keeping a dashboard right*](/data-analyst/keeping-a-dashboard-right/#the-big-idea) (chapter 6), what are the three checks?

   <details>
   <summary>answer</summary>

   Freshness (for each cafe), row count and reconciliation to the source.

   </details>

## How this capstone works

**Time.** About 3 hours, 4 at most: 30 minutes to gather and repair, 60 for the case study, 30 for the AI log, 30 to test and score, 30 spare.

**Any tool.** Tableau Public is the default of the track; Power BI, a spreadsheet or a code-first report also qualify if the rubric is met.

**AI is allowed at every step.** Start from a context pack, run the five checks of [*Trust, but verify: checking AI output*](/core/trust-but-verify-ai-output/) (C38) on every output, and log a verdict for each.

**Public means public.** The Tableau Public FAQ says workbooks and data published there are not private and are accessible to anyone. Publish only cafe-sales, which is synthetic, and never real customer or employer data.

**Who marks it.** This site stores nothing and has no accounts: mark yourself with the rubric, or ask a mentor. For a mentor, an optional route is a repository with the files and a README, and a pull request to review, as in [*Git: branches, pull requests and review*](/core/git-branches-and-pull-requests/) (C30).

**When to stop.** If the same check fails twice, stop and decide yourself, as in [*What stays human*](/core/what-stays-human/) (C42).

## The brief

Dana's brief from [*Designing a dashboard people will use*](/data-analyst/dashboard-design/) stays: the decision is where July's promotion money goes and whether Harbor needs more morning staff; the reader is Dana, on her phone, with two minutes; the data are cafe-sales, January 1 to June 30, 2026. You may choose another question about cafe-sales if you write it in the same five lines: decision, reader, questions, data, done when.

Treat these as facts. Revenue means net sales as your KPI entry defines it, and the case study says how you treated order 2075.

**The two-minute scan.** Give the page to someone who has not seen the project, or put it away for a day and reread it. Start a timer. After two minutes they must say four things from the page alone: the problem, the answer, one limit and the next step. Count how many they get.

**The AI log** has one row for every AI output that fed a result, in the format of the project step of [*RAG and agents: what they are and how to judge them*](/core/ai-literacy-rag-and-agents/) (C43). Under it write your name, the date and what you did not check. If you used no AI, say so and log your three riskiest steps the same way. The context pack comes first, as in [*Prompting with the data in the room*](/core/prompting-with-the-data-in-the-room/) (C37): the schema, the grain, a few real sample rows and a data dictionary, in that order. ## Rubric

Eight items, each scored 2 (full), 1 (partial) or 0 (none): 16 in all. The rubric grades what you shipped and how well it holds up, not the question you chose: any question, tool or story that meets it can score 16.

**1. Cleaning log.**

- **Full:** at least 5 log lines, each naming a rule and the rows or dollars it touched; a reconciliation that ends with nothing unexplained; the raw file kept unchanged.
- **Partial:** 1 to 4 lines, or no reconciliation.
- **None:** no log.

**2. Dashboard.** Count four things: the link opens with no sign-in; a phone view exists, either a phone layout or the dashboard tested on a phone; the freshness line and the definitions are on the screen; at least three cards are listed with a number computed another way.

- **Full:** all four.
- **Partial:** two or three.
- **None:** one or none.

**3. KPI entries.**

- **Full:** one entry for each card, each with rule, grain, owner, at least one caveat and a version with a date; one agreement test that counts the cells compared and the cells that differ.
- **Partial:** entries with a part missing, or no test.
- **None:** no entries.

**4. Checks.** Count five things: freshness for each cafe, not only for the table; a row-count check; a total reconciled to the source; an owner, a time and an action for each card; a dated run of the checks.

- **Full:** all five.
- **Partial:** three or four.
- **None:** two or fewer.

**5. Case study page.** Count four things: the problem in one sentence; the answer within the next two; a chart or table and a number for every claim; at most 300 words.

- **Full:** all four, and a reader gets 4 of 4 on the two-minute scan.
- **Partial:** two or three of the four, or a reader gets 2 or 3 of 4.
- **None:** otherwise.

**6. Limits and beyond the brief.**

- **Full:** at least three limits, each tied to a number or to a rule of the data; one thing beyond the brief with its evidence; one next step.
- **Partial:** one or two limits tied to a number, or a beyond-the-brief note with no evidence.
- **None:** only generic limits, or none.

**7. Traceability.** A reader picks three numbers from the case study and traces each, in under a minute, to a dashboard card or a query.

- **Full:** 3 of 3, and the page says how order 2075 was treated.
- **Partial:** 2 of 3, or 3 of 3 with no word on order 2075.
- **None:** 1 or none.

**8. AI log and explain-back.**

- **Full:** a context pack with its four parts in order; a row for every AI output that fed a result, each with output, checks run (named by number or by name), verdict and reason; at least one revise or reject, or a sentence on why none; your name, the date and what you did not check; an explain-back that names one number and one check.
- **Partial:** a log with verdicts but no reasons or no named checks, or an explain-back that could describe any project.
- **None:** no log, or a list of prompts with no checks.

A total of 13 or more is strong. From 8 to 12 is solid with gaps: redo the items that scored 0 or 1. Below 8, rebuild the shipped pieces before you write about them.

> **Where should July's promotion money go, and does Harbor need a morning barista?** Cafe-sales, a synthetic three-cafe chain, January 1 to June 30, 2026. Dashboard: the link.
>
> **Problem.** Dana owns three cafes and one promotion budget for July. She needs to know where it helps and whether Harbor's mornings need staff.
>
> **Answer.** Harbor does not need the money to catch up: per open day it sells between what Campus and Old Town do. Its 7 a.m. hour is the thin spot, and the data cannot say why.
>
> **Claim 1.** In the same 91 days, April 1 to June 30, Harbor sells \$50.62 per open day; Campus sells \$49.79 and Old Town \$51.32.
>
> **Claim 2.** At 7 a.m. Harbor takes 0.22 orders per open day (20 orders); Campus 0.47 (43) and Old Town 0.49 (45).
>
> **Limits.** Harbor has 91 days of history, one season, so all three cafes are compared over those 91 days. Net sales are completed orders at list price minus discount; order 2075 was typed as 20 sandwiches and is counted as 2. The data end on June 30 at 16:30.
>
> **Beyond the brief.** All 168 discounted lines belong to gold customers (14 of the 15), \$84.00 in all, 3.9 percent of what their purchases cost at list price. Dana did not ask, and it is the only discount in the data.
>
> **Next step.** Ask for Harbor's morning staffing hours. If 7 a.m. is quiet because one person runs the till or the door opens late, staff is not the lever; if not, test an offer for four weeks.

### The graded AI log

The pack, in order. Schema: the columns of `sales_clean`. Grain: one row is one sold line of a completed order. Sample rows: five real rows from the table. Dictionary: net sales is quantity × unit price − discount; order 2075 counts as 2 units; the data end on June 30, 2026 at 16:30. The rows below are illustrations written for this lesson, not a transcript of a tool; yours come from your own run.

| AI output | Checks run | Verdict | Reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| Freshness line: "Data through June 30, 2026" | predicted the newest order, then ran `MAX(ordered_at)`: June 30, 16:30 (check 1) | revise | the day is not complete; the line now says "through June 30, 2026, 16:30" |
| README sentence: "1,800 orders" | counted the rows before and after the status filter: 1,800 and 1,799 (check 2) | revise | one order was canceled on March 1; completed orders are 1,799 |
| Query for orders at 7 a.m. by store | compared with the dashboard card, April 1 to June 30: 43, 20 and 45 (check 3) | accept | two routes give the same three numbers |
| Sentence: "Harbor sells as much per open day" | recomputed per open day over the same 91 days: \$50.62 against \$49.79 and \$51.32 (check 3) | accept | Harbor is 1.7 percent above Campus and 1.4 below Old Town |
| Recommendation: "Hire a morning barista at Harbor" | asked what the data say about staffing (check 5) | reject | 20 orders in 91 days show a thin hour, not its cause; the next step asks for staffing hours instead |

*Signed: your name, the date. Not checked: the phone layout on an Android phone; how the tool rounds a card (the number was read on screen).*

This log scores 2 on item 8: the pack is in order, five rows have all four columns, each check is named by number, two rows are revised and one rejected, and it is signed with what was not checked. A log of two rows, "SQL for the cards, looked fine, accept, it worked" and "summary text, read it, accept, good", scores 1 at best: verdicts, but no check named and no reason a reader could test. A list of prompts with no checks scores 0. Without the explain-back, a log that is otherwise full still has to show one number and one check in the explain-back to reach 2.
