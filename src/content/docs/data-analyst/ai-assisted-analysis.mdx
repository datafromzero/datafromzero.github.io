## The question

Dana has one more question: how is Harbor doing against Campus and Old Town, month by month, in net sales per day? You will run the analysis with an AI assistant, and every answer it gives will go into a log with your verdict. NACE's release of July 22, 2026 reports that employers in its spring survey (185 respondents) say more than one third of entry-level jobs require AI skills, and gives checking AI outputs among its examples of such skills.

## Learn by doing

About 60 minutes, 5 ideas: the pack, the plan, the queries, the story, the log. The replies below are illustrations written for this lesson, not transcripts; Exercise 2 is your own run. The SQL runs in the page. Run each block after the ones above it.

### 1. Set up the room

**Idea.** Start every analysis with C37's pack, and make its dictionary say what the question needs. Never paste personal data: the cafe-sales files are synthetic and safe to share, a real cafe's customer export is not. OpenAI's Free tier page lists "Analyze and extract insights from your data" among what Free users can do, with separate usage limits for data analysis and file uploads, and its file uploads page says "Free users are limited to 3 file uploads per day" and gives about 50 MB for a CSV file. Claude's create-and-edit page says code execution and file creation are available to Free users, with files up to 30MB for uploads and downloads there; its upload page lists 500MB a file for chat uploads and 30MB for project files. The cafe files are far below every limit. These limits change, so read the pages before you rely on them.

Attach `orders.csv`, `order_items.csv` and `products.csv` from the [dataset folder](https://github.com/datafromzero/datafromzero.github.io/tree/v0.1.0/datasets/cafe-sales/v1) (three uploads, which on ChatGPT Free is the whole day's allowance: do Exercise 2 there on another day, or in Claude); the three-row `stores` table is in the pack. Take the schema, grain and sample rows of C37 unchanged, then paste the dictionary and the question:

```text
DICTIONARY
- Net sales (the Revenue card): quantity x unit_price_usd - discount_usd over lines of
  completed orders. Not gross sales (every order at list price).
- Caveat: order_item_id 6952 on order 2075 was typed as 20 units; count it as 2.
- Net sales per day: a cafe's net sales in a month divided by the calendar days of that month.
- The data end on June 30, 2026 at 16:30.

QUESTION: For April, May and June 2026, what were net sales per day at Harbor,
Campus and Old Town, by month?
Plan first: the tables, the join keys and the grain after each step. Do not write code yet.
If the data cannot answer something, say "not in the data". Never invent a column,
a number or a source.
```

**Try it.** Check the pack before you send it: a stale line makes the assistant confidently wrong. The dictionary says all three cafes sold on every day from April 1:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT s.store_name AS store,
       COUNT(DISTINCT CAST(o.ordered_at AS DATE)) AS days_with_orders,
       MIN(o.ordered_at) AS first_order,
       COUNT(*) FILTER (WHERE o.status = 'canceled') AS canceled
FROM orders o
JOIN stores s ON s.store_id = o.store_id
WHERE o.ordered_at >= DATE '2026-04-01'
GROUP BY s.store_name
ORDER BY s.store_name;`} />

**Predict:** is the line true for all three cafes?

Expected: 3 rows, 4 columns.

*Why:* each cafe has orders on 91 of 91 days, and Harbor's first order is on April 1, so the line holds; none of the three canceled an order since then. Now send the prompt.

### 2. The plan

**Idea.** Judge the plan before any code exists, by keys, cardinality and grain (C37).

<Aside type="note" title="A reply written for this lesson, in the style of an assistant">

**Plan.** 1. Keep completed orders from April 1 to June 30. 2. Join `order_items` to `orders` on `order_id`, then to `products` on `product_id` and to `stores` on `store_id`; one row stays one order line. 3. Net sales of a line is `quantity * unit_price_usd - discount_usd`, with line 6952 counted as 2 units. 4. Group by store and month and divide by the calendar days of the month.

</Aside>

**Try it.** The plan says the grain stays one order line. Count the rows at each step:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM orders WHERE status = 'completed' AND ordered_at >= DATE '2026-04-01') AS orders,
       (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM orders o JOIN order_items oi ON oi.order_id = o.order_id
        WHERE o.status = 'completed' AND o.ordered_at >= DATE '2026-04-01') AS lines,
       (SELECT COUNT(*) FROM orders o JOIN order_items oi ON oi.order_id = o.order_id
        JOIN products p ON p.product_id = oi.product_id JOIN stores s ON s.store_id = o.store_id
        WHERE o.status = 'completed' AND o.ordered_at >= DATE '2026-04-01') AS lines_after_all_joins;`} />

**Predict:** after joining products and stores, are there more, fewer or the same number of rows as lines?

Expected: 1 row, 3 columns.

*Why:* 1,082 orders, 1,979 lines, and 1,979 after the joins: the keys of `products` and `stores` are unique, so nothing multiplies. The plan matches the pack. **Accept**, with the counts as the check.

### 3. The queries

**Idea.** Code is a claim: run it before you read its numbers. Two replies follow, both illustrations.

<Aside type="note" title="A reply written for this lesson, in the style of an assistant">

**Query, version 1.** For "the last three months" I filtered `o.ordered_at >= CURRENT_DATE - INTERVAL 91 DAY` and divided each month's net sales by the days of that month.

</Aside>

**Try it.** First write your prediction: nine rows, three cafes by three months. Then run it:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT s.store_name AS store, strftime(o.ordered_at, '%Y-%m') AS month,
       ROUND(SUM(oi.quantity * p.unit_price_usd - oi.discount_usd) / MAX(day(last_day(o.ordered_at))), 2) AS net_sales_per_day
FROM orders o
JOIN order_items oi ON oi.order_id = o.order_id
JOIN products p ON p.product_id = oi.product_id
JOIN stores s ON s.store_id = o.store_id
WHERE o.status = 'completed' AND o.ordered_at >= CURRENT_DATE - INTERVAL 91 DAY
GROUP BY s.store_name, month
ORDER BY s.store_name, month;`} />

**Predict:** how many rows come back?

Expected: 3 columns.

*Why:* none, on any day after September 29, 2026: the data end on June 30, and the query looked back from today's clock, not from the data. Check 1 (predict) caught it: nine rows expected, none seen. **Revise**: use the dates of the question. The assistant's next reply fixes the dates:

<Aside type="note" title="A reply written for this lesson, in the style of an assistant">

**Query, version 2.** The window is now `ordered_at >= DATE '2026-04-01' AND ordered_at < DATE '2026-07-01'`. Net sales of a line is `quantity * unit_price_usd - discount_usd`.

</Aside>

**Try it.** Reconcile it with the dictionary's route (check 3): the cafes' totals as that query adds them, and by the entry that counts line 6952 as 2 units.

<RunSQL query={`WITH l AS (
  SELECT s.store_name AS store, oi.quantity AS typed_quantity,
         CASE WHEN oi.order_item_id = 6952 THEN 2 ELSE oi.quantity END AS quantity,
         p.unit_price_usd AS price, oi.discount_usd AS discount
  FROM orders o
  JOIN order_items oi ON oi.order_id = o.order_id
  JOIN products p ON p.product_id = oi.product_id
  JOIN stores s ON s.store_id = o.store_id
  WHERE o.status = 'completed' AND o.ordered_at >= DATE '2026-04-01' AND o.ordered_at < DATE '2026-07-01'
)
SELECT store,
       ROUND(SUM(typed_quantity * price - discount), 2) AS as_the_assistant_added,
       ROUND(SUM(quantity * price - discount), 2) AS by_the_entry,
       ROUND(SUM(typed_quantity * price - discount) - SUM(quantity * price - discount), 2) AS difference
FROM l
GROUP BY store
ORDER BY store;`} />

**Predict:** which cafe's total differs, and by how much?

Expected: 3 rows, 4 columns.

*Why:* Old Town, by \$166.50: the 18 extra sandwiches of order 2075, which the dictionary said to count as 2 units. Campus and Harbor reconcile. **Revise**: apply the caveat. Order 2075 touches this total, so it is named in the reason.

### 4. The story and the forecast

**Idea.** A narrative is another claim: check each sentence against the table it sits on, and refuse what the data cannot support.

<Aside type="note" title="A reply written for this lesson, in the style of an assistant">

**Summary.** Harbor has grown every month since it opened and is now the top cafe per day. At this pace its net sales will reach about \$55 a day in July.

</Aside>

**Try it.** The table of the corrected query, the one the summary sits on:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT s.store_name AS store, strftime(o.ordered_at, '%Y-%m') AS month,
       ROUND(SUM((CASE WHEN oi.order_item_id = 6952 THEN 2 ELSE oi.quantity END) * p.unit_price_usd - oi.discount_usd)
             / MAX(day(last_day(o.ordered_at))), 2) AS net_sales_per_day
FROM orders o
JOIN order_items oi ON oi.order_id = o.order_id
JOIN products p ON p.product_id = oi.product_id
JOIN stores s ON s.store_id = o.store_id
WHERE o.status = 'completed' AND o.ordered_at >= DATE '2026-04-01' AND o.ordered_at < DATE '2026-07-01'
GROUP BY s.store_name, month
ORDER BY s.store_name, month;`} />

**Predict:** in which direction does Harbor move from April to June, and where does it stand among the cafes in June?

Expected: 9 rows, 3 columns.

*Why:* Harbor falls each month, \$53.22, \$49.89, \$48.78, and in June it is second, behind Campus's \$53.03 and ahead of Old Town's \$47.53: both claims are wrong. **Revise** the summary. The forecast rests on three points and no stated method, and the data end on June 30, so nothing can check it: **reject**.

### 5. The log, and what stays yours

**Idea.** The log is the deliverable. C43's format is one row for each AI output: the checks you ran, accept, revise or reject, and one sentence of reason; under the table your name, the date, and what you did not check.

| AI output | checks run | verdict | reason |
|---|---|---|---|
| plan | against the pack; rows 1,082, 1,979, 1,979 (check 2) | accept | keys and grain match the pack |
| query, version 1 | predicted 9 rows, got none (check 1) | revise | it looked back from today; the data end June 30 |
| query, version 2 | cafe totals against the entry's route (check 3) | revise | order 2075 counted as 20 units, not 2 |
| summary | each sentence against the table | revise | Harbor falls each month and is second in June |
| July forecast | asked for the method and the data | reject | three points; the data end June 30 |

*Signed: your name, the date. Not checked: whether the cafes' days match the tills' clock.* Four things stay yours: what the question means, the definitions in the entry, the claim you sign, and what you paste into a tool.

<Aside type="tip" title="Remember">

- Build the pack, check its lines against the data, and never paste personal data.
- Ask for the plan first and judge it by keys, cardinality and grain.
- Run every query; predict rows, count them, reconcile a total by another route.
- Check each sentence of a summary against its table; reject what cannot be checked.
- Log every output: checks, verdict, reason, your name, what you did not check.

**Common trap:** a log of accepts, with no check named.

</Aside>

<Solution>

For (a), count the orders of each store and hour, and keep the hour with the most orders in each store:

```sql
SELECT s.store_name AS store, hour(o.ordered_at) AS hr, COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders o
JOIN stores s ON s.store_id = o.store_id
WHERE o.status = 'completed' AND o.ordered_at >= DATE '2026-04-01' AND o.ordered_at < DATE '2026-07-01'
GROUP BY s.store_name, hr
QUALIFY rank() OVER (PARTITION BY s.store_name ORDER BY COUNT(*) DESC) = 1
ORDER BY s.store_name;
```

For (b), add the units of each store, with line 6952 as 2:

```sql
SELECT s.store_name AS store,
       SUM(CASE WHEN oi.order_item_id = 6952 THEN 2 ELSE oi.quantity END) AS units
FROM orders o
JOIN order_items oi ON oi.order_id = o.order_id
JOIN stores s ON s.store_id = o.store_id
WHERE o.status = 'completed' AND o.ordered_at >= DATE '2026-04-01' AND o.ordered_at < DATE '2026-07-01'
GROUP BY s.store_name
ORDER BY s.store_name;
```

For (a), the three rows are Campus 7 a.m. with 43, Harbor 8 a.m. with 43 and Old Town 7 a.m. with 45. For (b): Campus 1,028 units, Harbor 1,041 and Old Town 1,061. If an assistant reports 1,079 for Old Town, it counted the sandwich line of order 2075 as typed, 20 units, and ignored the dictionary's 2. Your log differs from any other: what matters is a named check in each row and one row that is not an accept.

</Solution>
