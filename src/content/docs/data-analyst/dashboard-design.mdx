## The question

This is chapter 2 of the dashboard project. Dana sends the brief:

> **Decision:** where next month's promotion money goes, and whether Harbor needs more morning staff.
> **Reader:** me, on my phone on Monday morning, with two minutes.
> **Questions:** how much did we sell; which store and which category carry it; when is each store busy.
> **Data:** the table from chapter 1, January 1 to June 30, 2026.
> **Done when:** one screen answers the three questions and says how fresh it is and how each number is defined.

## The big idea

A dashboard answers a written brief: a few numbers chosen by the decision, the answer placed first, a finding above each chart, and a line on how fresh and how defined.

## Learn by doing

About 25 minutes, 4 ideas: KPIs from the decision, one screen, freshness and definitions, the phone. The editors hold `cafe-sales`; unit 1 rebuilds the chapter 1 table, and the later units use it.

### 1. Start from the decision

**Idea.** A *KPI* is a number the reader would act on, so each one answers "what would Dana do differently if it moved?" Seven candidates:

| candidate | would change a decision? | on the screen |
|---|---|---|
| revenue | yes: it sizes the budget | card |
| orders | yes: traffic, apart from price | card |
| revenue per order | yes: spend per visit | card |
| units sold | not for this brief | no |
| share paid by card | no: nothing here depends on it | no |
| new customers | not in this table | no |
| canceled orders | 1 of 1,800: too few to act on | no |

**Try it:** the three cards, from the table of chapter 1 (order 2075 corrected to 2 units).

<RunSQL query={`CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW sales_clean AS
SELECT oi.order_item_id AS line_id, o.order_id, o.ordered_at, CAST(o.ordered_at AS DATE) AS order_date,
       s.store_name AS store, p.product_name AS product, p.category,
       CASE WHEN oi.order_item_id = 6952 THEN 2 ELSE oi.quantity END AS quantity,
       p.unit_price_usd AS unit_price, oi.discount_usd AS discount,
       (CASE WHEN oi.order_item_id = 6952 THEN 2 ELSE oi.quantity END) * p.unit_price_usd - oi.discount_usd AS amount,
       o.payment_method, COALESCE(c.loyalty_tier, 'walk-in') AS loyalty_tier
FROM orders o
JOIN order_items oi ON oi.order_id = o.order_id
JOIN products p ON p.product_id = oi.product_id
JOIN stores s ON s.store_id = o.store_id
LEFT JOIN customers c ON c.customer_id = o.customer_id
WHERE o.status = 'completed';

SELECT SUM(amount) AS revenue,
       COUNT(DISTINCT order_id) AS orders,
       ROUND(SUM(amount) / COUNT(DISTINCT order_id), 2) AS revenue_per_order
FROM sales_clean;`} />

**Predict:** if order 2075 stayed as recorded (20 sandwiches), which of the three cards would change?

Expected: 1 row, 3 columns.

*Why:* the cards read \$22,582.75, 1,799 orders and \$12.55. As recorded, revenue is \$22,749.25 and revenue per order \$12.65; orders stay 1,799, because the line changes a quantity and not the number of orders. A card that counts something must say which version it shows.

### 2. One screen, the answer first

**Idea.** A reader scans from the top left. Put the cards in one row on top, then one chart for each question, each with a takeaway headline (C15) above it. Add one filter at most. Nothing goes on the screen that no question asks for.

**Try it:** the data behind the third chart, orders by hour for the morning hours of each store, with each count divided by the store's open days (days with at least one order).

<RunSQL query={`WITH d AS (
  SELECT store, COUNT(DISTINCT order_date) AS open_days
  FROM sales_clean
  GROUP BY store
)
SELECT s.store, EXTRACT(hour FROM s.ordered_at) AS hour, COUNT(DISTINCT s.order_id) AS orders,
       ROUND(COUNT(DISTINCT s.order_id) * 1.0 / d.open_days, 2) AS orders_per_open_day
FROM sales_clean s
JOIN d ON d.store = s.store
WHERE EXTRACT(hour FROM s.ordered_at) IN (7, 8, 9)
GROUP BY s.store, EXTRACT(hour FROM s.ordered_at), d.open_days
ORDER BY s.store, hour;`} />

**Predict:** Dana asked whether Harbor needs more morning staff. Per open day, is Harbor busier or quieter than the other stores at 7 a.m., and at 8 a.m.?

Expected: 9 rows, 4 columns.

*Why:* at 7 Harbor is quieter, 0.22 orders a day (20 in 91 open days) against 0.40 at Old Town (73 in 181) and 0.44 at Campus (79 in 181); the raw counts compare 91 days with 181, so divide by open days. At 8 it flips: 0.47 against 0.29 and 0.33. The staffing answer is the 8 a.m. hour, and the headline for Harbor's own shape can say it: "Harbor's 7 a.m. hour is its quietest: 20 orders".

### 3. Say how fresh and how defined

**Idea.** A *freshness line* tells the reader how recent the data is, so nobody trusts old numbers. A *definitions note* gives one line per card: the rows it counts and any choice. Both are computed, not typed, so they cannot go stale.

**Try it:** the freshness line, and the rows behind the revenue card.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT 'Data through ' || STRFTIME(MAX(ordered_at), '%B %-d, %Y') AS freshness,
       COUNT(DISTINCT order_id) AS completed_orders,
       STRFTIME(MAX(ordered_at), '%H:%M') AS last_order
FROM sales_clean;`} />

Expected: 1 row, 3 columns.

*Why:* "Data through June 30, 2026", 1,799 completed orders, the last at 16:30. The note under the cards reads: "Revenue: completed orders, quantity times price minus discount. One order (2075) had 20 sandwiches where 2 were sold; shown as 2." The canceled order is left out and the note says so.

<Exercise id="data-analyst-dashboard-design-1">

**A fairer headline.** The first chart's headline says "Harbor sold about half of what each older cafe sold, in half the time". Comparing the same months is fairer. The editor gives revenue by store and month for April to June, the months Harbor was open. Turn it into one total per store for the three months. Run unit 1 first. Expected: 3 rows, 2 columns.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT store, EXTRACT(month FROM order_date) AS month, SUM(amount) AS revenue
FROM sales_clean
WHERE order_date >= DATE '2026-04-01'
GROUP BY store, EXTRACT(month FROM order_date)
ORDER BY store, month;`} />

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Drop the month from the `SELECT` and from the `GROUP BY`, and add the `ORDER BY`.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Half-solution</summary>

```sql
SELECT store, SUM(amount) AS revenue_apr_to_jun
FROM sales_clean
WHERE ...
GROUP BY ...
```

</details>

<Solution>

```sql
SELECT store, SUM(amount) AS revenue_apr_to_jun
FROM sales_clean
WHERE order_date >= DATE '2026-04-01'
GROUP BY store
ORDER BY store;
```

Campus \$4,531.00, Harbor \$4,606.25 and Old Town \$4,670.25. The fairer headline: in the same three months Harbor is within \$64 of Old Town. The numbers use order 2075 as 2 units; as recorded Old Town would read \$4,836.75.

**A brief for someone else.** Harbor's manager wants to know what to bake before opening. Before you run anything, write as comments the five lines of her brief (decision, reader, questions, data, done when) and the cards and charts you would put on her screen. Then check the data holds her first question: for bakery items sold at Harbor before 10 a.m., the units of each product. Run unit 1 first. Expected: 3 rows, 2 columns.

<RunSQL query={`-- Write your brief and your query here, then press Run.`} />

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

Filter on the store name, the category and the hour (`EXTRACT(hour FROM ordered_at) < 10`), then group by product.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Half-solution</summary>

Expected output in words: *"Three bakery products, each with a number of units."*

```sql
SELECT product, SUM(quantity) AS units
FROM sales_clean
WHERE store = 'Harbor' AND ...
```

</details>

<Solution>

```sql
SELECT product, SUM(quantity) AS units
FROM sales_clean
WHERE store = 'Harbor' AND category = 'bakery' AND EXTRACT(hour FROM ordered_at) < 10
GROUP BY product
ORDER BY units DESC, product;
```

Croissant sells 32 units before 10 a.m., and Blueberry Muffin and Banana Bread tie at 20. A brief for her: decision, how many of each item to bake; reader, the manager at 6:30 a.m.; questions, units by product before 10 a.m. and by weekday; data, `sales_clean`; done when, a card per item and one chart by weekday. The data holds the first question (three bakery products); weekday would need one more column.

</Solution>

</Exercise>

<Exercise id="data-analyst-dashboard-design-3">

**Spot the flaw.** A colleague builds the hourly chart for Old Town and titles it "Old Town is busiest at 1 p.m." Her query:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT EXTRACT(hour FROM ordered_at) AS hour, SUM(amount) AS revenue
FROM sales_clean
WHERE store = 'Old Town'
GROUP BY hour
ORDER BY revenue DESC
LIMIT 3;`} />

Run unit 1 first. Expected: 3 rows, 2 columns. Then show with a second query that the title is not true of every reading of "busiest", and say what to change.

<Solution>

```sql
SELECT EXTRACT(hour FROM ordered_at) AS hour, COUNT(DISTINCT order_id) AS orders
FROM sales_clean
WHERE store = 'Old Town'
GROUP BY hour
ORDER BY orders DESC, hour
LIMIT 3;
```

By revenue the top hour is 13 (\$990.00), then 10 and 15. By orders it is 10 (76 orders), then 7 and 16 with 73 each. "Busiest" is ambiguous, and Dana's staffing question is about orders. Name the measure in the title ("Old Town takes the most money at 1 p.m.") and in the definitions note.

</Solution>

</Exercise>
