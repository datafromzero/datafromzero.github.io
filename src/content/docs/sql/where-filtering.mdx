This lesson builds on *SELECT, ORDER BY and LIMIT* (S2). In S1 you used `WHERE` with one `=` test. Here you learn to say
exactly which rows you want: other comparisons, several tests at once, lists, ranges, text patterns and date ranges.
Press **Run** under any query on this page.

## The question

The owner of the Old Town cafe asks:

> How many orders did we take in March 2026 that were paid by mobile or cash? And for the poster: which lattes cost
> between \$4.00 and \$5.00?

In E5 you filtered a sheet with the filter arrow, one column at a time. In SQL you write all the tests in one `WHERE`.
Old Town is `store_id` 1. Four rows of the `orders` table, from the last day of March:

| order_id | customer_id | store_id | ordered_at | status | payment_method |
|---:|---:|---:|---|---|---|
| 1711 | 101 | 2 | 2026-03-31 10:01:00 | completed | mobile |
| 1712 | 40 | 1 | 2026-03-31 10:10:00 | completed | card |
| 1713 | 16 | 1 | 2026-03-31 11:04:00 | completed | mobile |
| 1714 | 116 | 2 | 2026-03-31 11:12:00 | completed | mobile |

## Warm-up

Answer from memory before you open anything. Then click to check.

1. In *SELECT, ORDER BY and LIMIT* (S2) you cut a sorted list. Write a query that shows `order_id` and `ordered_at` of
   the three newest orders, newest first, with `order_id` as the tie-breaker.

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   ```sql
   SELECT order_id, ordered_at
   FROM orders
   ORDER BY ordered_at DESC, order_id DESC
   LIMIT 3;
   ```

   </details>

2. In Excel or Google Sheets (*Decisions with IF, AND and OR*, E6), when is `AND(test1, test2)` true, and when is
   `OR(test1, test2)` true?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   `AND` is true only when every test is true. `OR` is true when at least one test is true. SQL uses the same two words
   in `WHERE`.

   </details>

## The big idea

## Learn by doing

Five small steps. Press Run under each query. Each one works on its own, so you can come back to any step.

### 1. One test per row

`WHERE` works out its test for each row from that row's own values. The answer is true or false, and the row stays only
if it is true: DuckDB's Boolean page says rows whose test is true "will pass the filter", and rows whose test is false
or `NULL` are filtered out. (`NULL` is SQL's marker for a missing value; the next lesson is about it.) Later clauses see
only the kept rows.

The tests use `=`, `<>` (not equal), `<`, `<=`, `>` and `>=`. A number has no quotes.

**Try it**:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT product_name, unit_price_usd
FROM products
WHERE unit_price_usd >= 4.50;`} />

Read it: *keep the products whose price is at least 4.50*. Expected: 8 rows, 2 columns. *Excel:* a filter on the price
column (E5); the same comparisons sit inside `IF` (E6), and `=` and `<>` are the values you tick or clear in a filter list.

*Why:* `>=` keeps 4.50 itself.

### 2. Combine tests: AND, OR and parentheses

`AND` keeps a row only when both tests are true. `OR` keeps it when at least one is. SQL works out `AND` before `OR`, so
`a OR b AND c` means `a OR (b AND c)`. That is standard SQL, and the counts below show it. Parentheses turn an `OR` into
one test.

**Try it.** `COUNT(*)` is the row count from S1.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders
WHERE store_id = 1 AND payment_method = 'mobile';`} />

Expected: 1 row, 1 column: `217`. *Excel:* two filters on two columns: Microsoft Support says "each additional filter
is based on the current filter", which is `AND`. The same word is `AND(a, b)` in E6.

Now add `OR`, written the natural way.

**Try it**:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders
WHERE store_id = 1 AND payment_method = 'mobile' OR payment_method = 'cash';`} />

Expected: 1 row, 1 column: `470`. That is too many: the query reads *(Old Town and mobile) or (cash)*.

Now put parentheses around the `OR`.

**Try it**:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders
WHERE store_id = 1 AND (payment_method = 'mobile' OR payment_method = 'cash');`} />

Expected: 1 row, 1 column: `308`. *Excel:* an `AND(...)` that contains an `OR(...)` (E6).

*Why:* the parentheses make the `OR` a single test, and `AND` then needs it and the store. Whenever one `WHERE` has both
`AND` and `OR`, write the parentheses, even where they change nothing. The first picture shows the two versions on six
orders.

### 3. Shortcuts: IN, BETWEEN and LIKE

`x IN (1, 2)` gives the same answer as `x = 1 OR x = 2`, with no parentheses to forget. `x BETWEEN a AND b` includes both
ends. `text LIKE 'a%'` matches a pattern: `%` stands for any run of characters, including none, and `_` for exactly one.
The pattern must cover the whole text (DuckDB's pattern matching page), and it is case-sensitive; `ILIKE` ignores case.

**Try it.** The same count as unit 2, shorter:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders
WHERE store_id = 1 AND payment_method IN ('mobile', 'cash');`} />

Expected: 1 row, 1 column: `308`, the same as before. *Excel:* tick several values in the filter list (E5).

**Try it.** Text and a range in one `WHERE`:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT product_name, unit_price_usd
FROM products
WHERE product_name LIKE '%Latte%' AND unit_price_usd BETWEEN 4.00 AND 5.00;`} />

Expected: 2 rows, 2 columns. *Excel:* two filters on two columns (E5).

*Why:* `'%Latte%'` means *Latte anywhere in the name*, and 4.00 and 5.00 both count. Pumpkin Spice Latte has the word but
not the price (5.50). A lowercase `'%latte%'` finds nothing. That answers the second question: **Latte and Chai Latte**.

### 4. Date ranges

Dates are written year-month-day, as in DuckDB's date documentation. A date written without a time stands for 00:00 at
the start of that day when it is compared with a timestamp. So "all of March" starts at `>= '2026-03-01'` and stops
before `< '2026-04-01'`: the start includes midnight of March 1, and the stop leaves out midnight of April 1.

**Try it**:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders
WHERE store_id = 1
  AND payment_method IN ('mobile', 'cash')
  AND ordered_at >= '2026-03-01'
  AND ordered_at < '2026-04-01';`} />

Expected: 1 row, 1 column: `52`. That answers the first question: **52 orders**, of any status (the one canceled order
of the data is a Campus order, not Old Town's).

**Try it.** The same month for all stores, first with the range, then with `BETWEEN`. Before you run
the second: will it agree with the first?

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders
WHERE ordered_at >= '2026-03-01' AND ordered_at < '2026-04-01';`} />

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders
WHERE ordered_at BETWEEN '2026-03-01' AND '2026-03-31';`} />

Expected: 1 row, 1 column each. *Excel:* **Date Filters > Between** (E5).

*Why:* the counts are `258` and `250`. `BETWEEN a AND b` means `a <=` the value `<= b`, and the end `'2026-03-31'` means
midnight at the start of March 31, so every order later that day is lost. The 8 missing orders are the eight on March 31
in the second picture. The half-open range cannot lose a day.

### 5. Split the group, and check

`NOT` flips a test, and parentheses make it flip the whole test: `NOT (payment_method IN ('mobile', 'cash'))` keeps the
orders paid some other way. *Excel:* E5 and E6 have no counterpart.

**Try it.** Old Town's March orders in two groups, then all of them:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders
WHERE store_id = 1
  AND NOT (payment_method IN ('mobile', 'cash'))
  AND ordered_at >= '2026-03-01'
  AND ordered_at < '2026-04-01';`} />

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders
WHERE store_id = 1
  AND ordered_at >= '2026-03-01'
  AND ordered_at < '2026-04-01';`} />

Expected: 1 row, 1 column each: `81` and `133`. The 52 of unit 4 and the 81 card orders make 133, all of Old Town's March
orders. A second method, and the groups reconcile.

<Aside type="tip" title="Remember">

- A `WHERE` keeps a row only when its test is true.
- `AND` is worked out before `OR`: put parentheses around every `OR` that sits with an `AND`.
- `BETWEEN` includes both ends, `IN` is a list of `OR`s, and in `LIKE`, `%` is any run and `_` is one character.
- A date alone means midnight: start with `>=` the first day and stop with `<` the next.

**Common trap: "`OR` works like English, so `payment_method = 'card' OR 'cash'` means card or cash."** Each side of
`AND` and `OR` is its own true-or-false test, and `'cash'` alone is only text. DuckDB cannot read it as true or false and
stops with an error. Write `payment_method = 'card' OR payment_method = 'cash'`, or use `IN`.

</Aside>

**Spot the flaw.** A colleague counts the orders of June 2026 and gets `344`. The query runs without an error:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders

The end `'2026-06-30'` means midnight at the start of June 30, so every order placed during June 30 is missed. The fix is the

```sql
SELECT COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders
WHERE ordered_at >= '2026-06-01' AND ordered_at < '2026-07-01';
```

Expected: 1 row, 1 column: `353`. The 9 orders that `BETWEEN` lost are the orders of June 30.
