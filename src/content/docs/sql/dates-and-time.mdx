## Learn by doing

Five small steps. Run the notebook's setup cell first, then the cells named in each step.

### 1. Take a timestamp apart

A **date** names a day. A **timestamp** names a day and a time of day, and `ordered_at` is one. A `TIMESTAMP` holds no
time zone: DuckDB calls it a timestamp without time zone, usually a local reading of the clock in an unspecified zone.
The column cannot say which zone; the dataset notes do. In cafe-sales, `ordered_at` is local time at the cafes in the San
Francisco Bay Area (Pacific time), so 08:02 is 8:02 on the cafe's clock. Data from another zone needs converting first.

**Try it** (notebook step 1): look at the type.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT order_id, ordered_at, typeof(ordered_at) AS type
FROM orders
ORDER BY order_id
LIMIT 3;`} />

Expected: 3 rows, 3 columns; `type` reads `TIMESTAMP`. *Why:* the type has no zone, so you name it yourself: local time
at the cafes.

A **part function** takes one piece out of a timestamp: `year`, `month`, `day`, `dayname` (the weekday name) and `isodow`
(the weekday number, Monday = 1 to Sunday = 7). A part keeps less than you might expect: `month(x)` returns 1 to 12 with
no year, so January 2026 and January 2027 give the same number.

**Try it** (notebook step 2):

<RunSQL query={`SELECT order_id, ordered_at, year(ordered_at) AS yr, month(ordered_at) AS mo, day(ordered_at) AS dom,
       dayname(ordered_at) AS weekday, isodow(ordered_at) AS dow
FROM orders
ORDER BY order_id
LIMIT 3;`} />

Expected: 3 rows, 7 columns; all three orders fall on Thursday, January 1, `dow` 4. *Excel:* `YEAR`, `MONTH` and `DAY`.
*Why:* each function returns one part, so you can group or filter by it.

### 2. Dates are numbers of days

Subtract one date from another and you get the days between them. `x + INTERVAL 7 DAY` moves a date or a timestamp by an
interval; subtract it to move back. *Excel:* in *Dates as numbers* (E11), a date is a serial number, so dates work in
calculations the same way.

**Try it** (notebook step 3):

<RunSQL query={`SELECT store_name, opened_on, DATE '2026-06-30' - opened_on AS days_open
FROM stores
ORDER BY store_id;`} />

Expected: 3 rows, 3 columns; Harbor's `days_open` is `90`: 90 days between opening and June 30 (91 open days counting
both ends). *Why:* a date behaves like a number of days, as in a sheet.

**Try it** (notebook step 4): the last seven days before July 1.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS last_week_orders
FROM orders
WHERE ordered_at >= DATE '2026-07-01' - INTERVAL 7 DAY
  AND ordered_at < DATE '2026-07-01';`} />

Expected: 1 row, 1 column: `82`. *Why:* July 1 minus 7 days is June 24, so the range covers June 24 to 30, seven whole
days.

### 3. Cut to the start of a period

`date_trunc('month', x)` cuts a timestamp back to the start of its month: it keeps the year and the month and sets
everything after them to its starting value, so `2026-01-01 08:02:00` becomes `2026-01-01 00:00:00`. Every order of one
month gets the same value, so grouping by it makes months. It returns a `TIMESTAMP`, even when you give it a `DATE`:
wrap it in `CAST(... AS DATE)` to show a day without the time. The same function also cuts to `'week'` and `'day'`.

**Try it** (notebook step 5): the start of the day, week and month.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT order_id, ordered_at,
       CAST(ordered_at AS DATE) AS day,
       CAST(date_trunc('week', ordered_at) AS DATE) AS week_start,
       CAST(date_trunc('month', ordered_at) AS DATE) AS month_start
FROM orders
ORDER BY order_id
LIMIT 3;`} />

Expected: 3 rows, 5 columns; `week_start` is Monday, December 29, 2025, before the data begins. *Excel:* none; you would
build it with `DATE(YEAR(A2), MONTH(A2), 1)`.

**Try it** (notebook step 6): group by month.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT CAST(date_trunc('month', ordered_at) AS DATE) AS month, COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders
GROUP BY CAST(date_trunc('month', ordered_at) AS DATE)
ORDER BY month;`} />

Expected: 6 rows, 2 columns, January to June. *Why:* every order in a month is cut to the same first day, so the groups
are months. `ORDER BY` runs last and puts them in time order. All 1,800 orders are counted, the canceled one included.

### 4. Keep the last day

To count a whole month, start on its first day and stop before the first day of the next month, as in *Filtering rows
with WHERE* (S3).

**Try it** (notebook step 7). Two ways to count June:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS june_between
FROM orders
WHERE ordered_at BETWEEN '2026-06-01' AND '2026-06-30';`} />

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS june
FROM orders
WHERE ordered_at >= '2026-06-01' AND ordered_at < '2026-07-01';`} />

Expected: 1 row, 1 column each. The first returns `344`, the second `353`, which is the June row of unit 3. *Why:* the
`BETWEEN` version is short by the 9 orders placed on June 30.

<details>
<summary>Explain it in your own words: why does <code>BETWEEN '2026-06-01' AND '2026-06-30'</code> return fewer orders than June has?</summary>

A date written alone means the start of that day, 00:00:00. An order at 10:15 on June 30 comes after it, so it is left
out.

</details>

### 5. Flag a partial month

A month is **partial** when its last order day comes before its last calendar day. `last_day(x)` gives the last day of the
month of `x`.

**Try it** (notebook step 8). The real data ends on June 30. To practice, pretend the file was exported on June 12, by
keeping only orders before June 13.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT CAST(date_trunc('month', ordered_at) AS DATE) AS month,
       COUNT(*) AS orders,
       MAX(CAST(ordered_at AS DATE)) AS last_order_day,
       last_day(MAX(ordered_at)) AS month_end,
       MAX(CAST(ordered_at AS DATE)) < last_day(MAX(ordered_at)) AS is_partial
FROM orders
WHERE ordered_at < TIMESTAMP '2026-06-13'
GROUP BY CAST(date_trunc('month', ordered_at) AS DATE)
ORDER BY month;`} />

Expected: 6 rows, 5 columns; only June has `is_partial` true: 130 orders up to June 12, against 353 in the full month.
*Excel:* `EOMONTH(date, 0)` gives the same month end. *Why:* a partial month looks like a drop but is only missing days.
Without the `WHERE`, every month is complete.

<Aside type="tip" title="Remember">

- A `TIMESTAMP` holds a day and a time but no time zone; the dataset notes name the zone.
- `year`, `month`, `dayname` and `isodow` return one part. `date_trunc` cuts to the start of a period and keeps the year.
- `date_trunc` returns a `TIMESTAMP`: wrap it in `CAST(... AS DATE)` to show a plain day.
- To count a whole period, start on its first day and stop before the next period's first day.
- A month is partial when its last order day is before its last calendar day.

**Common trap: "`date_trunc` rounds to the nearest month."** It cuts down, never up. The last order in the data, order
2800, was placed at 16:30 on 2026-06-30, on the last day of June, and `date_trunc('month', ordered_at)` still gives
`2026-06-01`, not July 1.

</Aside>

**Spot the flaw.** A colleague counts orders per week and reports that the quietest weeks of the half-year are the week
of June 29 and the week of December 29, "so sales fall at New Year and at the end of June". The query:

<RunSQL query={`SELECT CAST(date_trunc('week', ordered_at) AS DATE) AS week_start, COUNT(*) AS orders
FROM orders
GROUP BY CAST(date_trunc('week', ordered_at) AS DATE)
ORDER BY orders
LIMIT 3;`} />

Expected: 3 rows, 2 columns. Find the flaw and fix the query.

<Solution>

The query returns the week of June 29 with 21 orders, the week of December 29 with 26, then the week of February 2 with 47. The first two weeks are partial. The data starts on Thursday, January 1, so the week that starts on Monday, December 29 holds 4 days; it ends on Tuesday, June 30, so the week of June 29 holds 2 days. Count the days in each week and keep only whole weeks:

```sql
SELECT CAST(date_trunc('week', ordered_at) AS DATE) AS week_start,
       COUNT(*) AS orders,
       COUNT(DISTINCT CAST(ordered_at AS DATE)) AS days
FROM orders
GROUP BY CAST(date_trunc('week', ordered_at) AS DATE)
HAVING COUNT(DISTINCT CAST(ordered_at AS DATE)) = 7
ORDER BY orders
LIMIT 3;
```

Expected: 3 rows, 3 columns. The quietest whole week is the week of February 2, with 47 orders.

</Solution>
