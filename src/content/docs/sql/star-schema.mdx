## The question

The owner of the cafes asks:

> Our reporting tool wants one tidy model it can filter by store, product and day. Today every report re-joins the
> tables. What does that model look like, and how do I know its totals are right?

The middle of that model is one row per order line: keys that point to descriptive tables, and numbers to add up (some
columns left out):

| 5001 | 2026-01-01 | 2 | 10 | 1 | 3.25 |
| 5002 | 2026-01-01 | 2 | 6 | 1 | 4.75 |
| 5003 | 2026-01-01 | 2 | 1 | 2 | 6.00 |

## The big idea

A **star schema** keeps the events you count in one middle table and the things you slice them by in small tables around
it, tied together by keys.

## Learn by doing

Four small steps: one idea, then you try it. Totals include the extra-zero line (order 2075); "completed" leaves out the one canceled order.

### 1. The fact table: one row per order line

The middle table is the **fact table**. It records events and their numbers; here, each sale. Say what one row stands
for before you build it. That is the **grain**, and here it is *one order line*. A fact table keeps one grain
throughout, as Microsoft's Power BI documentation advises.

**Try it.** The fact table is a **view**, a saved query (S28). DuckDB does not keep a second copy of
the rows: the query runs each time something reads the view.

<RunSQL query={`CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW fact_sales AS
SELECT i.order_item_id, i.order_id,
       CAST(o.ordered_at AS DATE) AS order_date,
       o.store_id, o.customer_id, i.product_id,
       o.status AS order_status,
       i.quantity, i.discount_usd,
       i.quantity * p.unit_price_usd - i.discount_usd AS amount_usd
FROM order_items AS i
JOIN orders AS o ON o.order_id = i.order_id
JOIN products AS p ON p.product_id = i.product_id;`} />

Expected: no result rows, just a note that the statement ran. Run it twice if you like: `CREATE OR REPLACE` makes the
same view again. *Why:* the view keeps every line, the canceled order's included, so its counts match the source. Its
columns are ids such as `order_item_id`, keys that point to other tables (`store_id`, `product_id`, `order_date`), and
measures you add up (`quantity`, `amount_usd`).

**Try it:** check the grain.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS lines, COUNT(DISTINCT order_item_id) AS distinct_lines,
       ROUND(SUM(amount_usd), 2) AS sales
FROM fact_sales;`} />

Expected: 1 row, 3 columns: `3287 | 3287 | 22754.25`. *Why:* as many distinct line ids as rows means one row per line.
The 3,287 equals `order_items` (S18), and 22,754.25 is all lines of every status.

### 2. The dimensions: one row per key

Around the fact table sit the **dimension tables**. They describe what the events happened to: who, what, where and
when. Kimball Group splits data the same way, into measurements and descriptive context. A dimension has a key column
that names every row uniquely, plus descriptive columns to filter and group by. It stays small (3 to 181 rows here)
while the fact table can grow large (3,287 rows).

**Try it.** The date dimension is the date spine of *Complete time
series and recursive CTEs* (S27) plus the date parts of *Working with dates and time* (S11).

<RunSQL query={`CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW dim_store AS
SELECT store_id, store_name, city FROM stores;

CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW dim_product AS
SELECT product_id, product_name, category FROM products;

CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW dim_date AS
SELECT CAST(generate_series AS DATE) AS order_date,
       year(generate_series) AS year, month(generate_series) AS month,
       dayname(generate_series) AS weekday,
       isodow(generate_series) >= 6 AS is_weekend
FROM generate_series(TIMESTAMP '2026-01-01', TIMESTAMP '2026-06-30', INTERVAL 1 DAY);`} />

Expected: no result rows. *Why:* `isodow` is 6 or 7 on Saturday and Sunday. Power BI's documentation says: "The most
consistent table you'll find in a star schema is a date dimension table."

**Try it.** A dimension must have one row per key, so each pair of
numbers below must match.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT 'dim_date' AS dimension, COUNT(*) AS dim_rows, COUNT(DISTINCT order_date) AS distinct_keys FROM dim_date
UNION ALL
SELECT 'dim_product', COUNT(*), COUNT(DISTINCT product_id) FROM dim_product
UNION ALL
SELECT 'dim_store', COUNT(*), COUNT(DISTINCT store_id) FROM dim_store
ORDER BY dimension;`} />

Expected: 3 rows, 3 columns: 181 and 181, 16 and 16, 3 and 3. *Why:* this is the uniqueness check of *Join fan-out*
(S21): the two numbers are equal only when no key value repeats.

Here is the whole star. Each line joins a key marked 1 at a dimension to the matching key of the fact table, marked
many. The customer dimension waits for Exercise 2.

### 3. Ask a question through the star

A question put to a star runs in four moves. Start `FROM` the fact table. Join each dimension the question needs, on its
key. Filter and group by columns of the dimensions, such as a category or a weekday. Add up a measure of the fact table.
The same Power BI documentation gives the reason: dimension tables enable filtering and grouping, fact tables enable
summarization.

**Try it:** completed sales by category, weekdays against weekends. Before you run it: how many rows,
with 4 categories and 2 kinds of day?

<RunSQL query={`SELECT p.category, d.is_weekend, ROUND(SUM(f.amount_usd), 2) AS sales
FROM fact_sales AS f
JOIN dim_product AS p ON p.product_id = f.product_id
JOIN dim_date AS d ON d.order_date = f.order_date
WHERE f.order_status = 'completed'
GROUP BY p.category, d.is_weekend
ORDER BY p.category, d.is_weekend;`} />

Expected: 3 columns. *Excel:* a PivotTable (E15, *Summarize with a PivotTable*) reads a table the same way: the fields in
Rows and Columns are what you group by, and the numeric field in Values is the measure, which Excel sums by default.

*Why:* **8** rows, one per category and kind of day: bakery on weekdays 2341.50 and on weekends 1482.75, for example. The
question uses one column of each dimension and one measure: group by the dimensions, sum the fact.

### 4. Check the totals

Joining the fact table to every dimension must not add or drop a line. If it does, a dimension has a repeated or
missing key.

**Try it.** Before you run it: how many lines come back?

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS lines, ROUND(SUM(f.amount_usd), 2) AS sales
FROM fact_sales AS f
JOIN dim_store AS s ON s.store_id = f.store_id
JOIN dim_product AS p ON p.product_id = f.product_id
JOIN dim_date AS d ON d.order_date = f.order_date;`} />

Expected: 1 row, 2 columns. *Why:* `3287 | 22754.25`, the same as unit 1. Add up the eight numbers of unit 3 and they come to
22,749.25: the completed-sales total of *Saving a query as a view* (S28) and of the Excel pivot lessons. The answer to the
owner: **yes, the star gives the same totals as the source.**

<details>
<summary>Explain it in your own words: why must this join return exactly 3,287 lines?</summary>

Every line has one store, one product and one day, and each dimension has exactly one row per key, so each fact row finds
exactly one match and the join neither drops nor repeats a line.

</details>

<Aside type="tip" title="Remember">

- One fact table at a stated grain; one row per key in every dimension.
- Facts hold events, ids, keys and measures. Dimensions describe, and you filter and group by them.
- A question: fact first, join the dimensions it needs, group by their columns, sum a measure.
- After every join, compare the count and the total with the source.

**Common trap: "a fact table holds only numbers."** It holds dimension keys too, beside the measures. Without
`store_id`, `product_id` and `order_date`, a line's amount could not be tied to a cafe, a product or a day.

</Aside>

<Exercise id="sql-star-schema-3">

**Spot the flaw.** A colleague builds a product dimension of only the products that have sold, from the lines, joins
`fact_sales` to it, adds up `amount_usd` over all lines, and reports 5,441,687.00. Run both statements; they need
`fact_sales` from unit 1.

<RunSQL query={`CREATE OR REPLACE VIEW dim_product_sold AS
SELECT p.product_id, p.product_name, p.category
FROM products AS p
JOIN order_items AS i ON i.product_id = p.product_id;

SELECT COUNT(*) AS lines, ROUND(SUM(f.amount_usd), 2) AS sales
FROM fact_sales AS f
JOIN dim_product_sold AS p ON p.product_id = f.product_id;`} />

Expected from that query: 1 row, 2 columns (the lines and the sales). What went wrong, and what is the fix?

<Solution>

The join returns `817661 | 5441687.00`, not `3287 | 22754.25`. The view has 3,287 rows but only 15 different
`product_id` values: one row per *line*, not per product, because the join to `order_items` repeats each product once per
line. Espresso appears 192 times, so every Espresso fact row is matched 192 times. The key check of unit 2 would have
caught it: `COUNT(*)` and `COUNT(DISTINCT product_id)` differ.

Use `dim_product` as it is (Pumpkin Spice Latte, never sold, may stay on the menu), or write the sold-products view with
`SELECT DISTINCT`. Either way, check `COUNT(*) = COUNT(DISTINCT key)` after building a dimension.

</Solution>

</Exercise>
