## The question

The owner of the cafes asks:

> Of the customers who join the loyalty program, how many come back? Do we lose them before the first order, or after?

Three customers, with the date of their first three completed orders:

| customer | signed up | 1st order | 2nd order | 3rd order |
|---:|---|---|---|---|
| 86 | Jan 2 | Jan 12 | Apr 10 | Apr 18 |
| 87 | Jan 3 | Jan 18 | Jan 29 | Feb 1 |
| 92 | Jan 20 | Apr 22 | May 15 | Jun 8 |

All three order a lot in the end, but not all within a month of joining. By the end you will have a funnel table for the
whole group, with the conversion of each step and what it is a share of.

A funnel needs a definition before any SQL. Write it as one sentence: *by reaching step k I mean … , counted over … ,
excluding …*. Ours:

> A customer reaches step k when they have at least k completed orders within 30 days of signing up (day 0 to day 30).
> I count customers, identified by `customer_id`, not orders. I count the 49 customers who signed up from January 1 to
> May 31, 2026, and leave out three groups: the 85 who signed up before 2026 (the data starts on January 1, so their first
> days are missing), the 16 who signed up in June (the data ends on June 30, so their 30 days are not over), and order
> 1335, which has no customer to follow.

Each choice answers a question the numbers depend on. The **counting unit** is the customer. The **same user** is the same
`customer_id`. The **window** is 30 days, and it is only fair to customers whose whole window is in the data: a June
signup with no order yet has not dropped out, we just have not seen enough of their month.

Two ratios, with different denominators: **step conversion** divides a step by the step above, **overall conversion**
divides it by the signups.

## Worked example

Question: **of 49 new customers, how many place a first, a second and a third order within 30 days?** Run the notebook's
setup cell, then one cell per step. Each step gives the shape of the result to expect. Orders are completed orders only.

1. **Fix the group.**

   ```sql
   SELECT COUNT(*) AS signed_up, MIN(signup_date) AS first_signup, MAX(signup_date) AS last_signup
   FROM customers
   WHERE signup_date >= DATE '2026-01-01' AND signup_date <= DATE '2026-05-31';
   ```

   Expected: 1 row, 3 columns: `49`, `2026-01-02` and `2026-05-30`. *Why:* May 31 plus 30 days is June 30, the last day of
   the data, so every customer here has a full window.

2. **Count each customer's orders in the window.** Grain: one row per customer.

   ```sql
   WITH cohort AS (
     SELECT customer_id, signup_date
     FROM customers
     WHERE signup_date >= DATE '2026-01-01' AND signup_date <= DATE '2026-05-31'
   ),
   in_window AS (
     SELECT c.customer_id, COUNT(o.order_id) AS orders_30d
     FROM cohort c
     LEFT JOIN orders o
       ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
      AND o.status = 'completed'
      AND CAST(o.ordered_at AS DATE) - c.signup_date <= 30
     GROUP BY c.customer_id
   )
   SELECT * FROM in_window
   ORDER BY customer_id
   LIMIT 5;
   ```

   Expected: 5 rows, 2 columns; customer 86 has 1 order in the window, 87 has 3, and 89 has 0. *Why:* the
   `LEFT JOIN` keeps customers with no order in the window, and all three conditions sit in `ON`, so they decide what
   matches, not who stays (S20). `date - date` gives days (S11). `COUNT(o.order_id)` counts matched orders only.

3. **Check the grain.** Replace the last `SELECT` of step 2 with:

   ```sql
   SELECT COUNT(*) AS customers, SUM(orders_30d) AS orders
   FROM in_window;
   ```

   Expected: 1 row, 2 columns: `49` and `143`. *Why:* one row per customer, so 49; 143 is the number of orders the
   funnel will be built from. Keep it for the check in step 6.

4. **Count the customers at each step.**

   ```sql
   WITH cohort AS (
     SELECT customer_id, signup_date
     FROM customers
     WHERE signup_date >= DATE '2026-01-01' AND signup_date <= DATE '2026-05-31'
   ),
   in_window AS (
     SELECT c.customer_id, COUNT(o.order_id) AS orders_30d
     FROM cohort c
     LEFT JOIN orders o
       ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
      AND o.status = 'completed'
      AND CAST(o.ordered_at AS DATE) - c.signup_date <= 30
     GROUP BY c.customer_id
   )
   SELECT COUNT(*) AS signed_up,
          COUNT(CASE WHEN orders_30d >= 1 THEN 1 END) AS first_order,
          COUNT(CASE WHEN orders_30d >= 2 THEN 1 END) AS second_order,
          COUNT(CASE WHEN orders_30d >= 3 THEN 1 END) AS third_order
   FROM in_window;
   ```

   Expected: 1 row, 4 columns. *Why:* conditional aggregation (S9) over customers, so each customer counts once per step.
   Having a third order means having a first and a second before it, so the steps are in order by construction. A funnel
   of different events (viewed, then paid) would compare their timestamps instead.

5. **Add the conversion.** Add a third step, `steps`, and read it:

   ```sql
   WITH cohort AS (
     SELECT customer_id, signup_date
     FROM customers
     WHERE signup_date >= DATE '2026-01-01' AND signup_date <= DATE '2026-05-31'
   ),
   in_window AS (
     SELECT c.customer_id, COUNT(o.order_id) AS orders_30d
     FROM cohort c
     LEFT JOIN orders o
       ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
      AND o.status = 'completed'
      AND CAST(o.ordered_at AS DATE) - c.signup_date <= 30
     GROUP BY c.customer_id
   ),
   steps AS (
     SELECT COUNT(*) AS signed_up,
            COUNT(CASE WHEN orders_30d >= 1 THEN 1 END) AS first_order,
            COUNT(CASE WHEN orders_30d >= 2 THEN 1 END) AS second_order,
            COUNT(CASE WHEN orders_30d >= 3 THEN 1 END) AS third_order
     FROM in_window
   )
   SELECT *,
          ROUND(100.0 * first_order / signed_up, 1) AS conv_1,
          ROUND(100.0 * second_order / first_order, 1) AS conv_2,
          ROUND(100.0 * third_order / second_order, 1) AS conv_3,
          ROUND(100.0 * third_order / signed_up, 1) AS overall
   FROM steps;
   ```

   Expected: 1 row, 8 columns: `49 | 40 | 33 | 24 | 81.6 | 82.5 | 72.7 | 49.0`.
   *Why:* `conv_1` to `conv_3` divide by the step above; `overall` divides by the signups. They answer different
   questions, so say which one you report.

6. **Verify.** A second method, with no `LEFT JOIN`: count the orders in the window directly.

   ```sql
   SELECT COUNT(*) AS orders_in_window
   FROM customers c
   JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
   WHERE c.signup_date >= DATE '2026-01-01' AND c.signup_date <= DATE '2026-05-31'
     AND o.status = 'completed'
     AND CAST(o.ordered_at AS DATE) - c.signup_date <= 30;
   ```

   Expected: 1 row, 1 column: `143`, the same as step 3. Answer: **40 of 49 customers (81.6%) place a first order within 30
   days, and 24 (49.0%) a third.** The steps lose 9, 7 and 9 customers, so no step is the one big leak; the third order
   has the lowest conversion, 72.7%.

<details>
<summary>Explain it in your own words: why are the conditions in ON, not in WHERE?</summary>

A `WHERE` on `o.status` or the date would remove the customers who have no order in the window, so `signed_up` would
shrink to 40 and every conversion would be computed over customers who had already ordered. In `ON`, the customer stays
with a count of 0.

</details>
