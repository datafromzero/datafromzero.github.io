## The question

The owner of the cafes asks:

> We want to send a welcome offer to customers who signed up but have never ordered. Who are they? And do we have
> customers whose only order was canceled?

Three rows of `customers`:

| customer_id | signup_date | city | loyalty_tier |
|---:|---|---|---|
| 140 | 2026-06-10 | Alameda | bronze |
| 141 | 2026-06-12 | Alameda | silver |
| 147 | 2026-06-22 | Alameda | bronze |

Customer 141 placed orders 2673 and 2744. Customers 140 and 147 appear in no order, so an inner join would never show
them.

## Warm-up

Answer from memory before you open anything. Then click to check.

1. In *INNER JOIN: pairing rows on a key* (S19), write the join of `orders` to `customers` on `customer_id` that
   returned 1,799 rows from 1,800 orders. Which order was missing, and why?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   ```sql
   SELECT COUNT(*)
   FROM orders AS o
   INNER JOIN customers AS c ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id;
   ```

   Order 1335, the walk-in, has an empty `customer_id`. A comparison with an empty value is never true, so it pairs
   with nobody.

   </details>

2. In *NULL: unknown is not zero* (S4), how do you test for an empty value, and why does `= NULL` not work?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   `IS NULL`. A comparison with an unknown value is never true, so `= NULL` keeps no row.

   </details>

### 1. LEFT JOIN keeps the left table's lonely rows

An inner join keeps only the pairs. A **LEFT JOIN** also keeps every row of the left table, the one written before
`JOIN`: a row with no match comes out once, with `NULL` (an empty value, S4) in every column of the right table.

*Excel:* when `XLOOKUP` finds nothing (E8, *Bring in a value by key with XLOOKUP*), the row stays and shows `#N/A`, or the
text you gave for that case. A `LEFT JOIN` does the same with `NULL`.

**Try it**: first what an inner join keeps.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS joined_rows,
       COUNT(DISTINCT c.customer_id) AS customers_paired
FROM customers AS c
INNER JOIN orders AS o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id;`} />

Expected: 1 row, 2 columns. *Why:* **1799** rows, the orders that name a customer (S19), and **145** customers. The
other 5 of the 150 pair with nothing, so the inner join dropped them without a word.

**Try it**: keep everyone with `LEFT JOIN`. Before you run it: how many rows?

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS joined_rows,
       COUNT(o.order_id) AS paired_rows,
       COUNT(DISTINCT c.customer_id) AS customers
FROM customers AS c
LEFT JOIN orders AS o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id;`} />

Expected: 1 row, 3 columns. *Why:* **1804** rows: the 1,799 pairs plus one row for each of the 5 customers with no
order. `COUNT(o.order_id)` skips `NULL` (S7), so it counts only the pairs. **150** customers: none lost. Order 1335 is not
here, because `customers` is the left table.

**Try it**: see the `NULL`s.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT c.customer_id, c.loyalty_tier, o.order_id, o.status
FROM customers AS c
LEFT JOIN orders AS o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
WHERE c.customer_id IN (42, 140, 141, 147)
ORDER BY c.customer_id, o.order_id;`} />

Expected: 5 rows, 4 columns. *Why:* customer 141 has two orders and so two rows. Customers 140 and 147 have one row
each, with `NULL` in `order_id` and `status`. Customer 42's only order, the canceled order 1461, pairs normally. The same
five rows, drawn:

### 2. Find the rows with no partner

To list the rows of one table that have no partner in another, keep the unpaired rows with a `LEFT JOIN`. Then keep the
ones where a column that is never empty on a real right-table row, such as its primary key, is `NULL`. Test it with
`IS NULL`, never `= NULL` (S4).

DuckDB can also write it directly: `ANTI JOIN` gives the left rows with no match, and `SEMI JOIN` the left rows with at
least one. Neither returns more rows than the left table has.

**Try it**: the anti-join with `LEFT JOIN` and `IS NULL`.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT c.customer_id, c.signup_date, c.loyalty_tier
FROM customers AS c
LEFT JOIN orders AS o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
WHERE o.order_id IS NULL
ORDER BY c.customer_id;`} />

Expected: 3 columns. *Why:* **5** rows: customers 140, 147, 148, 149 and 150, who all signed up between June 10 and
June 26. That answers the first question.

**Try it**: a second route, with `ANTI JOIN`.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT c.customer_id
FROM customers AS c
ANTI JOIN orders AS o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
ORDER BY c.customer_id;`} />

Expected: 1 column, the same customers as the `LEFT JOIN` query above. *Why:* the same list, with no `NULL` test to write.

### 3. Where the condition goes: ON or WHERE

`ON` decides which rows pair. `WHERE` runs afterward, on the finished rows, the padded ones included, and it sees their
`NULL`s (*How a query runs: the order of the clauses*, S12). So a condition on the right table gives a different answer
in each place.

**Try it**: the tempting way to ask about completed orders.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT COUNT(*) AS joined_rows,
       COUNT(DISTINCT c.customer_id) AS customers
FROM customers AS c
LEFT JOIN orders AS o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id
WHERE o.status = 'completed';`} />

Expected: 1 row, 2 columns. *Why:* **1798** rows and **144** customers. The 5 customers with no order are gone. On an unpaired row `o.status` is `NULL`, and `NULL = 'completed'` is not true, so the row leaves. The
LEFT JOIN has become an inner join.

**Try it**: put the condition in `ON`. Before you run it: how many customers have no completed order?

<RunSQL query={`SELECT c.customer_id
FROM customers AS c
LEFT JOIN orders AS o
       ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id AND o.status = 'completed'
WHERE o.order_id IS NULL
ORDER BY c.customer_id;`} />

Expected: 1 column. *Why:* **6** customers: the five from unit 2, and customer 42, whose only order was canceled, the one customer the `WHERE` above also lost. Now
`status` only decides who pairs, and customer 42 stays unpaired. "Never ordered" gives 5 and "no completed order" gives 6:
say which question you answer.

<details>
<summary>Explain it in your own words: why does the WHERE in unit 3 lose the five customers, while the same condition in ON keeps them?</summary>

A customer with no order has nothing in `status`, so a test on `status` has nothing to say yes to, and `WHERE` throws the
row away. In `ON` the test asks a different question: does this order qualify as a partner? A customer with no
qualifying order is just unpaired, and a LEFT JOIN keeps unpaired rows.

</details>

### 5. Check the numbers

No new query. Each join keeps the pairs plus its kept side's lonely rows: `INNER` 1,799 (S19); `LEFT` 1,799 + 5 = 1,804;
`RIGHT` 1,799 + 1 = 1,800; `FULL` 1,799 + 5 + 1 = 1,805. S8 counted 145 customers who ordered, and 150 − 145 = 5, the
list of unit 2. The sixth customer without a completed order is customer 42.

<Aside type="tip" title="Remember">

- `LEFT JOIN` keeps every row of the left table; with no match, the right table's columns are `NULL`.
- To find rows with no partner: `LEFT JOIN`, then the right table's key `IS NULL` (or `ANTI JOIN`).
- `ON` decides who pairs; `WHERE` filters the finished rows. A `WHERE` on the right table can undo a `LEFT JOIN`.
- `RIGHT` keeps the right table's unpaired rows, `FULL` both sides'.

**Common trap: "`NOT IN` is just another way to write an anti join."** DuckDB's page says an anti join has the logic of
`NOT IN`, except that it ignores `NULL` values from the right table. That exception matters here: the customer ids of
`orders` include a `NULL` (the walk-in order 1335), and a comparison with `NULL` is never true, so a `NOT IN` over those
ids gives wrong results, although some customers never ordered. *Subqueries and EXISTS* (S23) goes further.

</Aside>

<RunSQL query={`-- Write your query here, then press Run.`} />

## Check yourself

Without looking back: what do INNER, LEFT, RIGHT and FULL joins do with unpaired rows, and how do you list the rows of
one table that have no partner in another?

<details>
<summary>Answer</summary>

INNER drops unpaired rows from both tables. LEFT keeps those of the left table, RIGHT those of the right table, FULL
those of both, with `NULL` in the other table's columns. To list rows with no partner: `LEFT JOIN`, then `WHERE` the
right table's key `IS NULL`.

</details>
