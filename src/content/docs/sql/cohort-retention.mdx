## The question

The owner of the cafes asks:

> Of the customers who joined each month this year, how many were still ordering in the months after? Do newer customers
> come back as well as older ones?

Three customers and the cohort each one belongs to:

| customer_id | signup_date | cohort_month |
|---:|---|---|
| 86 | 2026-01-02 | 2026-01-01 |
| 87 | 2026-01-03 | 2026-01-01 |
| 97 | 2026-02-03 | 2026-02-01 |

## Warm-up

From memory first, then click to check.

1. In *Working with dates and time* (S11), write an expression that gives the first day of the month of `signup_date`, as a
   date.

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   `CAST(DATE_TRUNC('month', signup_date) AS DATE)`. `DATE_TRUNC` returns a timestamp, so the `CAST` turns it back into a
   date. For `2026-01-02` it gives `2026-01-01`.

   </details>

2. In *Funnels: where do people drop off?* (S25), what does overall conversion divide by, and what does step conversion
   divide by?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   Overall conversion divides a step by the signups; step conversion divides it by the step above.

   </details>

## The big idea

A **cohort** is a group of customers who start in the same month, and **retention** is the share of each cohort that is active again, month after month.

## Learn by doing

Five small steps. Each gives you one idea, then you try it in the editor under it. It is the funnel of S25 stretched over time, for the 65 customers who signed up in 2026. Every order here is a completed order, and the walk-in order 1335 has no customer, so the joins drop it.

### 1. One cohort per customer

A customer's cohort is the first day of their signup month. `DATE_TRUNC` cuts the signup date down to it, and `CAST` turns the result back into a date.

Retention divides by the **size** of the cohort: everyone who signed up that month, whether or not they ever ordered. It is the same number for every cell of a row.

**Try it**. Grain: one row per customer.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT customer_id, signup_date,
       CAST(DATE_TRUNC('month', signup_date) AS DATE) AS cohort_month
FROM customers
WHERE signup_date >= DATE '2026-01-01'
ORDER BY customer_id
LIMIT 5;`} />

Expected: 5 rows, 3 columns; customer 86 is in the cohort `2026-01-01`. Now the size of each cohort.

<RunSQL query={`SELECT CAST(DATE_TRUNC('month', signup_date) AS DATE) AS cohort_month, COUNT(*) AS cohort_size
FROM customers
WHERE signup_date >= DATE '2026-01-01'
GROUP BY cohort_month
ORDER BY cohort_month;`} />

Expected: 6 rows, 2 columns; the sizes add up to 65. *Why:* these are the denominators of the whole lesson.

### 2. Months since signup

A customer is **active** in a month when at least one completed order, at any store, falls in it. This is the lesson's own definition; other conventions are in use.

Months are calendar months, not blocks of 30 days. The DuckDB documentation says `DATE_DIFF` counts the part boundaries between two dates, inclusive of the later date and exclusive of the earlier one. From the first day of the cohort month, that gives whole **months since**: month 0 is the signup month.

**Try it**. Grain: one row per customer per active month.

<RunSQL query={`WITH cohorts AS (
  SELECT customer_id, CAST(DATE_TRUNC('month', signup_date) AS DATE) AS cohort_month
  FROM customers
  WHERE signup_date >= DATE '2026-01-01'
),
activity AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT c.cohort_month, c.customer_id,
         DATE_DIFF('month', c.cohort_month, CAST(o.ordered_at AS DATE)) AS months_since
  FROM cohorts c
  JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id AND o.status = 'completed'
)
SELECT * FROM activity
ORDER BY customer_id, months_since
LIMIT 6;`} />

Expected: 6 rows, 3 columns. Customer 86 has months 0, 3, 4 and 5, with no row for February and March. *Why:* `DISTINCT` keeps one row per customer per month, however many orders. The inner join leaves out customers with no order: they stay in the denominator, not here.

### 3. Count a cell, then divide

A **cell** is one cohort in one month since signup. Count the customers active in it, and divide by the cohort size. Each cell is judged on its own month: a customer active in month 3 but not in month 2 counts in month 3 only.

**Try it**. Before you run it: each cohort is followed from its own month to June. How many cells, so how many rows, will the result have?

<RunSQL query={`WITH cohorts AS (
  SELECT customer_id, CAST(DATE_TRUNC('month', signup_date) AS DATE) AS cohort_month
  FROM customers
  WHERE signup_date >= DATE '2026-01-01'
),
cohort_sizes AS (
  SELECT cohort_month, COUNT(*) AS cohort_size
  FROM cohorts
  GROUP BY cohort_month
),
activity AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT c.cohort_month, c.customer_id,
         DATE_DIFF('month', c.cohort_month, CAST(o.ordered_at AS DATE)) AS months_since
  FROM cohorts c
  JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id AND o.status = 'completed'
),
retained AS (
  SELECT cohort_month, months_since, COUNT(*) AS customers
  FROM activity
  GROUP BY cohort_month, months_since
),
retention AS (
  SELECT r.cohort_month, r.months_since, s.cohort_size, r.customers,
         ROUND(100.0 * r.customers / s.cohort_size, 1) AS pct
  FROM retained r
  JOIN cohort_sizes s ON s.cohort_month = r.cohort_month
)
SELECT * FROM retention
ORDER BY cohort_month, months_since;`} />

Expected: 21 rows, 5 columns. The first row is January, month 0. *Excel:* a PivotTable (E16, *Grouping and share of total in pivots*) can group dates into months. It shows these counts only from a list with one row per customer and active month, with the months since worked out in a helper column: put the cohort month in Rows, the months since in Columns and a count in Values. Its "% of Row Total" divides by the row's total, the sum of the counts, not by the cohort size.

*Why:* 21 is 6 + 5 + 4 + 3 + 2 + 1, the cells of the triangle. A cell where nobody was active would not appear at all, so the right row count is the check that none is missing.

### 4. Turn it into the triangle

Pivot the table (S9): one row per cohort, one column per month since, with `MAX` because each cell has exactly one value. A month the data does not cover stays empty. The data ends on June 30, so a later month was never observed, and 0% would claim that nobody came.

**Try it**: the same five steps, with a different last `SELECT`.

<RunSQL query={`WITH cohorts AS (
  SELECT customer_id, CAST(DATE_TRUNC('month', signup_date) AS DATE) AS cohort_month
  FROM customers
  WHERE signup_date >= DATE '2026-01-01'
),
cohort_sizes AS (
  SELECT cohort_month, COUNT(*) AS cohort_size
  FROM cohorts
  GROUP BY cohort_month
),
activity AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT c.cohort_month, c.customer_id,
         DATE_DIFF('month', c.cohort_month, CAST(o.ordered_at AS DATE)) AS months_since
  FROM cohorts c
  JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id AND o.status = 'completed'
),
retained AS (
  SELECT cohort_month, months_since, COUNT(*) AS customers
  FROM activity
  GROUP BY cohort_month, months_since
),
retention AS (
  SELECT r.cohort_month, r.months_since, s.cohort_size, r.customers,
         ROUND(100.0 * r.customers / s.cohort_size, 1) AS pct
  FROM retained r
  JOIN cohort_sizes s ON s.cohort_month = r.cohort_month
)
SELECT cohort_month,
       MAX(cohort_size) AS cohort_size,
       MAX(CASE WHEN months_since = 0 THEN pct END) AS m0,
       MAX(CASE WHEN months_since = 1 THEN pct END) AS m1,
       MAX(CASE WHEN months_since = 2 THEN pct END) AS m2,
       MAX(CASE WHEN months_since = 3 THEN pct END) AS m3,
       MAX(CASE WHEN months_since = 4 THEN pct END) AS m4,
       MAX(CASE WHEN months_since = 5 THEN pct END) AS m5
FROM retention
GROUP BY cohort_month
ORDER BY cohort_month;`} />

Expected: 6 rows, 8 columns; January's row is `54.5, 63.6, 63.6, 100.0, 100.0, 90.9`, and each later row has one `NULL` more at the end. *Why:* the `NULL` cells are the months the data does not cover.

Read it with care: each cohort has 8 to 16 customers, so one customer moves a cell by 6 to 12 points, and "active" is not "never left": customer 86 skipped February and March.

<Aside type="tip" title="Remember">

- A cohort is the customers who start in the same month. Retention is the share of it active in each later month.
- The denominator is the size of the cohort, whether or not they ever ordered.
- Months are calendar months from the cohort month. Active means at least one completed order that month.
- Count each customer once per month. An empty cell is not 0%.

**Common trap: "retention can only go down, like a funnel."** A funnel step counts people who reached it, and reaching step 3 includes reaching step 2, so the counts shrink. A retention cell counts people active in one month. A customer who skips a month is missing from it and is counted again on return, so a row can rise.

</Aside>

**By how many percentage points did the January cohort's share change from each month to the one before?** The editor below holds the five steps of unit 3, written for you. Only the last `SELECT` is yours. Expected: 6 rows, 4 columns
(the cohort month, the months since signup, the share and the change); the first change is `NULL`.

<RunSQL query={`WITH cohorts AS (
  SELECT customer_id, CAST(DATE_TRUNC('month', signup_date) AS DATE) AS cohort_month
  FROM customers
  WHERE signup_date >= DATE '2026-01-01'
),
cohort_sizes AS (
  SELECT cohort_month, COUNT(*) AS cohort_size
  FROM cohorts
  GROUP BY cohort_month
),
activity AS (
  SELECT DISTINCT c.cohort_month, c.customer_id,
         DATE_DIFF('month', c.cohort_month, CAST(o.ordered_at AS DATE)) AS months_since
  FROM cohorts c
  JOIN orders o ON o.customer_id = c.customer_id AND o.status = 'completed'
),
retained AS (
  SELECT cohort_month, months_since, COUNT(*) AS customers
  FROM activity
  GROUP BY cohort_month, months_since
),
retention AS (
  SELECT r.cohort_month, r.months_since, s.cohort_size, r.customers,
         ROUND(100.0 * r.customers / s.cohort_size, 1) AS pct
  FROM retained r
  JOIN cohort_sizes s ON s.cohort_month = r.cohort_month
)
SELECT cohort_month, months_since, pct
FROM retention
WHERE cohort_month = DATE '2026-01-01'
ORDER BY months_since;`} />

Now finish it: add a fourth column, `change`, that shows `pct` minus the share of the month before.

<details>
<summary>Hint</summary>

`LAG` looks at the row above within its window (S16). The row above, for a cohort, is the previous month since signup.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Half-solution</summary>

```sql
OVER (PARTITION BY cohort_month ORDER BY ...)
```

<Solution>

```sql
SELECT cohort_month, months_since, pct,
       ROUND(pct - LAG(pct) OVER (PARTITION BY cohort_month ORDER BY months_since), 1) AS change
FROM retention
WHERE cohort_month = DATE '2026-01-01'
ORDER BY months_since;
```

The changes are `NULL`, `9.1`, `0.0`, `36.4`, `0.0` and `-9.1`. The biggest step is from month 2 to month 3, when 4 more of the
11 customers were active (63.6% to 100.0%). The change of the first row is `NULL` because month 0 has no month before it.

</Solution>
