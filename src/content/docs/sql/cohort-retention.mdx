A **cohort** is a group of customers who start in the same period. **Retention** asks how many of each cohort are active
again, one month after another. Here the cohorts are the customers who signed up in each month of 2026, followed through
the six months of orders. It is the funnel of *Funnels: where do people drop off?* (S25) stretched over time.

Python. Each query sits between `con.sql("""` and `""").show()`: only the text inside the triple quotes is SQL. The
notebook cells leave out the closing `;` that this page shows.

Answer from memory before you open anything. Then click to check.

1. In *Working with dates and time* (S11), write an expression that gives the first day of the month of `signup_date`, as a
   date.

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   `CAST(DATE_TRUNC('month', signup_date) AS DATE)`. `DATE_TRUNC` returns a timestamp, so the `CAST` turns it back into a
   date. For `2026-01-02` it gives `2026-01-01`.

   </details>

2. In S25, what does overall conversion divide by, and what does step conversion divide by?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   Overall conversion divides a step by the signups; step conversion divides it by the step above.

   </details>

3. In *Comparing to the previous row: LAG, LEAD and moving averages* (S16), what does `LAG` return on the first row of its
   partition?

   <details>
   <summary>Answer</summary>

   `NULL`: there is no row above it.

   </details>

## The question

The owner of the cafes asks:

> Of the customers who joined each month this year, how many were still ordering in the months after? Do newer customers
> come back as well as older ones?

Three customers and the cohort each one belongs to:

| customer_id | signup_date | cohort_month |
|---:|---|---|
| 86 | 2026-01-02 | 2026-01-01 |
| 87 | 2026-01-03 | 2026-01-01 |
| 97 | 2026-02-03 | 2026-02-01 |

By the end you will have a table with one row per cohort and one column per month since signup, every percentage with its
denominator, and a clear line between a month that had no activity and a month the data does not cover yet.

Four decisions define the table. State them before the SQL, as in S25.

- **Cohort:** the signup month, for the 65 customers who signed up in 2026. For them, signup is the first event we can
  see. For the 85 who signed up earlier, the data starts on January 1, so a first order in January may not be their
  first (Exercise 2).
- **Months since:** the number of calendar months from the signup month to the month of the order. Month 0 is the signup
  month itself.
- **Active in month k:** at least one completed order in that month, at any store. A customer who skips a month and
  returns counts in the later month only.
- **Denominator:** the size of the cohort, every customer who signed up that month, including those who never ordered.
  It is the same for every month of a row. That is why month 0 is below 100%: signing up is not ordering.

How many months can each cohort be observed? The data ends on June 30. January signups have 6 months (0 to 5), February 5,
March 4, April 3, May 2 and June 1. The cells after that do not exist yet: they are empty, not 0%.

## Worked example

Question: **what share of each signup cohort ordered in month 0, 1, 2 and so on?** Run the notebook's setup cell, then
one cell per step. Each step gives the shape of the result to expect. Orders are completed orders only; the walk-in
order 1335 has no customer, so the join drops it.

1. **Assign each customer to a cohort.** Grain: one row per customer.

   ```sql
   SELECT customer_id, signup_date,
          CAST(DATE_TRUNC('month', signup_date) AS DATE) AS cohort_month
   FROM customers
   WHERE signup_date >= DATE '2026-01-01'
   ORDER BY customer_id
   LIMIT 5;
   ```

   Expected: 5 rows, 3 columns; customer 86 is in the cohort `2026-01-01`. *Why:* every customer gets one cohort, from the
   first event we can see. Now the size of each cohort:

   ```sql
   SELECT CAST(DATE_TRUNC('month', signup_date) AS DATE) AS cohort_month, COUNT(*) AS cohort_size
   FROM customers
   WHERE signup_date >= DATE '2026-01-01'
   GROUP BY cohort_month
   ORDER BY cohort_month;
   ```
