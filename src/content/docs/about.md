## The short version

I didn't come from IT or computer science. I came from business school, a market research desk, and
one unplanned conversation over coffee. If you're starting from spreadsheets and self-doubt, this
site was built for you.

## A pattern I didn't see

I studied business administration. Without really trying, I kept doing well in the same kinds of
classes: market research, data analysis, marketing metrics. My university piloted an extracurricular
course in management science, and my class was the first to take it, the guinea pigs. I finished
every module with top marks.

I still didn't notice what that was telling me. I thought I was good at marketing, so I did the
obvious thing and earned a master's in marketing. Along the way I was trained in predictive
analytics. That detail would matter later.

## Burnout, and a team I clicked with

After graduation I went into market research, because a professor saw potential in me there. A few
years in, as a research executive, I burned out. It wasn't what I was looking for.

But one thing kept standing out. I worked unusually well with the DP team: data processing, the
people who clean and process survey data so researchers can write their reports. I didn't understand
why yet.

## The coffee that changed direction

After I quit, I ran into someone from that DP team. We were both having coffee alone, so we started
talking, and he asked a simple question: why not try computer science, and data in particular?

That was the first time I realized data was a career. It also sounded impossible: syntax, data
structures, algorithms, and a thousand other things.

> I wasn't late to data. I just hadn't found its name yet.

## Starting from zero

So I started small, with a few SQL and Python courses on bootcamp sites. I loved them. Once my
queries felt solid, I applied to teach data analytics and machine learning, drawing on the predictive
analytics from my master's.

Students liked my classes. Some even paid to take them again.

That's when I decided to pursue computer science for real, especially its use in big data.

## Learning faster than ever

Today, AI tools help developers write and understand code. They have sped up how fast I learn and how
deep I can go into data infrastructure, and they are still getting better as I write this. So I
decided to build this site together with AI agents.

## Why datafromzero exists

datafromzero is the roadmap I wish I'd had when I walked out of that café: free, in order, and built
for people who start with spreadsheets, not code. It goes from Excel and Google Sheets to SQL and
Python, then an advanced core, and finally four data careers, with exercises and practice data you
can reuse, written to be read on a phone.

Whether you haven't started yet, have just begun, or are already on your way, I hope you find your
next step here, and the confidence to build on what you're already good at.

You don't need the "right" background. You need a first step.

## How the content is made

- **One voice.** The lessons are written from scratch by one author, so the voice and the order stay consistent.
- **Sourced and dated.** Every lesson lists its sources and the date it was last reviewed.
- **Versioned.** The curriculum has version numbers and a [changelog](/changelog/), and the whole work can be [cited](/cite/).
- **Open.** Content is CC BY 4.0, code is MIT, datasets are CC BY 4.0. See the [license](/license/) page.
