## The question

The retention lead of a budgeting app sends you a file, a clock and a question. She writes: *Support can write to about one in five new Plus subscribers a month. Who should it be? I want to know that a model beats a simple rule, and I do not want to find out in August that the number was too good to be true.* You have 90 minutes, and the rubric is below, so you can read it now.

There is no single right answer. Several models and several recommendations earn full marks; a column you use without saying when the app knew it earns none.

**Answer three questions.** How well can you rank who cancels at the first renewal, using only what the app knew at the prediction moment? Is that better than a rule? What do you tell the retention lead?

**The reviewer will ask three things.** Write one paragraph for each.

1. **Is the number real?** How was it scored, against what, and how sure are you?
2. **Could it be known in time?** For each column, when did the app know it, and what did you do about the ones that came late?
3. **What do we do with it?** Whom does support write to, what does that rest on, and when does it fail?

**Hand in three things, one page in all,** with the code that lets someone rerun them:

1. A **scope note**, written before you look at the file again, in at most five lines: the question in one sentence, the minutes for each step, two things you will not do, one assumption.
2. A **column log**: one line for each column, with when it was known and whether you used it.
3. A **defense page** of at most 300 words: the answer first, the three paragraphs, one limitations line and the recommendation.

## Rubric

Eight items, each scored 2 (full), 1 (partial) or 0 (none): 16 in all. The rubric grades the chain from file to recommendation: any model, column list or recommendation that your reasoning supports can score 16.

**1. Scope note.**

- **Full:** all four parts (question, minutes adding to 85 or less, two things left out, one assumption), written before the column log.
- **Partial:** two or three parts.
- **None:** one part or none, or written after the work.

**2. Frame.**

- **Full:** one sentence says what a row is; the target; the prediction moment as a time, not a date; and the people left out, each group with a count and a reason.
- **Partial:** three of the four.
- **None:** two or fewer.

**3. Column log.**

- **Full:** every column has a line saying when it was known against the prediction moment and whether you used it; every rejected column has its evidence (a rule of the dictionary, a number, or both); the final list is given.
- **Partial:** most columns have a line, or rejections have no evidence.
- **None:** columns used with no timing rule.

**4. Baselines.**

- **Full:** a flat guess and a one-column rule, scored on the same held-out people and the same metric as the model, and the model's gain over each with an interval.
- **Partial:** one baseline, or gains with no interval.
- **None:** no baseline.

**5. Evaluation.**

- **Full:** the split made before any column was chosen; candidates compared on the training people only; the held-out people scored once; the seed stated; and how far the score moves with the split.
- **Partial:** a held-out score with no sign of how far it moves.
- **None:** a choice made on the held-out people, or no held-out people.

**6. Defense page.**

- **Full:** the answer in the first sentence; each of the three questions in its own paragraph of at most three sentences, each with a number; at most 300 words.
- **Partial:** two questions answered with numbers, or over 300 words.
- **None:** one question or none.

**7. Limitations.**

- **Full:** at least three limits, each tied to a number or to a rule of the data; one thing you did not try.
- **Partial:** one or two tied limits.
- **None:** generic limits, such as "the data is small", or none.

**8. Recommendation.**

- **Full:** one action, one reason tied to a number, one risk and one next check.
- **Partial:** two or three of the four.
- **None:** one or none, or an action the numbers do not support.

A total of 13 or more is ready to send. From 8 to 12, redo the items that scored 0 or 1. Below 8, redo the frame and the column log before you redo the model.

### The frame

The file has 1,792 people and 348 cancelers, 19.4 percent. Of the 2,849 people who ever subscribed, 668 started on an annual plan and 389 started a monthly plan in June, and most of those had fewer than 30 days before the export: both groups are left out, because the target needs a monthly plan and a full 30 days. A person with several subscriptions counts once, with the first.

### The column log

Five number columns are known by day 14: `days_to_buy`, `activity_before`, `visits_14d`, `expenses_14d` and `plus_uses_14d`, and so are the signup columns `channel`, `platform`, `region` and `plan_at_signup`; `user_id` and `started_on` are labels. Four number columns are not. First the split, before any column is chosen: 25 percent of the people are held out, with the same share of cancelers in both parts. Then a quick screen on the training people scores each number column alone. It flags and chooses nothing.

```python
# The leakage check, part 1. The split comes first: 25 percent of the people are held out, with the same share of cancelers in both parts. Then a quick screen on the training people only: how well does each number column predict the outcome alone? A flag, not a verdict.
take_home["has_reason"] = take_home["cancel_reason"].notna().astype(int)           # 1 when a reason is filled in
take_home["annual_now"] = (take_home["plan_now"] == "plus_annual").astype(int)     # 1 when the latest saved row says annual
train, test = train_test_split(take_home, test_size=0.25, random_state=SEED, stratify=take_home["canceled"])
print(len(train), len(test), int(train["canceled"].sum()), int(test["canceled"].sum()))
CHECKED = ["days_to_buy", "activity_before", "visits_14d", "expenses_14d", "plus_uses_14d", "active_events", "plus_uses", "annual_now", "has_reason"]


def alone(col):
    """ROC AUC of one column used as a score on the training people, in either direction: 0.5 knows nothing, 1.0 knows everything."""
    a = roc_auc_score(train["canceled"], train[col])
    return round(float(max(a, 1 - a)), 3)


for col, score in sorted(((col, alone(col)) for col in CHECKED), key=lambda item: -item[1]):
    print(f"{col}: {score}")
print(train.groupby("annual_now")["canceled"].agg(["size", "sum"]).to_string())
```

Two columns score far above the rest. `cancel_reason` is written when a subscription ends, so it knows the answer; it is filled for people who cancel after day 30 as well, which is why it is not a perfect copy of the target. `plus_uses` counts Plus feature uses up to July 1, and the dataset README says these events exist only while a subscription is active, so a person who stays keeps adding to it. Two more columns pass the screen and still fail the rule. `annual_now` scores 0.519, because only 41 training people have it, and none of them canceled: a switch to annual happens at a later renewal, after the person stayed through the first. `active_events` counts visits and expenses up to July 1; the README says paying for Plus does not change how often someone visits, and the next cell shows it adds 0.001. The screen is a flag, not a verdict. The verdict is the timing rule.

```python
# The leakage check, part 2: the held-out people score the model with and without each late column.
CATEGORIES = ["channel", "platform", "region"]
HONEST = ["days_to_buy", "activity_before", "visits_14d", "expenses_14d", "plus_uses_14d"]       # every one known by day 14


def held_out_auc(num):
    """Fit on the training people with these number columns and score the held-out people."""
    model = recipe(CATEGORIES, num).fit(train[CATEGORIES + num], train["canceled"])
    return round(float(roc_auc_score(test["canceled"], model.predict_proba(test[CATEGORIES + num])[:, 1])), 3)


print("honest columns:", held_out_auc(HONEST))
print("plus_uses added:", held_out_auc(HONEST + ["plus_uses"]))
print("active_events added:", held_out_auc(HONEST + ["active_events"]))
print("every column:", held_out_auc(HONEST + ["plus_uses", "active_events", "has_reason", "annual_now"]))
```

### Baselines and model

```python
# Three candidates, compared by five-fold cross-validation on the training people only. Then the held-out people, used once.
CANDIDATES = {"channel only": (["channel"], []),
              "channel + 3 counts": (["channel"], ["visits_14d", "expenses_14d", "plus_uses_14d"]),
              "all honest columns": (CATEGORIES, HONEST)}
folds = StratifiedKFold(5, shuffle=True, random_state=SEED)
cv_means = {}
for name, (cat, num) in CANDIDATES.items():
    s = cross_val_score(recipe(cat, num), train[cat + num], train["canceled"], cv=folds, scoring="roc_auc")
    cv_means[name] = float(s.mean())
    print(f"{name}: {s.mean():.3f} ({s.min():.3f} to {s.max():.3f})")
chosen = max(cv_means, key=cv_means.get)
print("chosen:", chosen)

yt = test["canceled"].to_numpy()
cat, num = CANDIDATES[chosen]
p_model = recipe(cat, num).fit(train[cat + num], train["canceled"]).predict_proba(test[cat + num])[:, 1]
p_rule = recipe(["channel"], []).fit(train[["channel"]], train["canceled"]).predict_proba(test[["channel"]])[:, 1]       # the one-column rule: each channel's own cancel rate
p_flat = np.full(len(test), train["canceled"].mean())                                                                # the flat guess: everyone gets the training share
print([round(float(roc_auc_score(yt, p)), 3) for p in (p_flat, p_rule, p_model)])
print(auc_gain(p_model, p_rule, yt), auc_gain(p_model, p_flat, yt))
top = np.argsort(-p_model)[: len(test) // 5]                                                                         # the fifth of people with the highest chance
print(len(top), int(yt[top].sum()), round(float(yt[top].mean()), 3), round(float(yt.mean()), 3))
```
