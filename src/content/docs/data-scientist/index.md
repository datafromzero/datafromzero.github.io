## What you finish with

**There is no certificate.** datafromzero gives no verified certificate: it has no accounts, so it cannot check who finished. This is the message of the author of the site to every learner:

> A certificate has no value if you do not have personal projects that fit what employers need, and the skills and way of thinking to back them up. However strong the issuer is, if your ability does not yet fit or meet the job, the certificate is worth nothing.

What you can show instead is a project. If you want a sheet for yourself, the [completion page](/completion/) prints on two pages, and says on it that it is not a verified certificate.

### The capstone

The capstone, [*Capstone: from model to decision*](/data-scientist/decision-product-case-study/) (DS16), has you hand in [seven things](/data-scientist/decision-product-case-study/#deliverables): a framing page, baselines with a model and its evaluation, an error analysis with limits, a plan on paper (make, watch, retrain, undo), a one-page decision memo, an AI verification log and a short explain-back. You score them yourself with a 16-point rubric, or ask a mentor. Nothing is sent to this site.

### What employers screen for

[Research issue #118](https://github.com/datafromzero/datafromzero.github.io/blob/main/research/issues/118-what-employers-will-hire-for-in-2026-and-beyond-demand-entry.md) read 26 entry-level Data Scientist postings in full (2026-11-22). Python is named in 23 of them, SQL in 21, and presenting to non-technical readers in 22. A bachelor's degree is required in 14, a master's in 3 and a PhD in none. Project experience is valued in at least 6, and only 2 of the 26 describe an interview task. These postings are one sample, not the whole market.

### Show your project

A project does not promise an interview or a job. It is evidence you can point to, and the same issue puts work samples among the stronger predictors of job performance, though structured interviews score higher (its Finding 14).

1. Put the capstone in a public repository on GitHub: the notebook, the decision memo and the plan. Use only the synthetic data of the track, never real customer or employer data.
2. Start the README with the decision you framed and the rule that would make it a success. Then give the baselines, the model's score on people it did not learn from, where it fails, and the limits.
3. Add one line to your CV and one to your LinkedIn profile, in your own words and with what you actually did:
   - **CV:** "Framed a customer win-back decision on a synthetic app dataset, compared a model with baselines on held-out people, analyzed where it fails, and wrote a one-page decision memo with a plan to monitor, retrain and undo it; logged every AI-assisted step. (link)"
   - **LinkedIn:** "Model-to-decision project on practice data: a stated success rule, honest evaluation against baselines, and a memo a manager can act on. (link)"
4. Be ready to talk it through:
   - State the decision and the success rule before you show the model.
   - Give the baseline and the honest gain: what the model beats, on people it did not learn from.
   - Show where it fails, with the count of people in that slice.
   - Say what you would watch, when you would retrain, and how to undo it.

When you are done, you can print the [completion page](/completion/).
