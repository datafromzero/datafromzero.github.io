## The question

Maya Okafor, head of growth at the budgeting app, writes: *On day 7 after signup we could send a $2 win-back offer to the people likely to leave. Should we, and to whom? I need a yes or a no I can defend to finance, with the risk written next to it.* Maya and her question are invented; the data are the synthetic `app-events` data.

You have built the pieces over the track; here you put them in one memo she can use, with a log that shows you checked what an AI told you. There is no single right memo: several decisions earn full marks, and a number you cannot trace earns none.

## Rubric

Eight items, each scored 2 (full), 1 (partial) or 0 (none): 16 in all. The rubric grades what you shipped and how well it holds up, not the decision you chose: any decision that your reasoning supports can score 16.

**1. Framing page.** Count six things: a named stakeholder; the decision in one sentence; a success definition with a number and a rule; the target and the prediction moment as a time; every assumption listed; each marked as an assumption.

- **Full:** all six.
- **Partial:** four or five.
- **None:** three or fewer.

**2. Baselines and evaluation.**

- **Full:** a flat guess and a simpler rung or rule beside the model; the held-out people used only after every choice; each gain with an interval; the value of the rule for every 1,000 people next to doing nothing and offering everybody, with an interval.
- **Partial:** baselines but no intervals, or no value of the rule.
- **None:** the model alone, or a choice made on the held-out people.

**3. Error analysis.**

- **Full:** at least two slices with the people counted in each; who the rule misses and who it offers to without need, with numbers; one cause tied to a rule of the data; one thing the analysis cannot say.
- **Partial:** one slice, or slices with no counts.
- **None:** an overall score only.

**4. Limits.**

- **Full:** at least three limits, each tied to a number or a rule of the data, including the assumption the decision rests on and the value at which it flips.
- **Partial:** one or two tied limits.
- **None:** generic limits, such as "the data is small", or none.

**5. The plan: make and undo.**

- **Full:** when and from what a score is made; where it is stored with its model version and time; who reads it; what happens when a run fails; a rollback; an owner.
- **Partial:** four or five of the six.
- **None:** three or fewer.

**6. The plan: watch and retrain.**

- **Full:** at least three named checks covering inputs, outputs and the outcome, each with a threshold, an owner and an action; a retraining trigger stated as a number and tried on past data, with how often it would have fired; one thing the checks cannot catch.
- **Partial:** checks without thresholds, or a trigger never tried on the past.
- **None:** "watch for drift" and nothing more.

**7. Decision memo.**

- **Full:** the decision first; for whom; three numbers, each with its rule (which people, which money, which assumption); the flip value; the risk; the next check and its owner; at most 300 words.
- **Partial:** four of those parts, or over 300 words.
- **None:** the model first and the decision missing, or a decision the numbers do not support.

**8. AI log and explain-back.**

- **Full:** a context pack with its four parts in order; a row for every AI output that fed a result, each with output, checks run (by number or name), verdict and reason; at least one revise or reject, or a sentence on why none; your name, the date and what you did not check; an explain-back that names one number and one check.
- **Partial:** a log with verdicts but no reasons or no named checks, or an explain-back that could describe any project.
- **None:** no log, or a list of prompts with no checks.

A total of 13 or more is strong. From 8 to 12 is solid with gaps: redo the items that scored 0 or 1. Below 8, rebuild the evaluation before you write the memo.

### Baselines, the rule and what it earns

The first reference cell scores the baselines on the 4,454 held-out people: ROC AUC 0.500 for the flat guess, 0.668 for the signup columns, 0.690 for the day-7 model. The model's paired gain is +0.022 (0.012 to 0.033) over the signup columns and +0.190 (0.176 to 0.206) over the flat guess. Then the rule:

```python
# The rule: offer when the chance is at least the threshold that earns most on the validation people. The held-out people are used in this lesson only after every choice.
grid = np.round(np.arange(0.05, 0.96, 0.01), 2)
best = float(max(grid, key=lambda t: value_per_1000(p_val >= t, yv)))
model_rule = p_test >= best
paid_social = (X_test["channel"] == "paid_social").to_numpy()      # a rule that fits on a sticky note
everybody = np.ones(len(yt), dtype=bool)
print(best, int(model_rule.sum()), round(float(model_rule.mean()), 3), int(paid_social.sum()))
print(value_gain(model_rule, None, yt), value_gain(paid_social, None, yt), value_gain(everybody, None, yt))      # what each rule earns, with its interval
print(value_gain(model_rule, paid_social, yt))                                                              # model rule minus paid-social rule
```

The mean chance stays within 0.037 of the real churn and the weekly AUC between 0.636 and 0.732, although the paid-social share of signups jumps from 0.096 to 0.299 in the week of April 13. An input change is an alert to look, not to retrain. Three checks:

- **Inputs.** The paid-social share of signups, and each platform's share of people with a paywall event in their first 7 days, by signup week. Alert: a share more than 0.10 from its mean of the last four weeks. Owner: the data scientist. Action: look for the cause.
- **Outputs.** The mean chance against the real churn of weeks whose 30 days are over. Alert: a gap over 0.05 or a weekly AUC under 0.60. Owner: the data scientist. Action: look; two alerts in a row retrain.
- **Outcome.** The return rate of the offered people against the held-back fifth. Alert: below 0.212. Owner: growth. Action: stop the offer.

**What the checks cannot catch:** a column already wrong when the four-week window starts, because an input check compares with the recent past; a one-time check against the data dictionary does.

The input check names the platform because on May 18 the iOS app began to send `paywall_viewed` for `view_paywall`. A pipeline that knew only the old name sees the iOS share fall, by signup week, from 0.337 (May 4) to 0.000 (May 18), against 0.357 with both names, and the model runs on without an error. The 0.10 alert catches it (Rule 10 of the Rules of Machine Learning: "Watch for silent failures").

**Retrain.** Rule 8 asks how much a model loses if it is a day, a week or a quarter old. On the 5,077 May signups, the model fitted on 5,622 people (before March) gives AUC 0.716 and the one fitted on 12,739 (before May) 0.717; the newer model's paired gain is +0.001 (-0.001 to 0.003): no measurable loss from two months of age. So no schedule; the trigger is the outputs check, two weeks in a row. On the 13 back-test weeks it fires 0 times, which cannot show that it would catch real decay: this data holds none.
