## The question

The churn model has served the app since March 1, trained on the 5,622 people who signed up before then. In the week of April 13 the app got 952 new people, up from 737 the week before. *Is the model still right, how would we know, and what would we do about it?* Every request the service scored is a line in a log, and each person's real outcome arrives 30 days after signup. Four weeks of the log, in people:

| week of | March 2 | April 6 | April 13 | April 20 |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| people | 745 | 737 | 952 | 1,063 |

By the end you can log a service, read drift in its inputs and in its answers, write a retraining trigger and a rollback plan before you need them, and stop a worse model at the door.

<Aside type="note" title="You already know this">

**Match.** In [*Tracking experiments and making runs reproducible*](/ml-engineer/experiment-tracking-and-reproducibility/) (MLE3) a run's record held what went in, what came out and what it depended on. The request log does the same for each request the service answers.

**Diverge.** A run happens once and is finished. A service answers for months, so you read a window of its log, and a request's outcome arrives weeks after its answer: unit 3 shows what that delay costs.

### 1. Log what the service saw

**Idea.** A request log has one line for each request: what came in, what went out, how long it took and what it cost. To judge an answer later you need to know which person it was for, so the caller sends an id in the header `x-request-id` and the log keeps it. The cost is compute only: the seconds the model worked, at an assumed price (`PRICE_PER_HOUR`, $0.10 an hour), not a quote.

```python
def log_request(request_id, inputs, chance, version, seconds):
    """Append one line: the request's id, its ten inputs, the chance, the model version, the milliseconds and the dollars."""
    row = {
        "request_id": request_id,
        **inputs,
        "churn_chance": chance,
        "model_version": version,
        "latency_ms": round(seconds * 1000, 3),
        "cost_usd": seconds * PRICE_PER_HOUR / 3600,
    }
```

**Try it:** the given script replays the spring in this process, with no server to start. It trains the model as it was on March 1, then sends every person who signed up from March 2 to May 31 to the service, in signup order, with their `user_id` as the request id. Then look at the first line of the log, and summarize it.

```bash

```

### 2. Data drift: the inputs have changed

**Idea.** *Data drift* is a change in the inputs a model sees, compared with the people it learned from. It needs no outcomes, so it can be seen the day it starts. The *population stability index* (PSI) measures it for one input: split the input into bins (each category, or ten bins cut at the deciles of the reference) and compare the share of people in each bin now with the reference:

$$
\text{PSI} = \sum_{\text{bins}} (\text{current share} - \text{reference share}) \times \ln\frac{\text{current share}}{\text{reference share}}
$$

It is 0 when the shares are equal and grows as they differ. The reference is the 5,622 people the model was trained on. `monitor` computes the PSI of all ten inputs for each week of signups and keeps the largest. The alert level, 0.1, is our choice: ten times the largest PSI of the six quiet weeks. The Rules of Machine Learning ask for actionable alerts, so the level is a number somebody picked for a reason.

**Try it:** one line for each week of signups: the people, the input with the largest PSI and its PSI, the mean chance the model gave, the day the outcomes of that week are all known, and (the log is read after June 30) the real churn rate and the AUC.

### 3. Concept drift: the outcome has changed, and the label is late

**Idea.** *Concept drift* is a change in the link between the inputs and the outcome: people who look the same now leave for other reasons. It is a change in the outcome, so the outcome is where you look for it, and the outcome of a person scored on day 7 is known on day 30, 23 days later. A week of signups can be judged only when its last person's 30 days are over: the `labels_ready` column. A *retraining trigger* is a rule written before it is needed; ours is two weeks in a row with an AUC under 0.60, a floor 0.035 under the worst quiet week (0.635), because one week of about 700 people is noisy. The alert says look, the trigger says act. Rule 10 of the Rules of Machine Learning is the reason for both: silent failures happen more in machine learning systems than in other systems.

The data has no concept change, so the next run is made up: `--invented` replaces, from May 11, the outcome of 7 in 10 people by a coin flip, whatever they did in week 1 (seed 2026). The inputs are not touched.

**Try it:** the same weekly report, with the invented outcomes. `tail` keeps the last six weeks and the two lines that list the alert weeks and the day retraining is due.

```bash
uv run python -m ml_starter.monitor weekly logs/replay.jsonl --invented | tail -n 8
```

**Predict:** The inputs are the same as in unit 2. From the week of May 11, what will the PSI column do and what will the AUC do? On which day will the trigger say retraining is due?

The alert would have had you retrain from April 20 for nothing; the trigger waits for evidence, and the price is the weeks the labels take.

### 4. A gate before release, and a way back after

**Idea.** A *quality gate* is a test that fails the build when the held-out score drops. The Rules of Machine Learning say to check a model's performance on held-out data before exporting it, and that many teams check the AUC then. Ours trains what the commit's code builds and requires a held-out AUC of at least 0.68: under the day-7 model's 0.690 and over the 0.668 of a model that ignores week 1. The split is fixed, and nothing is tuned against these people: the gate checks a release, it never chooses one.

```python
def test_the_model_this_code_trains_clears_the_floor():
    X_train, X_test, y_train, y_test = split(load_table())
    model = build_pipeline().fit(X_train, y_train)
    auc = roc_auc_score(y_test, model.predict_proba(X_test)[:, 1])
    assert auc >= FLOOR, f"held-out AUC {auc:.3f} is below the floor {FLOOR}"
```

A gate cannot catch everything, so write the way back before you need it. `docs/rollback_plan.md` says when, who, how, what to check and what to write down:

```markdown
# Rollback plan: the churn service

**When.** `/health` is not 200 for five minutes after a release, or the new version's held-out AUC is under 0.68, or it answers
worse than the version before it on labeled people. A drift alert alone is a reason to look.

**Who.** The engineer on call decides.

**How.** `uv run python -m ml_starter.rollback back`, then restart the service (or run the image built from the earlier file).

**Check.** `/health` names the earlier version, and `scripts/person.json` gets the chance that version gave before.

**Afterwards.** Write down which version failed, which check should have caught it, and how many requests it scored.
```

The *how* is one command. It moves the registry's `champion` alias, a name that can move, to the version before the current one, and writes that version's file. Try it on a bad release made on purpose: train with `C` of 0.00001, promote that run, and ask for the person of `scripts/person.json`; then roll back and ask again. Restart the service after each change (it loads its file at startup) before the `curl` lines.

<Aside type="tip" title="Remember">

- A request log holds the request's id, its inputs, the chance, the model version, the milliseconds and the dollars.
- PSI compares the shares of the people in each bin now and in the reference. The reference is what the model learned from.
- Drift in the inputs needs no outcomes and says look; quality needs outcomes, which arrive late.
- A retraining trigger is a rule with a number and a count of weeks, written before it is needed.
- A gate is a test that fails the build when the held-out score drops, and a rollback plan is written and tried before the incident.

**Common trap:** retraining whenever an input drifts. In the replay the alert fired for seven weeks while the quality rose.

</Aside>
